Inspect a market after an escalation fork

Use the deterministic fork-auction fixture to reconstruct a completed local disagreement from its observable parent, child, and auction state. The fixture starts after the escalation fork; this tutorial does not ask you to manufacture an unreachable pending round.

Start the scenario

Serve the UI and open http://localhost:12345/?simulate=1&simScenario=securitypoolx2-auction#/security-pools?securityPoolsView=browse. Wait for the Security pool x2 auction scenario, then open Will this resolve? (securitypoolx2 #1).

Checkpoint: the fixture contains two original pools, and the first pool has entered fork handling.

Inspect the parent evidence

Open the first parent's reporting or fork view. Confirm that both Yes and No received the own-fork threshold deposit and that the parent no longer presents an ordinary unresolved reporting round. Those REP deposits are economic claims, not cost-free votes.

Checkpoint: the parent records an own-escalation fork instead of a locally selected winner.

Find the selected child

Open the fork workflow and follow the Yes branch into its child pool. The child identity is deterministic and records its parent and outcome. A No child need not be materialized merely because No participated in the parent dispute.

Checkpoint: a Yes child exists and links back to the first parent pool.

Inspect recovery state

Open the Yes child's truth-auction view. The fixture has advanced beyond the migration window, started the auction, and seeded ten bids at three price levels. This auction repairs child backing; it does not decide whether Yes is true.

Checkpoint: the auction bidbook contains ten bids and remains distinct from the parent escalation deposits.

What you learned

You reconstructed the boundary between a parent escalation fork, a materialized child branch, and child-pool repair from reachable fixture state. Continue with the child recovery tutorial, then consult the architecture explanation.
