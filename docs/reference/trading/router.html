Trading router

Trading deploys one canonical factory and one canonical router. The router handles entry and liquidity operations, share-transfer exits and complete-set redemption, and permit-assisted LP removal.

Entrypoints

TwoWayConstantProductRouter(factory) fixes the only eligible factory. All user mutations enforce a deadline and a reentrancy guard. The router's receiver callbacks accept only the active question ledger's canonical share token and universe IDs. receive() accepts ETH only while an expected callback is active: from the selected question ledger, its canonical security pool settlement engine, or the WETH wrapper during the router’s withdrawal of redeemed WETH.

  • enterPosition(pair, mintAttoEth, longOutcome, minLongSharesOut, recipient, deadline) is payable. It mints mintAttoEth of complete sets from the fee-inclusive msg.value budget, refunds excess ETH, verifies equal Invalid/Yes/No deltas, swaps all opposite shares, and delivers long shares plus Invalid. It reverts with Minimum long shares below minLongSharesOut. It returns EnterResult: ethSpent, completeSetShares, oppositeSharesSwapped, additionalLongShares, totalLongShares, invalidInsurance, feeAmount, conditionalYesBpsBefore, and conditionalYesBpsAfter.
  • createPairAndInitializeWithEth(pool, mintAttoEth, conditionalYesBpsValue, maxYesUsed, maxNoUsed, minYesUsed, minNoUsed, minLiquidity, recipient, deadline) creates or reuses the deterministic pair. An uninitialized pair is seeded atomically at a Conditional Yes price strictly between 0 and 10,000 basis points. If anyone has already initialized the pair, the call instead adds liquidity at the pair's price exactly as addLiquidityWithEth does, so it reverts only on the caller's own bounds. Both initialization paths enforce minYesUsed ≤ yesUsed ≤ maxYesUsed and minNoUsed ≤ noUsed ≤ maxNoUsed, reverting with Liquidity price slippage outside these bounds. Set both lower and upper bounds from the deposits previewed at your price and your slippage tolerance; minimum LP output alone does not protect the initial price.
  • initializeWithEth(pair, mintAttoEth, conditionalYesBpsValue, maxYesUsed, maxNoUsed, minYesUsed, minNoUsed, minLiquidity, recipient, deadline) does the same for an already created canonical pair.
  • addLiquidityWithEth(pair, mintAttoEth, maxYesUsed, maxNoUsed, minLiquidity, recipient, deadline) creates complete sets, deposits the authoritative proportional maximum, and returns Invalid and unused shares. It reverts with Liquidity price slippage when yesUsed > maxYesUsed or noUsed > maxNoUsed. minLiquidity alone does not bound the price: a swap toward even odds before the call raises both the deposit of the side with the smaller reserve and the minted LP.
  • removeLiquidityWithPermit(pair, liquidity, minYesOut, minNoOut, recipient, deadline, v, r, s) calls the pair's permit for the caller as owner, the router as spender, exactly liquidity as value, and the same deadline; the signature must cover those values. A failing permit is ignored when the router's allowance already covers liquidity; otherwise the call reverts with LP permit or allowance. The router then pulls the LP and returns raw Yes/No directly to the recipient.
  • enterPositionWithCollateral(pair, cash, outcome, minimum, recipient, deadline, budget), createPairAndInitializeWithCollateral(pool, cash, probability, maxYes, maxNo, minYes, minNo, minimum, recipient, deadline, budget), initializeWithCollateral(pair, cash, probability, maxYes, maxNo, minYes, minNo, minimum, recipient, deadline, budget) and addLiquidityWithCollateral(pair, cash, maxYes, maxNo, minimum, recipient, deadline, budget) provide the same operations for ERC-20 collateral. cash is the mint amount in raw collateral units. Approve the router for the fee-inclusive budget; the router pulls only the mint amount plus its purchase fee; unused allowance stays with the caller. These nonpayable entrypoints require a positive budget and cannot be nested (Invalid token budget).
  • factory() exposes the immutable factory.
  • onERC1155Received, onERC1155BatchReceived, and supportsInterface exist only for the receive-based operations described below and for the pair callbacks inside an operation.

The payable entrypoints require a positive mint amount (Mint amount is zero) and a fee-inclusive budget sufficient for that mint (Purchase fee exceeds budget). They require WETH collateral (Use collateral token entry). Every entrypoint requires a recipient that is neither zero nor the router (Invalid recipient) and block.timestamp ≤ deadline (Deadline expired); those that take a pair require it to be the factory's canonical pair for its question ledger (Unrecognized pair, Noncanonical pair). The three liquidity entrypoints that take ETH return LiquidityResult: pair, completeSetShares, yesUsed, noUsed, yesReturned, noReturned, invalidInsurance, and liquidity.

Every successful share-custody operation, including the receive-based operations below, asserts that the router's Invalid, Yes, and No balances equal their starting values, preserving pre-existing forced shares.

Exits and complete-set redemption by share transfer

The router has no exitPosition or redeemCompleteSet entrypoint. Users exit and redeem by calling the ShareToken's safeBatchTransferFrom with themselves as both caller and owner, the router as recipient, and an encoded request in the data field. No setApprovalForAll is required. The request is the ABI-encoded ReceiveRequest struct with fields in this order: version (must equal CALLBACK_VERSION, currently 3), operation (0 ExitPosition, 1 RedeemCompleteSet), questionLedger, pair, longOutcome, completeSetShares, maxLongSharesIn, minEthOut, payoutRecipient, refundRecipient, deadline, and minRepOut. encodeReceiveRequest produces the 12-word payload. After authenticating the question ledger through the configured factory, the router derives its share token and three outcome token IDs and verifies the actual transfer against them. The router accepts only owner-initiated top-level transfers (operator == from); internal pair callbacks are distinguished from top-level user callbacks.

  • Insured exit. The transfer carries exactly two IDs in the order [invalidTokenId, long token ID] with the values [q, maxLongSharesIn], where q is completeSetShares, and longOutcome is Yes (1) or No (2). The router buys exactly q opposite shares with an exact-output swap, redeems q complete sets at the question ledger, and returns any unused long shares to the bound refund recipient. It emits PositionExitedByTransfer.
  • Complete-set redemption. The transfer carries exactly three IDs in the order [invalidTokenId, yesTokenId, noTokenId], each with the value completeSetShares; longOutcome must be None (3) and maxLongSharesIn zero. The router redeems them at the exact canonical question ledger and emits CompleteSetRedeemedByTransfer.

Both operations require completeSetShares > 0, a payoutRecipient and a refundRecipient that are neither zero nor the router, and canonical question ledger, share-token, universe, question and outcome IDs. An insured exit requires the factory's canonical pair. Complete-set redemption permits a zero pair; a supplied nonzero pair must still be canonical. A child with no trading pair can therefore redeem directly through this transfer route.

Both operations measure the collateral and native REP produced by redemption and forward those deltas to the payout recipient. The collateral output must meet minEthOut in raw collateral units, and the REP output must meet minRepOut in attoREP. At least one asset must be positive. For a REP-only basket, set the collateral minimum to zero and set the REP minimum to the smallest payout you accept. Wrapped ETH collateral is unwrapped before payout.

For LP removal through the router, Safes and integrations without signature support may use ordinary exact allowances instead of a permit. The first-party UI calls the pair directly and needs no router allowance.
