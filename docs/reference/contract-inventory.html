Contract inventory

This page lists every production Solidity source by area. The contract interaction reference documents the entrypoints of the contracts users and keepers call; this page adds the caller and trust boundaries of the deployment helpers, delegate modules, and libraries that reference does not cover, the SecurityPoolUtils previews, and the Uniswap contracts a testnet deployment uses.

Caller and trust boundaries

Deployment helpers are not interchangeable with canonical protocol discovery. Several are intentionally permissionless and bind the caller into each deployed address, through a caller-namespaced salt or the caller in the init code; an application must discover canonical instances through SecurityPoolFactory, recognized security pools, and recognized forker events rather than treating every factory-created address as canonical.

Module Direct caller boundary Canonical-use boundary
SecurityPoolPortfolio An immutable admission policy, question horizon, economic configuration and canonical configuration seed define the portfolio. After construction, three permissionless prepareNext() transactions initialize settlement, reporter/fork modules, then capital and the shared feed. setupStage == 3 is required before accepting user funds or risk. Anyone may register an allowed, open question in the unforked origin universe. getPool(questionId, universeId) resolves the registered lineage through the configured factory. Each universe has one capital ledger and one shared REP/ETH feed. The central settlement book owns question cash and supply accounting; the capital ledger owns each provider’s REP and commitments; the reporter ledger separately owns report escrow. Native question contracts expose shares and reporting. Verify the factory and module bindings independently. See the portfolio model.
Question admission policies ISecurityPoolQuestionAdmissionPolicy.isQuestionAllowed(portfolio, universeId, questionId) is a read-only approval check. SecurityPoolOpenAdmissionPolicy always approves. SecurityPoolFixedAdmissionPolicy(universeId, questionIds) fixes a nonempty, duplicate-free list at construction with no mutation authority. Its paginated getter returns at most 64 entries. Policy approval is required for registration and purchase, including reuse of a registered question. Denial or a failed policy call does not block exits, claims or fork recovery. The UI offers the open and fixed policies; the contract API permits other policies.
Portfolio economic configuration EconomicsConfig fixes purchaseFeeBps (at most 10,000), annualFeeBps, minimumPurchaseFeeAttoEth, positive minimumCommitmentAttoEth and minimumExecutionReserveAttoEth, minimumOutcomeRate, and positive rewardVestingSeconds. Read these immutable settings from the portfolio’s corresponding getters. The security multiplier comes from the canonical seed. Each provider selects its own health target through setHealthTarget. Changing that target does not change the protocol’s backing requirement or transfer losses to other providers. Execution reserve scales with authorized commitment; it funds keeper progress rewards rather than question creation.
SecurityPoolCapitalLedger: provider accounts depositFor(owner, amount) is callable only by that owner and adds REP to their entire live position, or to idle principal when they have no commitment. authorize(commitment) backs exact ETH commitment K with all deposited REP C, subject to a fresh price, the owner’s health target and execution reserve. The reserve requirement is ⌈K / minimumCommitmentAttoEth⌉ × minimumExecutionReserveAttoEth. Each owner has at most one live position in a portfolio universe, active or retiring, with exact C/K amounts. ownerCollateral(owner) returns deposited principal, commitment and currently withdrawable principal, excluding ETH and REP earnings. The chosen health target controls authorization; liquidation and surplus withdrawal use the protocol floor, whose security multiplier is at least 10,500 basis points. withdrawExecutionReserve(amount) returns unassigned reserve; after the fork choice cutoff it also releases the unused assigned reserve of a position whose owner did not export a choice. Exported reserves follow the selected child.
Principal withdrawal and retirement withdrawPrincipal(amount, recipient) withdraws idle principal without a price requirement. A live position must be unforked and have a fresh price; its remaining REP must cover requiredRep(K, 10_000). requestRetirement(positionId) queues the owner’s commitment. cancelRetirement(positionId) returns it to active status and is required before further authorization. serveRetirement removes an aggregate eligible prefix before new purchases. Partial private retirement reduces K while retaining all C; the controller updates that boundary position’s health key once. Full retirement releases C, settled lazily by its owner. A new request after cancellation joins the next cohort. Surplus withdrawal does not depend on another owner’s health.
Purchases, redemptions and earnings createCompleteSet(universeId, questionId, cashAttoEth, recipient, minimumShares) requires admission and fresh healthy capacity. Every purchase pays the configured backing-based fee, including capacity reuse. Ongoing collateral fees use the creator’s immutable annual rate and stop at the question’s absolute end date; a fork pause does not extend that date. purchaseFee quotes that charge. Outcome shares receive only the purchase cash; excess ETH is refunded. Redeeming complete sets reduces that question’s cash and aggregate backing demand; the purchase fee is not refunded. Released capacity can serve retiring providers without a claim for every question. Earned fees accrue through aggregate indices; purchase and reporter-surplus rewards vest over the configured duration. claimEarnings collects the owner’s ETH and REP earnings.
SecurityPoolCapitalFeeStreams Only the bound capital ledger may mutate this accounting module; asset custody remains in that ledger. addStream(ethAmount, repAmount, duration) funds a schedule after checkpointing. checkpoint(eligibleThrough) returns released income through the eligible cutoff and defers later releases. defer(ethAmount, repAmount) records income with no recipient. resume(duration, index, price) starts a new full-duration stream when that index has eligible commitment. The ledger’s acceptPrice checkpoints income without resuming deferred fees, even when replacing a still-fresh price. The next capital operation that checkpoints fees, or a permissionless ledger checkpoint(), resumes deferred income once fresh eligible commitment exists. Deferred income stays funded; its new vesting period starts at that later call, with no accrual for the waiting interval. deferredEth and deferredRep expose waiting income; fundedOutstanding() returns only scheduled, unreleased income. preview(until) quotes a release and release(until) advances it. After a fork, the ledger calls takeDeferred() to clear and burn exactly the deferred amounts, without a cleanup reward. Existing streams retain their dates and credit eligible frozen parent commitments when the price was fresh at the fork. Principal, earned claims and execution reserves are excluded from that burn.
Personal liquidation and bounded accounting cure(positionId) removes unused unhealthy commitment; takeOver(positionId, commitment, minimumAward, maximumOwnRep) transfers a selected liability to a healthy, consenting replacement. A receiver’s existing live position must belong to the source cohort; a receiver with no commitment inherits that cohort. Execution reserves pay progress rewards, and the target’s REP funds the liquidation award. A valid distress timer’s initiator receives one quarter of each later progress reward, rounded down; the executor receives the rest. Starting the timer alone pays nothing, and recovery invalidates the timer. A partially taken-over private position retains every unawarded REP behind its remaining K. Other providers’ C/K balances are unchanged by a target liquidation. Balanced trees index live commitments and health; fixed-width persistent maps track fees and finality. Work follows tree paths and the owner’s single position, without scanning questions, providers or expired streams. Native share migration still requires one action per intervening universe.
SecurityPoolCommitmentIndex: liquidation accounting Only the bound capital ledger calls cure(id, price, multiplier, minimumCommitment, demand, keeper) or clearLiquidation(id). The index updates commitment and distress timers; the ledger retains custody and pays rewards. LiquidationStarted is emitted by the index when a new interval begins. liquidationState(id) returns the valid interval’s start and initiator, or zeros after recovery invalidates its health epoch. The capital ledger’s liquidationStarted(id) returns the same start timestamp. Repeated calls during uninterrupted distress preserve its original initiator and clock.
SecurityPoolPortfolioForkCoordinator: choices choose(sourceUniverse, outcome) freezes and exports the caller’s single position and its entire deposit, including surplus collateral, into one selected branch. Idle principal without a remaining commitment and old earnings remain in the parent. Choices close eight weeks after the native fork. startBranch(sourceUniverse, outcome) then copies aggregate settlement and reporter roots and migrates REP once for that branch. Choices are immutable once exported and preserve the position’s retirement cohort. The native clock fixes recapitalization through week nine and prevents a child fork before week ten. Late branch preparation does not extend these phases. Unselected backing becomes retained runoff and protected recap inventory; it never receives an automatic branch choice on the owner’s behalf.
Shared branch recapitalization buyRecapitalization atomically purchases a bounded amount of unchosen REP at the descending auction price, subject to the immutable pre-fork reserve price, cash need and cumulative protected inventory cap. The buyer consents to its assigned commitment, health target, own-REP top-up and execution reserve. finalizeBranch closes the result at the fixed deadline, including zero-sale branches. Each fill installs the buyer’s personal position immediately; no winner list or deferred winner claim is needed. The unsold inventory retains its proportional commitment.
Source recovery eligibility and rights ShareToken.isPortfolioRecoveryEligible(sourceUniverse) returns true only for a forked canonical portfolio question with live source liabilities. The settlement book’s hasLiveForkRecoveryRight(bookId, questionId, forkTimestamp) requires outstanding outcome supply outside frozen claims. Before sealing, a nonzero finalAfter < forkTimestamp excludes the question once forkTimestamp > minimumFinalAfter, even if finality has not yet been checkpointed. After sealing, the frozen-claim assignment is authoritative. Protected finalized shares retain ordinary source-pool redemption; transferring or consuming their recovery rights reverts with No live source recovery right. balanceOfPortfolioRecoveryRights(sourceUniverse, holder) returns unspent Invalid, Yes and No rights, or all zeros when ineligible; their minimum bounds complete-set cash recovery. Holders combine rights using transferPortfolioRecoveryRights(sourceUniverse, recipient, amountsAttoShares) without transferring source shares or child migration rights. recoverUnchosenCash(source, questionId, shares) consumes one right per outcome for each requested share. Protected finalized claims remain in the universe where they finalized.
Unchosen protocol reserve Anyone may call the fork coordinator’s collectProtectedPrincipal(child) for an active branch. It serves reserve retirement while that universe is unforked, collects the protocol-owned position’s earnings and released principal, and burns the collected REP and ETH. A later fork does not prevent collecting principal and income released earlier. The reserve retires after both private owner cohorts and supplies no capacity for new purchases. ETH disposal uses only the amount received from that collection; existing source recovery cash is preserved. Private principal and earned fee claims remain with their owners.
Reporter custody and native question materialization SecurityPoolReporterLedger holds funded per-question report balances in persistent books. Only canonical games can debit their own balances. A fork charges admission to the triggering question only; unrelated report balances cannot fund it. Strictly finalized escrow remains in its source universe; live books migrate once per branch. SecurityPoolForker.preparePortfolioQuestion(source, targetUniverse) creates the canonical question namespace separately from materializePortfolioQuestion, which installs at most one of three fixed outcome snapshots per call when needed; the question activates after the third. Bound questions do not deploy individual Truth Auctions or migrate provider vaults. Trader tokens retain their immediate-parent migration checks, so skipped native universes still need explicit token migration.
Portfolio module deployers SecurityPoolPortfolioModuleDeployers supplies separately deployed capital, commitment-index, fee-stream, settlement-book, settlement-engine and reporter-ledger constructors. The fork coordinator has its own deployer and recap-auction deployer. The capital and fork coordinator deployers share SecurityPoolCreationCodeDeployer; each stores its creation bytecode in two immutable, STOP-prefixed SecurityPoolCreationCode contracts. Each created module binds its authority to the requesting portfolio or configured controller. Deployment permission does not grant authority over another portfolio’s modules. Canonical bindings and initialization must be verified before use. These deployment boundaries keep creation code and individual initialization transactions within EVM limits.
EscalationGameFactory deployEscalationGame and deployEscalationGameFromFork have no permission check, but a successful caller must implement the ISecurityPool reads used during construction; an EOA or incompatible contract reverts. The CREATE2 salt is zero; the caller is bound into the init code as the new game's securityPool, so each caller has one game address; the factory itself becomes the game's immutable owner and immediately calls start or startFromFork. Ordinary deployment requires a threshold above one attoREP and lowers a start bond at or above that threshold to threshold - 1. Its constructor receives separately deployed claim and deposit delegates, in that order, and requires code at both addresses. Games share these immutable helpers while keeping their own accounting through delegatecall. Deploying the deposit delegate separately keeps factory init code within the EVM limit. The factory has no owner role and no later resumeFromFork relay. Accept a game only after EscalationGameSet from a recognized pool. In the supported continuation path, the owning pool calls resumeFromFork, which also verifies aggregate funding.
ShareTokenFactory Anyone can call deployShareToken to deploy or retrieve a token keyed by keccak256(abi.encode(msg.sender, salt, questionId)). The CREATE2 salt is the raw salt; the caller and question id enter through constructor arguments, and that caller is the token's initial authorized address. Origin lineage tokens are the instances created by SecurityPoolFactory and named in DeploySecurityPool; children reuse the parent's token.
UniformPriceDualCapBatchAuctionFactory Anyone can call deployUniformPriceDualCapBatchAuction. Its salt is namespaced by caller and supplied salt, and its operational owner is the caller-supplied owner address. During external-universe initiation, the source must be authorized by its declared share token; own-game initiation does not perform that check, and neither relationship proves configured-factory registration. Child linking requires a deployed auction that this forker has never trusted before and preserves the source's factory and forker. Canonical pools remain the instances registered by the configured SecurityPoolFactory.
OpenOraclePriceCoordinatorFactory Anyone can call deployOpenOraclePriceCoordinator under a caller-namespaced salt with caller-supplied OpenOracle, REP token, and positive initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas; coordinator construction rejects zero and values that would consume the reserved OpenOracle uint128 report and escalation-halt capacity. The caller therefore chooses this immutable gas-price assumption within that bound. The same call deploys and initializes the coordinator's LiquidationApprovalRegistry clone and binds it to the coordinator. A pool coordinator is the instance named by canonical DeploySecurityPool; SecurityPoolFactory atomically binds it to that pool before returning, and canonical child deployment inherits the value from the parent coordinator.
PriceCoordinatorDeploymentWorker, LiquidationApprovalRegistryDeployer, and CreationCodeChunk The coordinator factory accepts separately deployed coordinator and shared-feed workers and creates its registry deployer. Worker deployments use caller-scoped salts; only the coordinator's recorded deploying caller may request registry, feed, or pool wiring. The coordinator worker stores creation code in two CreationCodeChunk contracts and supplies its immutable, stateless OpenOracleReportDelegate. The registry deployer accepts only its factory and creates initialized minimal-proxy registry clones. Code-size and deployment plumbing with no protocol authority; users do not call them.
LiquidationApprovalRegistry implementation The implementation the registry deployer creates is never initialized, so anyone can call its initialize and bind it to an arbitrary coordinator. Clones are initialized in their deployment transaction. Trust registry state only at the address returned by a recognized coordinator's liquidationApprovalRegistry().
SecurityPoolDeployer and SecurityPoolDeploymentWorker Only the configured SecurityPoolFactory may call the deployer; only that deployer may call its worker. They are code-size deployment plumbing. The proxy deployer deploys one deterministic SecurityPoolOperationsDelegate, and its address is passed into the SecurityPoolFactory constructor. The proxy deployer is the deterministic CREATE2 deployment proxy that every canonical deployment goes through; it is not a Solidity source in this repository and appears in the deployment manifests as step proxyDeployer. The factory passes that address through its deployer and worker to every pool. Its deterministic address is published as securityPoolOperationsDelegate. Pools become canonical only when the factory emits SecurityPoolRegistered and DeploySecurityPool.
SecurityPoolMigrationProxy Only its immutable owner, the SecurityPoolForker that created it, may call lockRep, forkUniverse, splitToChild, or sweepChildRep. Its deterministic per-parent address is exposed by getMigrationProxyAddress and recorded in fork snapshot events; users do not call it.
EscalationGameDepositDelegate, EscalationGameClaimDelegate, EscalationGameForker, SecurityPoolForkerVaultMigrationDelegate, SecurityPoolSettlementDelegate, and SecurityPoolOperationsDelegate Their public methods are delegatecall implementations. The deposit delegate implements initializeForkCarryOutcome, depositRepOnOutcome, depositRepOnOutcomeWithPermit, depositRepOnOutcomeWithAuthorization, recordDeposit, recordForkedEscrowForOutcome, applyTruthAuctionHaircut, ordinary winner-reserve settlement, funding-gated resumeFromFork, consumeEscrowedRepForOwner, consumeCarriedDeposit, consumeUnresolvedRepForClaimOwners, creditClaimOwners, and creditExternalClaimOwners. The shared claim delegate handles bounded claim interval reads and permissionless checkpoint preparation; it has no ownership or import surface. The forker delegates implement escalation and vault migration. SecurityPoolSettlementDelegate is abstract and contributes complete-set minting validation and funded inherited-collateral installation to the single deployed SecurityPoolOperationsDelegate. That operations delegate implements underwriting commitment changes, aggregate mint backing checks, and authorized liquidation transfers that conserve total underwriting commitments without writing off residual exposure. REP backing and fees are accounted separately. See the SecurityPool and EscalationGame interaction references for the public entrypoints, including the selectors each contract's fallback() routes to its delegate. A direct call may revert against uninitialized state or may mutate only the helper's isolated storage; it is not a protocol action. Accept resulting state and logs only when the recognized game, pool, or forker executes the matching wrapper through delegatecall. State changes and events then occur in that recognized contract's context. Canonical liquidation enters through SecurityPool.performLiquidation, which only the pool's VaultOperations executor may call; continuation activation enters through SecurityPool.resumeForkedEscalationGame.
SecurityPoolEventEmitter and SecurityPoolForkEventEmitter SecurityPoolEventEmitter is a separately deployed fixed delegate supplied to the factory after the operations delegate. It inherits the pool’s complete storage layout through SecurityPoolPortfolioAccountingDelegate and SecurityPoolLinearFees, implementing fee accrual, fee redemption, authenticated portfolio backing credits and checkpoint emission. SecurityPoolForkEventEmitter inherits the forker's typed SecurityPoolForkerStorage base and exposes emitForkSnapshotEvents. The event entrypoints are externally callable and payable, but direct calls execute against the helper's own storage and emit from the helper address. Payability permits delegatecalls from value-bearing protocol flows; callers must not send ETH directly because it is not protocol collateral and the helpers have no recovery surface. Pools delegate accounting checkpoints to their pool-layout emitter, while the forker delegates fork snapshots to its forker-layout emitter, so logs are emitted from the recognized pool or forker address. Indexers must reject matching signatures from either helper or any unrecognized emitter. Storage-layout tests protect both typed delegate layouts.
EscalationGameProofVerifier, MerkleMountainRange, and SecurityPoolUtils Math and proof routines have no lifecycle authority. Public verifier calls and SecurityPoolUtils pure previews can be used for previews, but do not mutate a game or pool. SecurityPoolUtils also has state-changing external library functions, configureForkMigratedVault, consumeUnassignedCommitment, and creditForkAuctionVault, which execute through delegatecall in the forker's context; library call protection rejects direct calls to them. A result becomes protocol state only through a recognized game, pool, or forker transaction and its events.
DeploymentStatusOracle Anyone can call getDeploymentMask; the constructor alone fixes the ordered address list and emits DeploymentAddressesSet. It reports code presence only, not canonical wiring or readiness. Decode it using its constructor event or the matching deployment manifest order.

The previews distinguish a vault's underwriting commitment from its current proportional share of open interest. The previews have no lifecycle, caller, freshness, or canonical-pool authority; only a recognized pool or coordinator transaction can apply their result.
