Statoblast and Zoltar invariants

An invariant is a statement that must remain true at every successful external-call boundary, across every legal lifecycle transition, or under the economic assumptions named beside it. Reverts may interrupt a transition, but they must roll back all of its state and asset effects.

The catalog separates local contract guards, automated release-tooling guards, cross-contract properties, and economic assumptions. Each entry names the strongest enforcement and evidence currently present; a documented implementation choice is not automatically a safe invariant.

See the Statoblast security model for assumptions A01–A28. Funded and underfunded clearing are explained in the Truth Auction clearing guide.

Classification and scope

Each entry separates the kind of property from its current enforcement status. A status describes the strongest claim supported by the local implementation and tests; it is not a formal proof.

Type Meaning
Safety A forbidden caller, state, transition, or replay cannot succeed.
Conservation Assets, liabilities, supplies, and claims reconcile without duplication or loss.
Liveness A required permissionless action remains executable under the stated conditions.
Enforcement status Meaning
Contract guard A direct authorization, bound, or state check enforces the property locally.
Reviewed preservation Several contracts cooperate to preserve the property in reviewed flows and tests.
External assumption Solidity cannot guarantee the property without honest participation, liquidity, transaction inclusion, or external token behavior.
Automated tooling guard Release, deployment, or CI tooling rejects a mismatched artifact, address, or runtime-code identity before it is accepted.

Invariant identifiers are stable review labels. The prefix names the boundary rather than a Solidity file: UNI universes, QST questions, AUTH authority, BAL pool balances, SHARE shares, VAULT vaults, FORK forks, ESC escalation, ORA the oracle, AUC auctions, LIFE lifecycle, OBS observability, and EXT external tokens, callbacks, block ordering, and lineage lookups. The index and the explorer's subsystem filter group entries by the nine page sections: BAL, SHARE, and VAULT share one section, as do LIFE, OBS, and EXT.

Terms used in the catalog

Pool question
The question whose outcome determines the SecurityPool's winning outcome shares and Escalation Game payouts.
Fork question
The ended question recorded by the universe fork. It may differ from the pool question.
Child branch
A well-formed answer encoding for the fork question, used to derive one child universe from its parent. SecurityPool's binary pool-question paths specifically use Invalid, Yes, and No.
Continuation game
A child Escalation Game initialized from an unresolved parent game's fork snapshot. Its child pool is in fork continuation until the game is fully resumed; the game itself remains the continuation game afterward.
Carry
The unresolved parent escalation deposits exported into a fork snapshot and paid out by continuation games against Merkle Mountain Range proofs.
Nullifier
A continuation game's record that one carried deposit identity has been consumed, so its proof cannot be replayed.
Fixed payout outcome
The pool-question outcome a continuation game must use after its deadline, regardless of its copied balance leader.
Direct fork
A caller invokes Zoltar's universe-fork entrypoint directly rather than through a pool's own non-decision path.
Recursive fork
A fork of a child universe created by an earlier fork.
Tracked balance
An amount recorded by protocol accounting; it excludes unsolicited assets unless a named transition explicitly credits them.
Consumed claim
A deposit, bid, or proof that has already been settled, refunded, exported, or otherwise made unusable.

Zoltar invariants

These properties govern core universe creation, REP supply, universe forks, deterministic child identities, and branch-specific REP migration.

Universes, REP supply, and migration

UNI-01One fork per universe

Required property

An initialized universe records at most one fork time and one fork question.

Example: After a universe forks on question 17, a second call attempting to fork it on question 22 reverts.

UNI-02Valid fork questions

Required property

forkUniverse accepts only a question that exists in ZoltarQuestionData and whose end time has been reached (block.timestamp >= endTime). A pool may separately require the fork question to equal its pool question. Zoltar intentionally imposes no question-creation age, universe registration, or pool-relevance condition.

Example: At exactly a known question's end time, a caller may fork a universe even when that question came from another universe or was created with an already-past end time; an unknown or still-open question is rejected. UNI-03Fork threshold burn and credit

Required property

The fork threshold is ⌈parent theoretical supply / forkThresholdDivisor⌉ for nonzero supply, and zero only when supply is zero. Starting a fork burns that amount of the initiator's parent REP, reduces the parent theoretical supply by the same amount, and credits the threshold minus ⌊threshold / forkBurnDivisor⌋ for migration. The configured forkBurnDivisor is at least 5, so the uncredited fork haircut cannot exceed 20%.

Example: With a 1,000 REP theoretical supply, threshold divisor 20, and burn divisor 5, the initiator commits 50 parent REP, pays a 10 REP fork haircut, and receives 40 REP of migration credit.

Pool assets, shares, and vaults

Per-function accept and reject conditions are in the security pool guardrails.

The raw ETH balance needs a surplus term. Without it, forced ETH is indistinguishable from collateral and can change an exchange rate or lifecycle precondition.

rawEthBalanceAttoEth = settlementCollateralAttoEth + unallocatedAccruedFeesAttoEth + totalClaimableVaultFeesAttoEth + derivedSurplusAttoEth

All terms are in attoETH. The first three are stored by the pool; derived surplus is the remainder and has no storage variable. BAL-01 states the invariant.

BAL-01ETH conservation

Required property

At every successful external-call boundary, raw ETH = tracked collateral + unallocated fee reserve + total claimable vault fees + derived surplus. Derived surplus is the remainder; it has no storage variable and is never a protocol liability.

Example: If the pool holds 12 ETH, owes 2 ETH in fees, and tracks 9 ETH of collateral, the remaining 1 ETH is surplus rather than additional collateral.

BAL-03Collateral capacity and backing

Required property

Each vault's underwriting commitment X (underwritingLimitAttoEth) is separate from its REP backing. The pool maintains the total commitment T incrementally; L is tracked settlement collateral.

  • Obligation. With positive T, a vault's obligation is L × X / T, rounded upward to the next atomic unit with full-precision arithmetic. For L ≤ T, each rounded obligation is at most X; independently rounded obligations need not sum exactly to L.
  • Authorization. REP deposits do not increase X. Redemptions and fees reduce L without reducing X, empty settlement epochs do not revoke it, and price or backing changes leave it unchanged.
  • Minting. Minting requires L + deposit ≤ T and no escalation or fork-continuation game. It reads no oracle price and checks no REP backing: every vault already answers for its full commitment, so collateral within T adds no REP exposure. Minters therefore accept the current backing of the pool's commitments; minting is not blocked when that backing is insufficient. The usable collateral ceiling equals T.
  • Withdrawal. A REP withdrawal must preserve aggregate backing for all of T and the withdrawing vault's full-commitment backing.
  • Individual health. Each vault remains subject to full-commitment backing requirements and liquidation; aggregate health does not imply that every vault is healthy. Liquidation transfers commitment and backing to an authorized, sufficiently backed receiver without writing off collateral or changing T, so recovery depends on an eligible receiver.

Example: A has a 10 ETH underwriting commitment and the pool holds 10 ETH settlement collateral. B separately deposits sufficient REP and commits 5 ETH. Their obligations become 6.666666666666666667 ETH and 3.333333333333333334 ETH. At 15 ETH settlement collateral, both obligations equal their commitments. Changing REP prices leaves the commitments and the 15 ETH collateral ceiling unchanged but can make a vault liquidatable.

BAL-04REP coverage boundaries

Required property

For a commitment X at REP/ETH price p, with pool multiplier m in BPS and health factor h in BPS (10,000 unless a delegated receiver approved more), each product rounding upward:

  • Associated-REP branch. Pool-held vault REP plus the vault's locally dispute-staked REP is at least X × p × m / 10,000 × h / 10,000.
  • Pool-held-REP branch. Pool-held vault REP alone is at least X × p × max(10,500, 10,000 + (m − 10,000) / 2) / 10,000 × h / 10,000. 10,500 BPS is the liquidation-award reserve: 10,000 plus the 500-BPS liquidation bonus.
  • Where both apply. A REP withdrawal or vault-funded escalation deposit must leave the affected vault passing both branches, and both operations apply the same two branches to the pool aggregate against the total underwriting commitment. A liquidation receiver must pass both after accepting the transferred commitment; liquidation does not require the unhealthy target to become healthy. A wallet-funded escalation deposit moves no vault backing and runs neither check.
  • Repricing. A later price change can make an existing position fail a branch without reducing its commitment; that enables liquidation and reverts nothing retroactively.

Example: With m = 20,000, the pool-held-REP multiplier is 15,000 BPS; with m = 10,500 it is the 10,500-BPS floor. A receiver approved at h = 12,000 must hold 1.2× both upward-rounded requirements after accepting the transferred commitment; passing only one branch is insufficient.

BAL-08Aggregate pool-ledger coherence

Required property

  • Fee ledger. totalClaimableVaultFeesAttoEth equals summed unpaid vault fees.
  • Operational fee eligibility. In Operational, feeEligibleUnderwritingLimitAttoEth equals summed live vault underwriting commitments plus sold Truth Auction commitment awaiting claim. uncheckpointedFeeEligibleUnderwritingLimitAttoEth covers fee-eligible commitment not yet reconciled to the current global index, including an unclaimed auction allocation after the index advances.
  • Fork migration. During ForkMigration, configured migrated commitments remain pending outside those pool aggregates. A PoolForked parent retains its fork-time total and fee-eligible snapshots as individual vaults migrate.
  • Auction activation. After a positive-purchase Truth Auction activation, total commitment equals fee-eligible commitment, with the outstanding unclaimed auction allocation in both aggregates. If the auction purchases zero REP, the unmigrated remainder stays an explicit unassigned commitment outside fee eligibility, as the auction settlement specification defines.
  • Commitment ownership. At finalization and after every claim, every commitment unit the child counts belongs either to a real vault or to the nonwithdrawable unassigned position.
  • Fee remainders. Per-vault remainder carries make ordinary fee claims independent of checkpoint frequency and claim order.
  • Terminal residue. At a terminal fork, after all commitment behind the final index has been checkpointed, the only unreconciled whole-attoETH reserve is cumulative division residue, strictly less than N attoETH. N counts each vault entitlement ledger, any separately checkpointed unassigned auction entitlement, and each denominator epoch whose global remainder is cleared when fee-eligible commitment changes; repeated checkpoints within the same ledger and epoch carry their remainder and do not increase N.
  • Terminal recovery. Because the configurable vault floor and auction settlement give N no small protocol-wide bound, the retained terminal recovery path releases that reserve only after uncheckpointedFeeEligibleUnderwritingLimitAttoEth reaches zero.

Example: Migrating one of two vaults that each hold a 3 ETH underwriting commitment leaves the parent's frozen 6 ETH total unchanged and records 3 ETH of commitment as unassigned in the child. With purchased REP, finalization adds those 3 ETH of sold commitment to fee eligibility immediately, and later claims only transfer the position to bidder vaults. With zero purchased REP, the same 3 ETH remains unassigned and fee-ineligible until an authorized, fully backed takeover.

LIFE-04Deadline equality rules

Required property

Every deadline assigns equality to one side of the boundary. These entries own their boundaries: FORK-05 (pool migration stays open at equality; auction start opens afterward), AUC-01 (bidding closes at equality, when finalization opens), ORA-03 (cached prices are stale at equality), and ORA-08 (OpenOracle disputes close and settlement opens at equality). The remaining boundaries are:

  • A universe fork and the first escalation deposit are both allowed at question-end equality.
  • Staged operations remain executable through equality and expire afterward.
  • An escalation result becomes final only after its end timestamp.
  • ERC-2612 permits include equality, while ERC-3009 authorizations use a strict validity window.

Example: Exactly at a question's end time, both forkUniverse and the first depositToEscalationGame succeed; one second earlier, both revert.

LIFE-05Bounded transition work

Required property

No mandatory transition iterates over the whole deposit, bid, or vault history in one transaction; callers use bounded pages, fixed outcome sets, proofs, or balanced-tree traversal.
