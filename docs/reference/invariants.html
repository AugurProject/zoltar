Classification and scope

Each entry separates the kind of property from its current enforcement status. A status describes the strongest claim supported by the local implementation and tests; it is not a formal proof.

Type Meaning
Safety A forbidden caller, state, transition, or replay cannot succeed.
Conservation Assets, liabilities, supplies, and claims reconcile without duplication or loss.
Liveness A required permissionless action remains executable under the stated conditions.
Enforcement status Meaning
Contract guard A direct authorization, bound, or state check enforces the property locally.
Tested property The cited regression suite checks this condition on concrete inputs or state transitions.
Reviewed preservation Several contracts cooperate to preserve the property in reviewed flows and tests.
External assumption Solidity cannot guarantee the property without honest participation, liquidity, transaction inclusion, or external token behavior.
Automated tooling guard Release, deployment, or CI tooling rejects a mismatched artifact, address, or runtime-code identity before it is accepted.

The index lists the core universe properties and security pool accounting boundaries. Identifiers name the property being reviewed; they are not contract entrypoints.

Terms used in the catalog

Ledger question
The question whose outcome determines the question ledger's winning outcome shares and Escalation Game payouts.
Fork question
The ended question recorded by the universe fork. It may differ from the ledger question.
Child branch
A well-formed answer encoding for the fork question, used to derive one child universe from its parent. Question ledgers' binary question paths specifically use Invalid, Yes, and No.
Continuation game
A child Escalation Game initialized from an unresolved parent snapshot. See fork continuation.
Carry
Unresolved deposits carried across a fork. See Merkle Mountain Range carry proofs.
Fixed payout outcome
The ledger question's outcome a continuation game must use after its deadline, regardless of its copied balance leader.
Direct fork
A caller invokes Zoltar's universe-fork entrypoint directly rather than through a question ledger's own non-decision path.
Recursive fork
A fork of a child universe created by an earlier fork.
Tracked balance
An amount recorded by protocol accounting; it excludes unsolicited assets unless a named transition explicitly credits them.
Consumed claim
A deposit, bid, or proof that has already been settled, refunded, exported, or otherwise made unusable.

Zoltar invariants

These properties govern core universe creation, REP supply, universe forks, deterministic child identities, and branch-specific REP migration.

Universes, REP supply, and migration

UNI-01One fork per universe

Required property

An initialized universe records at most one fork time and one fork question.

Example: After a universe forks on question 17, a second call attempting to fork it on question 22 reverts.

Type
Safety
Enforcement status
Contract guard
Primary evidence
Zoltar.forkUniverse
UNI-02Valid fork questions

Required property

forkUniverse accepts only a question that exists in ZoltarQuestionData and whose end time has been reached (block.timestamp >= endTime). A question ledger may separately require the fork question to equal its ledger question. Zoltar intentionally imposes no question-creation age, universe registration, or security-pool-relevance condition.

Example: At exactly a known question's end time, a caller may fork a universe even when that question came from another universe or was created with an already-past end time; an unknown or still-open question is rejected. Requiring a universe-relevant or sufficiently aged question is not a Zoltar invariant.

Type
Safety
Enforcement status
Contract guard
Primary evidence
forkUniverse, question model, question-end fork boundary test
Related assumption
A15 intended question selection
UNI-03Fork threshold burn and credit

Required property

The fork threshold is ⌈parent theoretical supply / forkThresholdDivisor⌉ for nonzero supply, and zero only when supply is zero. Starting a fork burns that amount of the initiator's parent REP, reduces the parent theoretical supply by the same amount, and credits the threshold minus ⌊threshold / forkBurnDivisor⌋ for migration. The configured forkBurnDivisor is at least 5, so the uncredited fork haircut cannot exceed 20%.

Example: With a 1,000 REP theoretical supply, threshold divisor 20, and burn divisor 5, the initiator commits 50 parent REP, pays a 10 REP fork haircut, and receives 40 REP of migration credit.

UNI-04Deterministic child universe IDs

Required property

A child universe identity is exactly the truncated hash of its parent universe and fork outcome; one parent-outcome pair maps to one child.

Example: Repeatedly deriving the Yes child of the same parent returns the same universe ID, while deriving its No child returns a different ID.

Capital health and retirement

INV-01Healthy price boundary

Required property

Backing R is sufficient exactly when price p is at or below the healthy-price boundary:

p ≤ maximumHealthyPrice(⌊R × 10,000 / H⌋, K, M)

Here K is commitment, M is the security multiplier, and H ≥ 10,000 is target health. Commitment, multiplier and price must be positive; all helper results, including intermediate values, must fit in uint256.

The next price above the boundary requires more backing or overflows the forward quote. A boundary of 2²⁵⁶ − 1 has no next price. If the inverse boundary exceeds that limit, the helper reverts rather than saturating.

INV-04Minimum required cash

Required property

For a positive target denominator d and nonnegative inputs with ⌈u × n / d⌉ + r ≤ 2²⁵⁶ − 1, required cash is the least raw amount c such that (c × S − r) × d ≥ u × n, where S = 10⁹, r is the carried fee remainder, u the units, and n/d the target ratio. Computing this without the carried remainder can change admission by one raw unit. A representable raw answer alone does not ensure that the scaled intermediate fits.

Production books keep u × n / d ≤ (2¹²⁸ − 1) × S and 0 ≤ r < S. Targets start at 1:1. Forks copy the parent’s units and either snapshot a nonempty funded parent’s cash times S over units or inherit its target. Minting requires funded backing and rounds added units down, keeping the target allocation no greater than the new cash times S, with cash below 2¹²⁸. Fee burns, redemptions, and finalization only decrease units; empty books reset the target. These transitions bound the rounded allocation plus carry below 2¹⁵⁹ for both ordinary and fork cash queries; fork queries use zero carry.

The generated-model proof and compiled-contract witness establish that unrestricted uint256 inputs can overflow before conversion to raw cash. That witness is outside this domain and remains an expected revert.

INV-27Outward price bounds

Required property

With raw uint256 price p, percentage precision D = 10,000,000 and uncertainty 0 ≤ e < D, the liquidation bound is ⌊pD/(D+e)⌋. The conservative bound is ⌈pD/(D−e)⌉ when p ≤ ⌊M(D−e)/D⌋, where M = 2²⁵⁶−1; larger prices revert with Conservative oracle price exceeds uint256. These domain expressions use mathematical integers. Questions and universes

Registry
Creating a question appends its deterministic ID once and preserves earlier records and labels. Adjacent pages reconstruct the same registry slice. questionData.test.ts.
Answers
The malformed predicate agrees with the public answer name for categorical, scalar and reserved-bit cases. Invalid is a supported answer. questionData.test.ts.
Fork finality and eligibility
An initialized universe forks once on a registered, ended question. The end timestamp itself is eligible. An unrelated ledger question can continue in a child. zoltar.test.ts, auditE2ELazyAndMultiQuestion.test.ts.
Fork accounting
The threshold burn, initiator credit and common child supply snapshot reconcile using the rounding rules in UNI-03 and UNI-08. A positive theoretical supply produces a positive threshold. zoltar.test.ts.

Deployment, enumeration and migration agree on the child derived from the parent and outcome. Malformed answers cannot acquire children. zoltar.test.ts.
Migration and supply
Cumulative minting is capped independently for each migrator and child. Summed holder balances equal actual child supply, bounded by the child snapshot. zoltar.test.ts, auditForkMigrationTwoGenerations.test.ts.
