Reference

Protocol glossary

Canonical, plain-language definitions for terms used throughout the documentation.

Universes and REP

Universe
A Zoltar ledger and identity for universe-specific REP and application state. Universe 0 is the initialized root; later universes are descendants created through forks.
Child universe
A deterministic descendant that represents one valid answer to its parent's fork question. In a binary-market example, a Statoblast fork on its own question creates Invalid, Yes, and No children. If an unrelated fork interrupts that market, its children instead follow the unrelated categorical or scalar fork question's valid answers.
Question
A global Zoltar object that identifies a proposition, its end time, and its answer options.
Outcome
One possible answer to a question. Statoblast binary markets use Invalid, Yes, and No.
Invalid
The legitimate third outcome for ambiguous, malformed, or otherwise unanswerable market wording.
REP
Universe-specific reputation tokens used as reporting, dispute, and security capital.
Theoretical REP supply
Zoltar's per-universe REP supply figure, from which it derives the fork and non-decision thresholds. Burns reduce it, and each child universe starts from its parent's supply after the fork threshold burn plus the fork initiator's migration balance.
Fork threshold
The amount of parent-universe REP that a fork initiator supplies and burns to begin a fork, derived by getForkThresholdAttoRep from that universe's theoretical REP supply. See migration accounting.
Fork haircut
The part of the fork threshold that Zoltar burns without crediting to the initiator's migration balance: ⌊fork threshold ÷ forkBurnDivisor⌋, at most 20%. Contract comments call it the burn haircut. REP added to a migration balance after the fork starts is credited in full.
Migration balance
A parent-universe credit (migrationRepBalanceAttoRep) created when REP is burned or committed for a fork. The same credited amount can be minted independently in each valid child, while per-child accounting prevents an account from consuming more than that amount in any one child.
Child REP
REP minted for one child universe. Its ledger and economic value are separate from parent and sibling REP.

SecurityPools, shares, and vaults

SecurityPool
The Statoblast contract for one question, universe, and pool configuration (its security multiplier, which sets the REP-to-ETH backing it requires, and the gas priority fee it budgets for oracle price reports); each lineage has at most one pool per universe. It holds settlement collateral and coordinates shares, vaults, resolution, migration, and repair.
Lineage
An origin SecurityPool and every child pool forked from it. Pools in one lineage share an origin ID and one outcome-share token contract.
Origin pool
A SecurityPool deployed for a question with no parent pool (deployOriginSecurityPool). Child pools created by forks descend from it and form its lineage.
Settlement collateral
The ETH a SecurityPool records as backing its outcome shares (settlementCollateralAttoEth). Complete sets and winning shares redeem against it pro rata, and fees decay out of it into the fee reserve.
Open interest
The ETH exposure of outstanding complete sets that vaults underwrite. The contracts measure it with settlement collateral: getVaultOpenInterestAttoEth returns a vault's share of settlement collateral in proportion to its underwriting commitment. When a forked child holds less settlement collateral than its inherited shares claim, the gap is a shortfall for a Truth Auction to repair, not open interest.
Complete set
One matching unit each of Invalid, Yes, and No shares, minted against ETH and redeemable together while the pool is operational.
Outcome share
An ERC-1155 token representing one outcome claim in one universe-specific SecurityPool.
Vault
A SecurityPool account that records a participant's REP backing, underwriting commitment, obligations, and fees.
Backing units
Accounting units that represent a proportional claim on REP held for SecurityPool vaults.
Underwriting commitment
A vault owner's standing ETH underwriting amount, separate from REP backing. The code field is underwritingLimitAttoEth; liquidation parameters and events name the same quantity debt or securityBondDebt. Its share of the pool total determines proportional settlement obligations and fee rights.
Security multiplier
A pool's required ratio, in basis points, of a vault's REP value to its underwriting commitment (statoblastSecurityMultiplierBps). It is fixed when the origin pool is deployed and must exceed 10,001 BPS.
Vault health
Whether a vault's REP backs its whole underwriting commitment at the cached REP-per-ETH price (isVaultHealthy). An unhealthy vault can be liquidated; see when a vault is liquidatable for the two constraints.
Health factor
A multiplier in basis points applied to both vault-health requirements of the receiver in a liquidation. 10,000 BPS is the protocol minimum; a liquidation approval can demand more through minPostLiquidationHealthFactorBps. The targetHealthFactorBps of a REP deposit is a separate value that must be at least the pool's security multiplier.
Bad debt
An ETH amount recorded against a vault (vaultBadDebtAttoEth) and summed for the pool (totalBadDebtAttoEth). A vault that carries any can be neither the target nor the receiver of a liquidation. Fork migration moves it to the child pool with its vault, and it is cleared when the pool's last share is redeemed. Liquidation itself records none.
Minting capacity
The ceiling on a pool's settlement collateral: its total underwriting commitments while no Escalation Game exists, and zero afterwards (getCurrentMintingCapacityAttoEth).
Utilization
Settlement collateral divided by minting capacity. It sets the retention rate.
Fee reserve
ETH that has decayed out of settlement collateral as fees and is not yet assigned to a vault (unallocatedAccruedFeesAttoEth). A vault checkpoint moves the vault's share into its claimable fees.
Retention
The per-second fraction of a SecurityPool's outstanding complete-set collateral that stays with complete-set holders; the remainder moves continuously into the pool's fee reserve. Because retention is below one, the ETH backing one complete set falls over time; see open-interest fees.

Escalation Game

Escalation Game
The local Statoblast dispute mechanism in which REP-backed balances accumulate behind Invalid, Yes, and No.
Start bond
The minimum Escalation Game deposit: the larger of 1 REP and the universe's theoretical REP supply when the pool was deployed ÷ 107. Origin-pool deployment requires it to be below the non-decision threshold; a game started with a bond at or above that threshold is clamped to one atto-REP below that threshold. A smaller deposit is accepted only when it fills an outcome exactly to that threshold. A child pool continuing a forked game keeps its parent's start bond.
Binding capital
The median of the three outcome balances that determines the Escalation Game's scheduled deadline.
Required support
The REP an outcome must hold to stay in contention, called attrition cost in the contracts (totalCostAttoRep). In an ordinary game whose universe remains unforked, it is zero for the three-day activation delay, then rises from the start bond to the non-decision threshold over 49 days. The scheduled deadline estimates when it passes binding capital.
Non-decision threshold
The balance that two outcomes must each reach for the Escalation Game to enter non-decision: half of the universe's fork threshold, rounded up, read when the game opens and fixed for that game and any game carried from it.
Non-decision
The Escalation Game state in which two outcomes have each reached the non-decision threshold (NonDecisionState.Local). The game picks no winner, and anyone can fork the universe on the pool's own question.
Reward-eligible cap
One and a half times binding capital. Only the portion of winning Escalation Game deposits up to this cap earns a bonus; deposits beyond it return principal only. See the EscalationGame reference.
Escalation haircut
Two fifths of binding capital, burned at Escalation Game settlement instead of being paid to winners as a bonus, unless the pool's own fork already paid it through the fork haircut. The contracts call it the winner haircut (burnEscalationWinnerHaircut).

Forks and repair

SecurityPoolForker
The shared Statoblast contract that runs a SecurityPool through a fork: it snapshots the parent pool, migrates its REP and vaults into child pools, and runs each child's Truth Auction.
Migration window
The eight weeks (MIGRATION_TIME) after a parent pool enters fork mode, during which its vaults and REP can move into child pools. A child's Truth Auction can start only after it closes.
Carried game
An Escalation Game that a child pool continues from its parent's unresolved game, with the parent's balances and elapsed time. The game's forkContinuation flag marks it permanently, and it never accepts deposits from a wallet. The reference pages also call it a continuation game.
Fork continuation
The temporary state of a child pool whose carried game has not yet been resumed (awaitingForkContinuation). While it is pending, the pool rejects complete-set minting and Escalation Game deposits.
Truth Auction
A Statoblast auction that sells child REP to repair a child pool's ETH collateral shortfall. It does not select truth.
Clearing tick
The price tick at which a Truth Auction clears (clearingTick). In a funded auction, bids above it win in full, bids below it lose, and bids at it fill in arrival order, all at its price.
Underfunded threshold
The ETH-per-REP price at which selling the whole REP sale cap raises exactly the ETH raise cap, rounded up (underfundedThreshold). In an underfunded auction, where bids never reach either cap at their own prices, only bids at or above it buy REP, and together they receive the whole REP sale cap.

Liquidation

Target vault
The unhealthy vault whose underwriting commitment a liquidation reduces (targetVault).
Receiver vault
The vault that accepts the transferred commitment and the REP award (receiverVault). It must stay healthy on its whole resulting commitment.
Operator
The account that stages a liquidation (operator). It is the receiver unless the receiver approved it through the LiquidationApprovalRegistry, and it receives no protocol payment.

Prices

OpenOracle
An economically contestable price-reporting contract used by Statoblast for fresh REP-per-ETH prices (written REP/ETH elsewhere in these pages), not market outcomes.
Price coordinator
The per-pool contract that requests OpenOracle prices, caches accepted reports, and queues solvency-sensitive SecurityPool operations.
Sponsor lane
The rule that, while a price report is pending, only the account that opened it (pendingReportSponsor) can add operations to that settlement. Other callers wait until the report finishes.
VaultOperations executor
The VaultOperations contract each price coordinator deploys. It stores a vault owner's bundle of up to four actions and is the only caller a SecurityPool accepts for price-checked REP withdrawals and liquidations.

Trading

Trading pool
The constant-product pair (TwoWayConstantProductPair, called the pair in the Trading reference) that trades one SecurityPool's Yes and No shares against each other. The Trading factory creates at most one per SecurityPool.
Trading router
The TwoWayConstantProductRouter contract that turns ETH into a position and a position back into ETH by combining SecurityPool complete-set calls with trading-pool swaps.
Long shares
The Yes or No shares a trader holds on the side they expect to win. Entering through the trading router also leaves the trader one Invalid share for each complete set it minted, fewer than the long shares received.
Insured exit
A trading-router exit that swaps some long shares for the opposite outcome, then redeems them with the trader's Invalid shares as complete sets for ETH.
