OpenOracle integration reference

OpenOracle supplies a fresh REP/ETH price for Statoblast's solvency-sensitive operations. It does not decide market truth; local Escalation Games and Zoltar remain responsible for truth and branching.

The OpenOracle explanation covers why Statoblast uses a contestable price and which operations depend on it. This page specifies the integration: formulas, parameters, and settlement-callback behavior. Per-function callers, preconditions, and events are in the coordinator contract reference.

Token pair and cached price

Statoblast configures WETH as token1 and REP as token2. Keeping WETH on the exact side lets the coordinator size the report directly from ETH-denominated gas costs without first assuming a REP/ETH conversion price. When a report settles, the coordinator converts the settled token amounts into REP per ETH:

This raw-amount ratio is valid because the configured WETH and REP contracts both use 18 decimals. The coordinator does not normalize token decimals in the settlement callback.

An accepted report becomes the coordinator's cached REP/ETH price. Its freshness window is one hour on Sepolia and five minutes on other chains. The window starts at settlement eligibility (the final report timestamp plus settlementTime), not when settle is called, and lastSettlementTimestamp records that start. A late settlement therefore cannot extend the life of a price that was frozen earlier. The coordinator does not meter how much operation volume uses that price during the freshness window.

Report flags

The coordinator always submits flags 7: FLAG_TIME_TYPE (1, a timestamp clock), FLAG_TRACK_DISPUTES (2, per-dispute history), and FLAG_STORE_ALL (4, the complete report state in storage). It never sets the optional flags 8, 16, 32, or 64; the OpenOracle provenance record identifies the vendored source and build profile.

Integration architecture

Every security pool has its own OpenOraclePriceCoordinator. The coordinator connects users, the pool, and the shared OpenOracle instance. It keeps the pool's cached REP/ETH price, pending report state, staged-operation queue, and settlement callback state. Keeping this state per pool prevents one pool's pending report or freshness window from becoming a protocol-wide queue.

OpenOraclePriceCoordinator is deployed with an immutable OpenOracle address. For each origin or child security pool, SecurityPoolFactory asks OpenOraclePriceCoordinatorFactory for a fresh coordinator wired to the shared OpenOracle instance, shared WETH, and that universe's REP token. SecurityPoolDeployer passes the coordinator to the pool, after which the factory calls setSecurityPool once with the nonzero pool address.

Only the configured pool may seed the coordinator's inherited lastPrice. A child pool inherits the parent's numeric price as a starting value, but its settlement timestamp remains zero, so that number is unusable until the child accepts its own report.

Price request lifecycle

Liquidations, REP withdrawals, and staged underwriting-commitment changes (OperationType.SetVaultUnderwritingLimit) begin at the pool's coordinator. The coordinator validates and stages the operation, then uses a cached price or opens an OpenOracle report. Owners may also set commitments directly at the pool. REP deposits add backing without changing commitments. Exact permissions and guards are in the SecurityPool reference.

  1. Validate and stage the operation. The coordinator checks the caller, target vault, requested amount, and validity window before any report is requested.
  2. Check the cached price. If the cached REP/ETH price is fresh, the coordinator attempts the operation immediately.
  3. Open a report when the price is stale. The refresh sponsor supplies the request bounty and initial WETH/REP position, and the coordinator creates the report as its initial reporter.
  4. Allow corrections. Other participants may replace the report by posting the position required by OpenOracle. Every valid replacement installs a new reporter and restarts the settlement window.
  5. Settle the current report. Once the settlement deadline is reached without another dispute, any account can call OpenOracle's settle, which finalizes the report and calls the coordinator.
  6. Accept or reject the price. The coordinator validates the report identity, history, economics, amounts, and settlement-time base fee before updating the cache.
  7. Execute queued operations. An accepted fresh price allows the coordinator to attempt the queued pool operations, each of which still performs its own current safety checks.

Staging preconditions

A request that violates a staging precondition reverts before any report is requested. The preconditions of each entry point, including amounts, the validity window, vault targets, liquidation approvals, and the pending-report sponsor rule, are listed in the coordinator contract reference.

Execution batches

For a single operation with a valid cached price, the coordinator records the operation and attempts it in the same transaction. A liquidation gate or another pool check can still fail; the transaction then succeeds and the operation is consumed with ExecutedStagedOperation reporting success = false.

An operation that waits for a report can also fail when it is attempted: after its expiry (Staged operation expired), when a liquidation target's backing units or underwriting commitment changed after staging (Stale liquidation), or when a withdrawal now previews to zero REP (Withdraw amount has no effect).

When a new report is needed, one settlement callback can automatically attempt up to MAX_PENDING_SETTLEMENT_OPERATIONS (four) price actions, counting each commitment change, liquidation, or withdrawal inside a vault bundle separately. Deposits use no callback slot. A bundle must fit the remaining capacity; additional single staged operations remain active outside the callback batch. If they have not expired and the accepted price remains fresh, they can be executed later with executeStagedOperation.

For a vault bundle, a fresh-price failure reverts the entire submission. With a stale price, the deposit completes immediately and the deferred commitment changes, liquidations, and withdrawal later execute or fail together. See the coordinator interaction reference.

Escalation Game deposits

Escalation Game REP deposits do not use the staged callback path. When a pool has any underwriting commitment, depositToEscalationGame requires an already-fresh coordinator price and reverts when the price is stale. After previewing the accepted REP deposit, the pool uses lastPrice for the post-transfer vault and pool coverage checks.

Funding and report ownership

Report funding is caller supplied. The coordinator's getRequestPriceCostAttoEth() getter calculates the minimum request bounty from the current base fee, callback gas limit, and the coordinator's own report processing budget:

Every entry point that can open a report takes an explicit bountyAttoEth. What happens to msg.value depends on the call:

  • The call opens a report. bountyAttoEth must be at least getRequestPriceCostAttoEth() in the inclusion block, and msg.value must be at least bountyAttoEth. The coordinator forwards exactly bountyAttoEth to OpenOracle as the report's settlerReward and refunds the rest. The same transaction transfers the sponsor's initial WETH and REP position, so the sponsor funds the bounty and the position up front.
  • A staging call finds a fresh cached price. No bounty is taken, and all of msg.value is refunded.
  • A staging call adds an operation while a report is pending. No bounty is taken, and all of msg.value is refunded.

requestPrice, requestPriceIfNeededAndStageOperation, and requestPriceIfNeededAndStageLiquidation refund msg.sender. stageVaultOperations, which only the coordinator's vaultOperations executor can call, refunds the bundle owner. Each refund is a low-level native-ETH call made only for a positive amount. If the recipient rejects it, the whole transaction reverts, including operation staging, immediate execution, and any report opened in the same transaction.

Sponsor lane

The sponsor lane is the exclusive right of pendingReportSponsor, the account that funded the pending report, to add operations to that report's settlement. Those follow-up operations pay no additional bounty; other callers must wait until the report finishes. requestPrice reverts while the cached price is fresh or a report is pending.

Report sizing and correction incentives

The coordinator sizes each report so that correcting a price error of at least targetPriceErrorForDispute stays profitable after OpenOracle's fees and the modeled gas cost of one dispute multiplied by openOracleSecurityMultiplierBps. That multiplier is separate from a SecurityPool's backing multiplier, statoblastSecurityMultiplierBps.

A report can be corrected in either direction. The formula assumes the less profitable one, in which the true REP/ETH price is (1 + targetError) times the reported price, and models the correction as releasing the fraction (targetError - fees) / (1 + targetError) of the WETH position before gas. The required position is the multiplied gas cost divided by that fraction.

The minimum initial WETH position also has a floor of 1% of the pool's settlement collateral (settlementCollateralAttoEth), and the same floor applies to the escalation halt:

reportAttoEth(gasPriceAttoEthPerGas) = ⌈ gasPriceAttoEthPerGas⋅gasUnitsForOneDispute⋅openOracleSecurityMultiplierBps ⋅(percentagePrecision+targetPriceErrorForDispute) 10000⋅(targetPriceErrorForDispute-protocolFee-feePercentage) ⌉ openInterestReportAttoEth = ⌈settlementCollateralAttoEth100⌉ minimumToken1ReportAttoEth = reportAttoEth(initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas) + max(reportAttoEth(block.basefee),openInterestReportAttoEth) escalationHaltAttoEth = max(⌊initialWethReportAttoEth⋅escalationHaltMultiplierBps10000⌋,openInterestReportAttoEth)

All amounts are in attoETH. percentagePrecision is 10,000,000, the denominator of targetPriceErrorForDispute, protocolFee, and feePercentage.

No REP price, expected REP price movement, or individual protected-operation notional enters the minimum WETH calculation.

The coordinator sets initialWethReportAttoEth = max(minimumToken1ReportAttoEth(), requestedInitialAttoWeth), uses that value as OpenOracle's currentAmount1, and derives amount2 = ⌈initialWethReportAttoEth * proposedRepPerEthPrice / 1e18⌉ in the same transaction.

Construction bounds and parameter effects

Coordinator construction requires:

  1. positive dispute gas units
  2. positive request gas: gasConsumedSettlement or gasConsumedOpenOracleReportPrice is nonzero
  3. a nonzero initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas
  4. a target error no greater than 100%
  5. an OpenOracle security multiplier of at least 1x and at most type(uint256).max / (percentagePrecision + targetPriceErrorForDispute)
  6. combined OpenOracle fees below the target error
  7. a nonzero escalationHaltMultiplierBps
  8. a timestamp clock with dispute tracking (timeType and trackDisputes both true)
  9. maxSettlementBaseFeeMultiplierBps of at least 1x
  10. minLiquidationPriceDistanceBps no greater than 100%

Construction also bounds initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas so that the priority-fee component of the minimum report, reportAttoEth(initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas), does not exceed half of min(type(uint128).max, type(uint128).max × 10000 / escalationHaltMultiplierBps); a larger fee reverts with Initial report priority fee exceeds OpenOracle limits. OpenOraclePriceCoordinatorFactory stores the shared configuration without validating it, so an invalid configuration deploys the factory successfully and makes every later coordinator deployment revert.

At a 30 nanoETH base fee, a configured 10 nanoETH priority fee, and 100 WETH of settlement collateral, the modeled gas cost of one dispute is 0.012 ETH, the minimum report is 3.230769230769230770 WETH, and the escalation halt is 32.307692307692307700 WETH.

Settlement validation, rejection, and recovery

Low-level callback failure

OpenOracle invokes the coordinator through a low-level callback whose success is intentionally neither stored nor emitted. A failed callback does not automatically undo an otherwise valid OpenOracle settlement. The coordinator accepts callbacks only from the configured OpenOracle and only for the current pending report id. If those checks revert inside the low-level call, the report can still settle while the coordinator remains pending.

If the settlement transaction cannot retain enough gas headroom after the callback attempt, OpenOracle reverts settlement with InvalidGasLimit. In that case, the report has not settled and coordinator recovery is not yet available.

Coordinator settlement validation

A callback that successfully enters the coordinator can still produce a rejected price. The coordinator clears the pending report state, emits PriceReportRejected with the quoted reason, and leaves the price cache unchanged when the first of the following conditions holds:

  • the settlement block's base fee exceeds the request-time cap, which is the base fee implied by the committed bounty scaled by maxSettlementBaseFeeMultiplierBps: Base fee too high;
  • storedGame(reportId).numReports is saturated at the uint24 maximum: Counter saturated;
  • numReports is zero, or the settled WETH amount is below reportAttoEth(initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas) + reportAttoEth(recordedBaseFee) from the sizing formula, where recordedBaseFee is the base fee OpenOracle stored for the final report in disputeHistory(reportId, numReports - 1): Report uneconomic;
  • the freshness window measured from settlement eligibility has already closed: Report stale;
  • either settled token amount is zero: Empty oracle settlement;
  • the integer REP/ETH calculation produces a zero price: Oracle price is zero.

These checks establish only that the final WETH position meets the modeled security floor and that the callback data is structurally acceptable. They do not prove that the accepted price is externally correct, that an independent corrector exists, or that a correcting transaction will obtain inclusion.

Rejected-price cleanup

A rejected report does not replay its pending settlement operations. Instead, the coordinator terminally fails every operation attached to that report, removes it from the active and pending sets, and releases any liquidation-approval reservation. A caller must stage a new operation to try again with a later price.

Recovering a settled report after callback failure

When OpenOracle has already settled but the coordinator remains pending because the low-level callback did not complete, anyone can call recoverSettledPendingReport. Recovery clears the pending report, sponsor, and base-fee cap; withdraws the coordinator's OpenOracle WETH and REP balances to the sponsor; and terminally fails every pending settlement operation attached to the report, releasing each liquidation-approval reservation. It does not accept the report as a new price.

Liveness and exposure limits

The coordinator has no notional exposure budget and no absolute settlement deadline; see excluded guarantees EG03 and EG04 and the OpenOracle explanation.

Dispute escalation amounts

In this integration, WETH is the escalation variable. Before escalationHalt, OpenOracle requires min(⌊oldAmount1 × multiplier / 100⌋, escalationHalt). With the configured multiplier of 115, integer flooring leaves token1 amounts from one through six attoETH unchanged; seven attoETH is the first amount that grows. At or above the halt, each replacement instead requires oldAmount1 + 1, adding one attoETH of WETH.

Stress model: external payoff and censorship

This section defines an analytical model, not contract behavior. It estimates the cost to an attacker who posts a manipulated REP/ETH report and censors corrections until settlement, receiving an outside payoff only if the accepted price makes a liquidation executable. It is a continuous approximation; the OpenOracle explanation states how far its results can be relied on.

honestDisputeBarrierFraction is a model input, not a coordinator field: the cost, as a fraction of the honest price, that an honest corrector must overcome. Its 1.1% default is the fixed-fee floor, protocolFee plus feePercentage.

Price errors and the dispute barrier are fractions of the honest price. externalPayoff and oracleReportLiquidity use the same ETH-denominated unit. The liquidation design owns the exact integer threshold and distance checks.

targetGriefRatio is the desired attacker loss net of externalPayoff, divided by that payoff; 1 targets censorship cost equal to twice the payoff. One model step is the interval over which censorshipRate accrues; the model does not tie it to a block, second, or dispute round.

durationToTargetCost is the number of model steps of censorship after which censorshipCost reaches (targetGriefRatio + 1) × externalPayoff. It is not a maximum protocol delay.
