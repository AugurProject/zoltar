OpenOracle integration reference

OpenOracle supplies a fresh REP/collateral price for Statoblast's solvency-sensitive operations. It does not decide market truth; local Escalation Games and Zoltar remain responsible for truth and branching.

The OpenOracle explanation covers why Statoblast uses a contestable price and which operations depend on it. This page specifies the integration: formulas, parameters, and settlement-callback behavior. Per-function callers, preconditions, and events are in the coordinator contract reference.

Token pair and cached price

Statoblast configures the portfolio’s immutable collateral token as token1 and universe REP as token2. Reports quote their direct raw-unit ratio, without a collateral/ETH feed:

The price is raw REP units per raw collateral unit, scaled by 1018. The callback does not normalize decimals. For six-decimal USDC or USDT, one REP per whole collateral token is encoded as 1030; for 18-decimal WETH it is 1018.

An accepted report becomes the coordinator's cached REP/collateral price. lastSettlementTimestamp records settlement eligibility: the final report timestamp plus settlementTime. The freshness window starts there, so settling late cannot extend it. The shared feed freshness window is settlementTime plus one hour on Sepolia or five minutes on other chains. With the mainnet eight-minute challenge period, a shared price is usable for 780 seconds after eligibility, or at most 21 minutes after the final report. priceValidUntil() returns the canonical expiry; a price is stale at that timestamp. The coordinator does not meter operation volume during the window.

Shared feeds size reports and escalation halts against the largest of current portfolio commitment, backing demand and the maximum commitment a preparing branch auction can add to chosen backing. The feed reads these aggregates without scanning questions or bidders. Unused capacity from an oversized earlier report does not increase the minimum size of the next report.

A shared feed permits concurrent candidates, including renewal before the accepted price expires. Each candidate has its own SharedPoolReportEscrow, which holds its reporter identity and funding credits. pendingReportId and the associated pending getters identify only the latest candidate; they do not enumerate or exclude other pending candidates. A pending, disputed or rejected report never extends accepted-price freshness.

Shared acceptance atomically checkpoints portfolio fees under the previous price and updates capital eligibility. A failed mandatory checkpoint rolls back acceptance. Ordinary capital operations execute separately and recheck current safety constraints against the accepted price. An optional batch of sponsor-authorized capital operations is attempted immediately after acceptance.

Portfolio branch preparation

SecurityPoolFactory.preparePortfolioPriceFeed(sourcePool, child) prepares the canonical child feed without deploying a child question. sourcePool must be a canonical question pool already admitted to this portfolio, with its portfolio binding recorded by the factory. The configuration seed is itself a canonical portfolio question. SecurityPoolPortfolio.isPriceReportingOpen(child) permits requests and acceptance after startBranch, until the fixed native recapitalization deadline at week nine. From that deadline until activation, the accepted observation is frozen. Reporting reopens on activation if the child universe has not forked; a forked universe rejects new prices. Late branch preparation does not extend the window.

An accepted preparation report caches the price and funded exposure; it does not activate trading, start holding fees or start imported positions’ liquidation history. Activation uses the cached price only while it remains fresh. A stale or absent price does not block branch activation. Callbacks during a closed window reject the candidate price while preserving report funding recovery.

Shared report coverage and capital prices

During auction preparation, minimumToken2ReportAttoRep() requires one hundredth of fresh sale inventory, rounded up. minimumToken1ReportAttoEthForPrice(price) also raises the collateral minimum enough to fund that REP amount at the proposed price. Direct sponsorship through requestPortfolioPrice rejects a signed collateral budget below the required amount. Acceptance checks both sides: acceptedRepExposureLimitAttoRep is final REP funding times 100. An arbitrarily cheap proposed REP price therefore cannot remove the REP inventory challenge requirement.

A canonical acceptance sets acceptedExposureLimitAttoEth to (final collateral − configured collateral stake floor) × 100 and increments acceptedEpoch. An ordinary setCommitment raise cannot lift aggregate exposure above that accepted limit. Pending funding grants no capacity. To raise a commitment, an owner can instead call setCommitmentWithReport(reportId, commitment) with an independently settled, fresh candidate whose final funding covers the projected total exposure. reportAuthorization(reportId, newExposure) validates that proof even if a newer, smaller canonical report superseded it.

For accepted price q, target error e, and percentage precision P = 10,000,000, the upper bound is q × P / (P − e), rounded up, and the liquidation bound is q × P / (P + e), rounded down. Capital entry, withdrawals, capacity and fee eligibility use the conservative upper bound; rewarded adverse actions use the lower bound. Proof authorization also retains the higher of the owner’s current conservative price and the proof’s upper bound for that owner’s collateral checks until a later canonical epoch covers the enlarged live exposure. Selecting a smaller report cannot remove this owner-specific floor.

Report flags

The coordinator always submits flags 7: FLAG_TIME_TYPE (1, a timestamp clock), FLAG_TRACK_DISPUTES (2, per-dispute history), and FLAG_STORE_ALL (4, the complete report state in storage). It never sets the optional flags 8, 16, 32, or 64; the OpenOracle provenance record identifies the vendored source and build profile.

Integration architecture

Each question has an OpenOraclePriceCoordinator adapter attached to one SharedPoolPriceFeed per portfolio and universe. The feed owns the accepted REP/collateral price and freshness clock. Every candidate has its own escrow. The factory authenticates coordinator creation and binds each question once.

A child universe gets a new feed for its own REP token, initially with no accepted price. Adding a question does not reset an existing feed. Capital deposits, commitments and retirement use the portfolio directly. Capital operations may also execute through an ordered sponsor-authorized batch attached to a shared report.

Price request lifecycle

  1. The sponsor approves collateral and universe REP, chooses a candidate price and report budget, and opens a report through a question coordinator or the shared feed.
  2. OpenOracle accepts valid corrections during the challenge window. A correction replaces the current reporter and restarts settlement timing.
  3. After the challenge window, anyone can settle the report. Its isolated escrow validates the result and offers it to the shared feed.
  4. The feed accepts only a qualifying, fresh observation with sufficient economic coverage. Rejected candidates cannot invalidate the previously accepted observation.
  5. Owners and keepers normally submit capital operations separately. A report may instead carry a sponsor-authorized operation batch; both paths recheck current safety constraints.

Escalation reports use wallet REP in separate reporter custody. They do not consume underwriting REP or use an oracle operation queue.

Funding and report ownership

The sponsor funds its own collateral, REP and transaction gas. The minimum public settlement bounty is 101 attoETH; it does not reimburse the sponsor’s gas. The sponsor may supply a larger bounty.

requestPortfolioPrice(proposedRepPerEthPrice, requestedInitialAttoWeth, bountyAttoEth) transfers exactly the reviewed collateral budget and its corresponding REP from the caller. It rejects a budget below the inclusion-time minimum instead of silently increasing it. Only the recorded sponsor receives that candidate’s returned funding. Portfolio settlement collateral does not fund reports.

A coordinator request forwards the specified bounty and refunds excess native ETH to the caller. Rejecting that refund reverts the whole request. A direct feed request requires exactly its specified bounty.

Sponsor-authorized operation batches

requestPortfolioPriceWithOperations(price, wethBudget, bounty, batch) funds a direct report and records ordered capital-ledger calls. The funding caller supplies the owner authority for every call; withdrawal recipients remain explicit in the signed calldata. A batch fixes accepted-price bounds, an expiry, a total gas allowance, and an array of operations containing calldata and individual gas limits. There is no fixed operation-count cap.

The ledger supports takeOver, takeOverWithBounds, takeOverAuctionBid, depositFor, depositAndSetHealthTarget, withdrawPrincipal, setHealthTarget, setCommitment, setCommitmentWithReport, claimEarnings, serveRetirement and materializeAuctionBid. Privileged accounting and fork entrypoints are excluded. Each call retains its ordinary owner, collateral, cohort, funding and reentrancy checks. A report-bound takeover may use zero for expectedAcceptedPriceEpoch to accept the settlement epoch; direct takeovers retain exact epoch checks. Funding can be deposited beforehand or by an earlier batch operation with the required REP approval.

The declared batch allowance must cover each individual limit plus 100,000 gas per operation. The OpenOracle callback budget is 4,000,000 gas plus that allowance; ordinary reports retain 4,000,000. After mandatory acceptance and portfolio checkpoints, the feed attempts every operation in order through executeReportOperation, which accepts only the canonical feed and authenticates a ledger self-call as the sponsor. Failed operations, including exhausted individual allowances and reverted auction materialization, roll back individually; later operations still run. Expired or out-of-range batches emit failed outcomes without executing their calls. ReportOperationAttempted(reportId, sponsor, operationIndex, success) records each outcome. Insufficient callback headroom reverts acceptance and earlier operations rather than publishing a price after skipping a promised attempt.

The batch runs inside its report's acceptance transaction regardless of who settles. It receives no priority over another report that settles first, no exclusive target reservation, and no reimbursement. Practical transaction and block gas limits bound execution. sponsorOperationReport(sponsor) identifies the latest batch report; reportOperationBatch(reportId) exposes its terms. Independent reports and ordinary recovery remain available.

Independent candidates

Reporting has no exclusive sponsor lane. Anyone may fund another candidate while earlier candidates are pending or the accepted price is still fresh. Disputing one candidate cannot reserve all reporting access.

Report sizing and correction incentives

The creator sets a positive minimumReportCollateral in raw collateral units at portfolio creation. It is immutable and inherited by every universe in the portfolio. The minimum report adds that floor to one hundredth of aggregate collateral exposure, rounded up. REP inventory coverage can raise it further at the proposed price.

minimumToken1ReportAttoEth=minimumReportCollateral+⌈aggregateExposureAttoEth100⌉

Amounts use raw collateral units, including accessors with legacy AttoEth names. The escalation halt remains the larger of the exposure minimum and the configured fraction of initial collateral funding.

The creator must size this floor to satisfy A18: independent, gas-economic correction throughout the portfolio’s use, including at low exposure. This is a system assumption, not an onchain gas-cost guarantee. No collateral/ETH feed is queried, and a higher floor requires a new portfolio.

The coordinator sets initialWethReportAttoEth = max(minimumToken1ReportAttoEthForPrice(proposedRepPerEthPrice), requestedInitialAttoWeth), uses that value as OpenOracle's currentAmount1, and derives amount2 = ⌈initialWethReportAttoEth * proposedRepPerEthPrice / 1e18⌉ in the same transaction.

Construction bounds and parameter effects

Coordinator construction requires:

  1. positive dispute gas units
  2. positive request gas: gasConsumedSettlement or gasConsumedOpenOracleReportPrice is nonzero
  3. a nonzero initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas
  4. a target error no greater than 100%; shared feeds additionally require it to be strictly below 100%
  5. an OpenOracle security multiplier of at least 1x and at most type(uint256).max / (percentagePrecision + targetPriceErrorForDispute)
  6. combined OpenOracle fees below the target error
  7. a nonzero escalationHaltMultiplierBps
  8. a timestamp clock with dispute tracking (timeType and trackDisputes both true)
  9. maxSettlementBaseFeeMultiplierBps of at least 1x

Construction also bounds initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas so that the legacy priority-fee sizing expression, reportAttoEth(initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas), does not exceed half of min(type(uint128).max, type(uint128).max × 10000 / escalationHaltMultiplierBps); a larger fee reverts with Initial report priority fee exceeds OpenOracle limits. OpenOraclePriceCoordinatorFactory stores the shared configuration without validating it, so an invalid configuration deploys the factory successfully and makes every later coordinator deployment revert.

Settlement validation, rejection, and recovery

Low-level callback failure

OpenOracle invokes the price source through a low-level callback whose success is intentionally neither stored nor emitted. A failed callback does not automatically undo an otherwise valid OpenOracle settlement. A shared escrow authenticates OpenOracle and its own report ID; the feed accepts settlement only from that candidate’s registered escrow. Failure can leave an individually settled candidate unprocessed without blocking other candidates.

If the settlement transaction cannot retain enough gas headroom after the callback attempt, OpenOracle reverts settlement with InvalidGasLimit. In that case, the report has not settled and coordinator recovery is not yet available.

Price-source settlement validation

A callback that successfully enters the price source can still produce a rejected price. The price source emits PriceReportRejected and leaves the price cache unchanged when the first applicable condition below holds. A shared rejection records reportOutcome(reportId) = 2 and clears the latest-candidate getters only if they still name that report:

  • for a shared feed, the portfolio reporting window is closed: Portfolio reporting window closed;
  • the settlement block’s base fee exceeds the request-time cap: the feed uses request block.basefee + initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas. This is scaled by maxSettlementBaseFeeMultiplierBps / 10000: Base fee too high;
  • storedGame(reportId).numReports is saturated at the uint24 maximum: Counter saturated;
  • numReports is zero, or the settled collateral amount is below the configured floor plus ⌈live aggregate exposure / 100⌉: Report uneconomic. Final REP funding must also cover the live REP inventory minimum;
  • for a shared feed, settlement eligibility is earlier than the accepted observation (Report timestamp not newer), or equal without larger funded coverage (Report adds no newer observation or capacity);
  • the freshness window measured from settlement eligibility has already closed: Report stale;
  • either settled token amount is zero: Empty oracle settlement;
  • the integer REP/collateral calculation produces a zero price: Oracle price is zero.

These checks establish only that the final collateral position meets the configured stake and exposure floors and that the callback data is structurally acceptable. They do not prove that the accepted price is externally correct, that an independent corrector exists, or that a correcting transaction will obtain inclusion.

Rejected-price cleanup

A rejected candidate leaves the accepted price unchanged. It cannot cancel other reports or reserve execution rights. Capital transactions remain subject to their own fresh state checks.

Recovering a settled report after callback failure

Anyone can recover a settled candidate through its report escrow. Shared feeds expose recoverReport(reportId) for any unprocessed settled candidate; recoverSettledPendingReport() is only a convenience for the latest ID. Shared recovery marks that candidate rejected and releases its escrow credits without accepting a price. Independently, anyone may call an escrow’s withdrawFunding() to deliver already-released credits to its original sponsor, including credits released by a dispute before settlement. Each escrow isolates those credits from other reports.

Liveness and exposure limits

Capital growth requires the accepted coverage or an independently funded proof. The feed does not impose an absolute deadline on an individual dispute game; shared candidates can proceed independently when another game remains disputed. See excluded guarantees EG03 and EG04.

Dispute escalation amounts

In this integration, collateral is the escalation variable. Before escalationHalt, OpenOracle requires min(⌊oldAmount1 × multiplier / 100⌋, escalationHalt). With the configured multiplier of 115, integer flooring leaves token1 amounts from one through six raw collateral units unchanged; seven units is the first amount that grows. At or above the halt, each replacement instead requires oldAmount1 + 1, adding one raw collateral unit.
