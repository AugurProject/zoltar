OpenOracle integration reference

OpenOracle supplies a fresh REP/collateral price for Statoblast's solvency-sensitive operations. It does not decide market truth; local Escalation Games and Zoltar remain responsible for truth and branching.

The OpenOracle explanation covers why Statoblast uses a contestable price and which operations depend on it. This page specifies the integration: formulas, parameters, and settlement-callback behavior. Per-function callers, preconditions, and events are in the feed contract reference.

Token pair and cached price

Statoblast configures the security pool’s immutable collateral token as token1 and universe REP as token2. Reports quote their direct raw-unit ratio, without a collateral/ETH feed:

The price is raw REP units per raw collateral unit, scaled by 1018. The callback does not normalize decimals. For six-decimal USDC, one REP per whole collateral token is encoded as 1030; for 18-decimal WETH it is 1018.

The reference OpenOracle arbitrager always supports WETH collateral. It disputes and settles reports for a non-WETH security pool only when the token meets the bot’s transfer and allowance requirements and the operator allowlists it; it skips every other collateral. For an allowlisted token it hedges REP against that token and values profit and risk in WETH through the token's Uniswap V3 WETH pool. Security pools whose collateral no running bot allowlists still require an independent corrector and settler.

An accepted report with a strictly newer eligibility timestamp becomes the feed's cached REP/collateral price. lastSettlementTimestamp records settlement eligibility: the final report timestamp plus settlementTime. The freshness window starts there, so settling late cannot extend it. The shared feed freshness window is settlementTime plus one hour on Sepolia or five minutes on other chains. With the mainnet eight-minute challenge period, a shared price is usable for 780 seconds after eligibility, or at most 21 minutes after the final report. priceValidUntil() returns the canonical expiry; a price is stale at that timestamp. The feed does not meter operation volume during the window. Independently challenged candidates with equal eligibility timestamps may each be accepted and execute their own authorized operation batches; equality alone is not a rejection reason. Only a strictly newer eligibility timestamp replaces the shared price, exposure limits and epoch. For equal timestamps, the first accepted report supplies those shared values; settlement ordering can therefore influence the initial choice, but later equal-time settlements cannot overwrite it.

A shared feed's aggregate exposure is the largest of current security pool commitment, backing demand and the maximum commitment a preparing branch auction can add to chosen backing. The feed reads these aggregates without scanning questions or bidders. Escalation halts use aggregate exposure.

A shared feed permits concurrent candidates, including renewal before the accepted price expires. Each candidate has its own SharedPoolReportEscrow, which holds its reporter identity and funding credits. pendingReportId and the associated pending getters identify only the latest candidate; they do not enumerate or exclude other pending candidates. A pending, disputed or rejected report never extends accepted-price freshness.

Accepting a strictly newer shared observation atomically checkpoints security pool fees under the previous price and updates capital eligibility. A failed mandatory checkpoint rolls back that acceptance. Ordinary capital operations execute separately and recheck current safety constraints against the accepted price. An optional batch of sponsor-authorized capital operations is attempted immediately after acceptance.

The request form shows current underwriting exposure, the exposure its selected report stake can cover, and the remaining growth headroom. Commitments can grow while a report waits to settle. Settlement therefore rechecks funding against current exposure: accepting an underfunded price could weaken the correction incentive just as more capital depends on it. An underfunded report is rejected. Any attached operations follow the canonical-price fallback. Headroom is shared and can change before inclusion; it is not a capacity reservation. REP exposure coverage remains a separate required bound.

Accepted report capacity is shared across security pool owners; sponsoring a larger report does not reserve its added capacity. Another owner can consume it before a separate commitment-growth transaction. Attach growth to requestSecurityPoolPriceWithOperations to attempt it atomically after acceptance, with an operation gas allowance, deadline and price bounds. Each operation still rechecks safety constraints and can fail independently; inspect ReportOperationAttempted before treating growth as complete.

Security pool branch preparation

SecurityPoolFactory.prepareSecurityPoolPriceFeed(source, universeId) prepares the canonical child feed without deploying a child question. source must be a canonical question ledger already admitted to this security pool, with its security pool binding recorded by the factory. Security pool configuration is supplied directly at deployment; preparing its initial shared feed does not require a seed question. SecurityPool.isPriceReportingOpen(universeId) permits requests and acceptance after startBranch, until the fixed native recapitalization deadline at week nine. From that deadline until activation, the accepted observation is frozen. Reporting reopens on activation if the child universe has not forked; a forked universe rejects new prices. Late branch preparation does not extend the window.

A strictly newer accepted preparation report caches the price and funded exposure; it does not activate trading, start holding fees or start imported positions’ liquidation history. Activation uses the cached price only while it remains fresh. A stale or absent price does not block branch activation. Callbacks during a closed window reject the candidate price while preserving report funding recovery.

Shared report coverage and capital prices

minimumToken2ReportAttoRep() requires one hundredth of pledged underwriting REP plus any fresh fork-auction sale inventory, rounded up. Pledged REP is included because a liquidation auction can transfer all of it. minimumToken1ReportAttoEthForPrice(price) also raises the collateral minimum enough to fund that REP amount at the proposed price. The shared feed rejects a signed collateral budget below the required amount. Acceptance checks both sides: acceptedRepExposureLimitAttoRep is final REP funding times 100. An arbitrarily cheap proposed REP price therefore cannot remove the REP inventory challenge requirement.

A canonical acceptance sets acceptedExposureLimitAttoEth to (final collateral − configured collateral stake floor) × 100 and increments acceptedEpoch. An ordinary setCommitment raise cannot lift aggregate exposure above that accepted limit. An owner can also call setCommitmentWithReport(reportId, commitment) using a fresh report the shared feed accepted whose final funding covers the projected exposure. reportAuthorization(reportId, newExposure) validates that proof even if a newer, smaller canonical report superseded it.

For accepted price q, target error e, and percentage precision P = 10,000,000, the upper bound is q × P / (P − e), rounded up, and the liquidation bound is q × P / (P + e), rounded down. Capital entry, withdrawals, capacity and fee eligibility use the conservative upper bound; rewarded adverse actions use the lower bound. Proof authorization also retains the higher of the owner’s current conservative price and the proof’s upper bound for that owner’s collateral checks until a later canonical epoch covers the enlarged live exposure. Selecting a smaller report cannot remove this owner-specific floor.

Report flags

The feed always submits flags 7: FLAG_TIME_TYPE (1, a timestamp clock), FLAG_TRACK_DISPUTES (2, per-dispute history), and FLAG_STORE_ALL (4, the complete report state in storage). It never sets the optional flags 8, 16, 32, or 64; the OpenOracle provenance record identifies the vendored source and build profile.

Integration architecture

Each question references the canonical SharedPoolPriceFeed for its security pool and universe. The feed owns the accepted REP/collateral price and freshness clock. Every candidate has its own escrow. The factory binds each question to its canonical feed; exposure checkpoints authenticate the calling ledger against factory records.

A child universe gets a new feed for its own REP token, initially with no accepted price. Adding a question does not reset an existing feed. Capital deposits, commitments and retirement use the security pool directly. Capital operations may also execute through an ordered sponsor-authorized batch attached to a shared report.

Price request lifecycle

  1. The sponsor approves collateral and universe REP, chooses a candidate price and report budget, and opens a report directly through the shared feed.
  2. OpenOracle accepts valid corrections during the challenge window. A correction replaces the current reporter and restarts settlement timing.
  3. After the challenge window, anyone can settle the report. Its isolated escrow validates the result and offers it to the shared feed.
  4. The feed accepts only a qualifying, fresh observation with sufficient economic coverage. Rejected candidates cannot invalidate the previously accepted observation.
  5. Owners and keepers normally submit capital operations separately. A report may instead carry a sponsor-authorized operation batch; both paths recheck current safety constraints.

Escalation reports use wallet REP in separate reporter custody. They do not consume underwriting REP or use an oracle operation queue.

Funding and report ownership

The sponsor is the caller requesting a price report and funding its collateral, REP, transaction gas and public settlement bounty. Whoever settles earns the bounty as an OpenOracle ETH credit, including the sponsor if they settle their own report. Settlement does not immediately return that ETH to a wallet: the credited caller collects it through OpenOracle’s withdraw(address(0), amount), available under Oracle balances in the OpenOracle app. Returned report collateral and REP belong to the recorded sponsor.

A negligible bounty leaves settlement largely to sponsors. A sponsor can wait after a candidate becomes eligible and accept its older price only when that price favours a withdrawal or takeover. The gas-funded minimum encourages independent settlers to accept eligible reports promptly, reducing that timing advantage. It does not guarantee a settler, timely inclusion, or a current market price.

The minimum bounty is the callback gas budget, plus its EIP-150 forwarding allowance and 310,000 gas for settlement overhead and reward collection, padded by 20% plus 20,000 gas for the two transaction margins, multiplied by the configured settlement fee allowance, plus 101 attoETH. The fee allowance uses the request block's base fee plus the configured priority fee, multiplied by the settlement base-fee multiplier. The ordinary callback budget is 4,000,000 gas; attached operation batches add their declared gas budget. Sponsors can offer more. A rising inclusion-time base fee can require a new quote; the contract rejects an insufficient bounty instead of charging more silently.

requestSecurityPoolPrice(proposedRepPerEthPrice, requestedInitialAttoWeth, bountyAttoEth) transfers exactly the reviewed collateral budget and its corresponding REP from the caller. It rejects a budget below the inclusion-time minimum instead of silently increasing it. Security pool settlement collateral does not fund reports.

Both shared-feed request methods commit the specified bounty and refund excess native ETH to the caller after storing the candidate and any operation batch. Rejecting that refund reverts the whole request.

Sponsor-authorized operation batches

requestSecurityPoolPriceWithOperations(price, wethBudget, bounty, batch) funds a direct report and records ordered capital-ledger calls. The funding caller supplies the owner authority for every call; withdrawal and earnings recipients remain explicit in the signed calldata. A batch fixes accepted-price bounds, an expiry, a total gas allowance, and an array of operations containing calldata and individual gas limits. There is no fixed operation-count cap; each operation's calldata is at most MAXIMUM_REPORT_OPERATION_DATA_BYTES (512 bytes).

The ledger supports takeOver, takeOverWithBounds, takeOverRepairAuctionBid, depositFor, depositAndSetHealthTarget, withdrawPrincipal, setHealthTarget, setCommitment, setCommitmentWithReport, claimEarnings, serveRetirement and materializeRepairAuctionBid. Privileged accounting and fork entrypoints are excluded. Each call retains its ordinary owner, collateral, cohort, funding and reentrancy checks. A report-bound takeover may use zero for expectedAcceptedPriceEpoch to accept the settlement epoch; direct takeovers retain exact epoch checks. Funding can be deposited beforehand or by an earlier batch operation with the required REP approval.

The declared batch allowance must cover each individual limit plus 100,000 gas per operation. The OpenOracle callback budget is 4,000,000 gas plus that allowance; ordinary reports retain 4,000,000. A request whose callback budget would exceed 10,000,000 gas reverts with Callback gas exceeds limit, which leaves at most 6,000,000 for the batch. After mandatory acceptance and security pool checkpoints, the feed attempts every operation in order through executeReportOperation, which accepts only the canonical feed and authenticates a ledger self-call as the sponsor. Failed operations, including exhausted individual allowances and reverted auction materialization, roll back individually; later operations still run. Expired or out-of-range batches emit failed outcomes without executing their calls. ReportOperationAttempted(reportId, sponsor, operationIndex, success) records each outcome. A settlement that forwards less than the callback budget cannot publish a price after skipping a promised attempt: it reverts as described under callback gas.

The batch runs inside its report's acceptance transaction regardless of who settles. It receives no priority over another report that settles first, no exclusive target reservation, and no reimbursement. Practical transaction and block gas limits bound execution. sponsorOperationReport(sponsor) identifies the latest batch report; its terms are the arguments of the request transaction. Independent reports and ordinary recovery remain available.

Independent candidates

Reporting has no exclusive sponsor lane. Anyone may fund another candidate while earlier candidates are pending or the accepted price is still fresh. Disputing one candidate cannot reserve all reporting access.

Report sizing and correction incentives

The creator sets a positive minimumReportCollateral in raw collateral units at security pool creation. It is immutable and inherited by every universe in the security pool. The minimum report adds that floor to one hundredth of aggregate collateral exposure, rounded up, whether or not the accepted price is fresh. Unused capacity from an oversized or escalated report does not raise renewal funding. A pending renewal does not reserve capacity or block independently funded growth. If actual exposure grows beyond its final funding before settlement, the renewal’s price is rejected as Report uneconomic. If a separately accepted canonical price is still fresh, the sponsor’s queued operations are attempted under that price and their signed bounds; the rejected report grants no growth authorization. Sponsors may fund headroom to tolerate growth. REP exposure coverage can raise the minimum further at the proposed price.

minimumToken1ReportAttoEth=minimumReportCollateral+⌈aggregateExposure100⌉

Amounts use raw collateral units, including accessors with legacy AttoEth names. The escalation halt remains the larger of one hundredth of aggregate exposure, rounded up, and the configured fraction of initial collateral funding.

The creator must size this floor to satisfy A18: independent, gas-economic correction throughout the security pool’s use, including at low exposure. This is a system assumption, not an onchain gas-cost guarantee. No collateral/ETH feed is queried, and a higher floor requires a new security pool.

The feed requires requestedInitialAttoWeth to meet minimumToken1ReportAttoEthForPrice(proposedRepPerEthPrice) at inclusion. It uses the signed collateral amount as OpenOracle's currentAmount1 and derives amount2 = ⌈currentAmount1 × proposedRepPerEthPrice / 1e18⌉.

Construction bounds and parameter effects

Feed factory construction requires:

  1. a deployed shared-feed deployment worker
  2. a target error strictly below 100%, with combined OpenOracle fees below that error
  3. a nonzero escalationHaltMultiplierBps
  4. settlementTime greater than disputeDelay, and a dispute multiplier of at least 100
  5. maxSettlementBaseFeeMultiplierBps of at least 1x
  6. minLiquidationPriceDistanceBps no greater than 100%

The priority-fee bound uses the fixed gas and security factors above and half the smaller of the maximum report amount and the escalation-halt capacity. A larger fee reverts with Initial report priority fee exceeds OpenOracle limits. SharedPoolPriceFeedFactory validates report timing, fees and risk configuration at construction. Each feed implementation validates its positive priority fee against that configuration.

Settlement validation, rejection, and recovery

Low-level callback failure

OpenOracle invokes the price source through a low-level callback whose success is intentionally neither stored nor emitted. A failed callback does not automatically undo an otherwise valid OpenOracle settlement. A shared escrow authenticates OpenOracle and its own report ID; the feed accepts settlement only from that candidate’s registered escrow. Failure can leave an individually settled candidate unprocessed without blocking other candidates.

If the settlement transaction cannot retain enough gas headroom after the callback attempt, OpenOracle reverts settlement with InvalidGasLimit. In that case, the report has not settled and feed recovery is not yet available. A shared report escrow enforces this for an under-funded settlement: when its callback is entered with less than the report's callback budget minus 100,000 gas, it consumes all the gas it was given, so the settlement reverts with InvalidGasLimit instead of settling without accepting the price.

Price-source settlement validation

A callback that successfully enters the price source can still produce a rejected price. The price source emits PriceReportRejected and leaves the price cache unchanged when the first applicable condition below holds. A shared rejection records reportOutcome(reportId) = 2 and clears the latest-candidate getters only if they still name that report:

  • for a shared feed, the security pool reporting window is closed: Security pool reporting window closed;
  • storedGame(reportId).numReports is saturated at the uint24 maximum: Counter saturated;
  • numReports is zero, or the settled collateral amount is below the configured floor plus ⌈live aggregate exposure / 100⌉: Report uneconomic;
  • final REP funding is below the live REP exposure minimum: Report REP inventory budget insufficient;
  • for a shared feed, settlement eligibility is earlier than the accepted observation (Report timestamp not newer);
  • the freshness window measured from settlement eligibility has already closed: Report stale;
  • either settled token amount is zero: Empty oracle settlement;
  • the integer REP/collateral calculation produces a zero price: Oracle price is zero.

Each report also records a settlement base-fee cap: request block.basefee + initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas, scaled by maxSettlementBaseFeeMultiplierBps / 10000. Because anyone may choose the settlement block, a base fee above the cap never rejects a report. When a candidate passes price validation, or its signed operations can use a fresh canonical price, and the base fee exceeds the cap, the feed emits PriceReportDeferred(reportId, baseFee, maxSettlementBaseFee) instead. OpenOracle completes settlement and pays the final reporter. Released funding remains available through the report escrow’s separate withdrawal methods. The report stays pending: reportOutcome(reportId) stays 0, the latest-candidate getters keep naming it, and no price or sponsor operation is applied. Recovery completes it later, as described below.

These checks establish only that the final collateral position meets the configured stake and exposure floors and that the callback data is structurally acceptable. They do not prove that the accepted price is externally correct, that an independent corrector exists, or that a correcting transaction will obtain inclusion.

The shared feed emits PriceCandidateAccepted with the candidate price, the resulting canonical price, the observation timestamp and whether the canonical observation changed. Equal-time acceptance leaves the existing canonical observation in place. Sponsor operations still use the canonical price when checking their signed price bounds. PriceReported carries the resulting canonical price for each acceptance.

Rejected-price cleanup

A rejected candidate leaves the accepted price unchanged. Economic insufficiency or an older observation does not discard signed operations when a separately accepted canonical price is still fresh. Those operations use the canonical price, original expiry and price bounds; rejection never grants report-based growth authority. Other rejection reasons do not attempt operations. Capital transactions retain their own state checks.

Recovering a settled report after callback failure or deferral

Anyone can call recoverReport(reportId) on the shared feed for an unprocessed settled candidate. Call recoverSettledPendingReport() on the feed to recover the latest pending ID. Shared recovery retries acceptance from the amounts stored by OpenOracle, using the original settlement-eligibility timestamp and the usual freshness, funding and reporting-window checks. A still-valid candidate is accepted and attempts its sponsor operations, replacing the shared price only when its eligibility timestamp is strictly newer; an invalid candidate is rejected. Recovery never transfers funding to the sponsor, so a failed payout cannot roll back report processing. A deferred candidate is recovered the same way: in a block whose base fee is at or below its cap, recovery processes the report with the original settlement-eligibility timestamp, so waiting never extends freshness, and runs its operation batch. Recovery reverts with Base fee too high above the cap when either the candidate price is valid or its signed operations can use a fresh canonical price. The latter path can remain available after the candidate itself expires: validation can first classify it as economically insufficient or older than the canonical observation. A candidate with neither a valid price nor a canonical-operation path is rejected at any base fee.

Withdrawing released report funding

Anyone can call withdrawFunding() on the report escrow to send both available tokens to the original sponsor. Call withdrawTokenFunding(token, recipient) on the escrow to withdraw collateral or REP independently. Anyone can send the token to the sponsor. The sponsor can specify a different recipient other than the escrow. Both methods can withdraw funds released by a dispute before settlement. Each escrow holds credits for one report.

Liveness and exposure limits

The challenge window assumes at least one funded corrector can obtain transaction inclusion before settlement eligibility. Excluding every correction throughout the window can let a wrong price settle; a higher gas bid cannot guarantee inclusion. The settlement base-fee cap can defer acceptance during expensive blocks, but does not prevent selective censorship. Recovery retains the original observation timestamp and cannot extend freshness. See A16 for the inclusion assumption and A18 for correction incentives.

Capital growth requires the accepted coverage or an accepted report with sufficient funding. The feed does not impose an absolute deadline on an individual dispute game; shared candidates can proceed independently when another game remains disputed. See excluded guarantees EG03 and EG04.

Dispute escalation amounts

In this integration, collateral is the escalation variable. Before escalationHalt, OpenOracle requires min(⌊oldAmount1 × multiplier / 100⌋, escalationHalt). With the configured multiplier of 115, integer flooring leaves token1 amounts from one through six raw collateral units unchanged; seven units is the first amount that grows. At or above the halt, each replacement instead requires oldAmount1 + 1, adding one raw collateral unit.
