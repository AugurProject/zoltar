OpenOracle integration reference

OpenOracle supplies a fresh REP/ETH price for Statoblast's solvency-sensitive operations. It does not decide market truth; local Escalation Games and Zoltar remain responsible for truth and branching.

The OpenOracle explanation covers why Statoblast uses a contestable price and which operations depend on it. This page specifies the integration: formulas, parameters, and settlement-callback behavior. Per-function callers, preconditions, and events are in the coordinator contract reference.

Token pair and cached price

Statoblast configures WETH as token1 and REP as token2. Keeping WETH on the exact side lets the coordinator size the report directly from ETH-denominated gas costs without first assuming a REP/ETH conversion price. When a report settles, the coordinator converts the settled token amounts into REP per ETH:

This raw-amount ratio is valid because the configured WETH and REP contracts both use 18 decimals. The coordinator does not normalize token decimals in the settlement callback.

An accepted report becomes the coordinator's cached REP/ETH price. lastSettlementTimestamp records settlement eligibility: the final report timestamp plus settlementTime. The freshness window starts there, so settling late cannot extend it. The shared feed freshness window is settlementTime plus one hour on Sepolia or five minutes on other chains. With the mainnet eight-minute challenge period, a shared price is usable for 780 seconds after eligibility, or at most 21 minutes after the final report. priceValidUntil() returns the canonical expiry; a price is stale at that timestamp. The coordinator does not meter operation volume during the window.

Shared feeds size reports and escalation halts against the largest of current portfolio commitment, backing demand and the maximum commitment a preparing branch auction can add to chosen backing. The feed reads these aggregates without scanning questions or bidders. Unused capacity from an oversized earlier report does not increase the minimum size of the next report.

A shared feed permits concurrent candidates, including renewal before the accepted price expires. Each candidate has its own SharedPoolReportEscrow, which holds its reporter identity and funding credits. pendingReportId and the associated pending getters identify only the latest candidate; they do not enumerate or exclude other pending candidates. A pending, disputed or rejected report never extends accepted-price freshness.

Shared acceptance atomically checkpoints portfolio fees under the previous price and updates capital eligibility. A failed mandatory checkpoint rolls back acceptance. Capital operations execute separately and recheck current safety constraints against the accepted price.

Portfolio branch preparation

SecurityPoolFactory.preparePortfolioPriceFeed(sourcePool, child) prepares the canonical child feed without deploying a child question. sourcePool must be a canonical question pool already admitted to this portfolio, with its portfolio binding recorded by the factory. The configuration seed is itself a canonical portfolio question. SecurityPoolPortfolio.isPriceReportingOpen(child) permits requests and acceptance after startBranch, until the fixed native recapitalization deadline at week nine. From that deadline until activation, the accepted observation is frozen. Reporting reopens on activation if the child universe has not forked; a forked universe rejects new prices. Late branch preparation does not extend the window.

An accepted preparation report caches the price and funded exposure; it does not activate trading, start holding fees or start imported positions’ liquidation history. Activation uses the cached price only while it remains fresh. A stale or absent price does not block branch activation. Callbacks during a closed window reject the candidate price while preserving report funding recovery.

Shared report coverage and capital prices

During auction preparation, minimumToken2ReportAttoRep() requires one hundredth of fresh sale inventory, rounded up. minimumToken1ReportAttoEthForPrice(price) also raises the WETH minimum enough to fund that REP amount at the proposed price. Direct sponsorship through requestPortfolioPrice rejects a signed WETH budget below the required amount. Acceptance checks both sides: acceptedRepExposureLimitAttoRep is final REP funding times 100. An arbitrarily cheap proposed REP price therefore cannot remove the REP inventory challenge requirement.

A canonical acceptance sets acceptedExposureLimitAttoEth to (final WETH − priority-fee report component) × 100 and increments acceptedEpoch. An ordinary setCommitment raise cannot lift aggregate exposure above that accepted limit. Pending funding grants no capacity. To raise a commitment, an owner can instead call setCommitmentWithReport(reportId, commitment) with an independently settled, fresh candidate whose final funding covers the projected total exposure. reportAuthorization(reportId, newExposure) validates that proof even if a newer, smaller canonical report superseded it.

For accepted price q, target error e, and percentage precision P = 10,000,000, the upper bound is q × P / (P − e), rounded up, and the liquidation bound is q × P / (P + e), rounded down. Capital entry, withdrawals, capacity and fee eligibility use the conservative upper bound; rewarded adverse actions use the lower bound. Proof authorization also retains the higher of the owner’s current conservative price and the proof’s upper bound for that owner’s collateral checks until a later canonical epoch covers the enlarged live exposure. Selecting a smaller report cannot remove this owner-specific floor.

Report flags

The coordinator always submits flags 7: FLAG_TIME_TYPE (1, a timestamp clock), FLAG_TRACK_DISPUTES (2, per-dispute history), and FLAG_STORE_ALL (4, the complete report state in storage). It never sets the optional flags 8, 16, 32, or 64; the OpenOracle provenance record identifies the vendored source and build profile.

Integration architecture

Each question has an OpenOraclePriceCoordinator adapter attached to one SharedPoolPriceFeed per portfolio and universe. The feed owns the accepted REP/ETH price and freshness clock. Every candidate has its own escrow. The factory authenticates coordinator creation and binds each question once.

A child universe gets a new feed for its own REP token, initially with no accepted price. Adding a question does not reset an existing feed. Capital deposits, commitments, retirement and liquidation use the portfolio directly; they are not queued by the coordinator.

Price request lifecycle

  1. The sponsor approves WETH and universe REP, chooses a candidate price and report budget, and opens a report through a question coordinator or the shared feed.
  2. OpenOracle accepts valid corrections during the challenge window. A correction replaces the current reporter and restarts settlement timing.
  3. After the challenge window, anyone can settle the report. Its isolated escrow validates the result and offers it to the shared feed.
  4. The feed accepts only a qualifying, fresh observation with sufficient economic coverage. Rejected candidates cannot invalidate the previously accepted observation.
  5. Owners and keepers submit capital operations separately. Each transaction rechecks current state, price bounds, coverage and authorization.

Escalation reports use wallet REP in separate reporter custody. They do not consume underwriting REP or use an oracle operation queue.

Funding and report ownership

The sponsor funds its own WETH, REP and transaction gas. The minimum public settlement bounty is 101 attoETH; it does not reimburse the sponsor’s gas. The sponsor may supply a larger bounty.

requestPortfolioPrice(proposedRepPerEthPrice, requestedInitialAttoWeth, bountyAttoEth) transfers exactly the reviewed WETH budget and its corresponding REP from the caller. It rejects a budget below the inclusion-time minimum instead of silently increasing it. Only the recorded sponsor receives that candidate’s returned funding. Portfolio settlement collateral does not fund reports.

A coordinator request forwards the specified bounty and refunds excess native ETH to the caller. Rejecting that refund reverts the whole request. A direct feed request requires exactly its specified bounty.

Independent candidates

There is no exclusive sponsor lane. Anyone may fund another candidate while earlier candidates are pending or the accepted price is still fresh. Disputing one candidate cannot reserve all reporting access.

Report sizing and correction incentives

The coordinator sizes each report so that correcting a price error of at least targetPriceErrorForDispute stays profitable after OpenOracle's fees and the modeled gas cost of one dispute multiplied by openOracleSecurityMultiplierBps. That multiplier is separate from a SecurityPool's backing multiplier, statoblastSecurityMultiplierBps.

A report can be corrected in either direction. The formula assumes the less profitable one, in which the true REP/ETH price is (1 + targetError) times the reported price, and models the correction as releasing the fraction (targetError - fees) / (1 + targetError) of the WETH position before gas. The required position is the multiplied gas cost divided by that fraction.

The minimum initial WETH position also has a floor of 1% of the portfolio’s aggregate exposure (aggregateExposureAttoEth()), and the same floor applies to the escalation halt:

reportAttoEth(gasPriceAttoEthPerGas) = ⌈ gasPriceAttoEthPerGas⋅gasUnitsForOneDispute⋅openOracleSecurityMultiplierBps ⋅(percentagePrecision+targetPriceErrorForDispute) 10000⋅(targetPriceErrorForDispute-protocolFee-feePercentage) ⌉ exposureReportAttoEth = ⌈aggregateExposureAttoEth100⌉ minimumToken1ReportAttoEth = reportAttoEth(initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas) + max(reportAttoEth(block.basefee),exposureReportAttoEth) escalationHaltAttoEth = max(⌊initialWethReportAttoEth⋅escalationHaltMultiplierBps10000⌋,exposureReportAttoEth)

All amounts are in attoETH. percentagePrecision is 10,000,000, the denominator of targetPriceErrorForDispute, protocolFee, and feePercentage.

This is the ETH-exposure floor. The feed also requires REP-side funding of at least 1% of its REP exposure. At a proposed price, converting that REP minimum to ETH can increase the required initial WETH above this floor.

The coordinator sets initialWethReportAttoEth = max(minimumToken1ReportAttoEthForPrice(proposedRepPerEthPrice), requestedInitialAttoWeth), uses that value as OpenOracle's currentAmount1, and derives amount2 = ⌈initialWethReportAttoEth * proposedRepPerEthPrice / 1e18⌉ in the same transaction.

Construction bounds and parameter effects

Coordinator construction requires:

  1. positive dispute gas units
  2. positive request gas: gasConsumedSettlement or gasConsumedOpenOracleReportPrice is nonzero
  3. a nonzero initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas
  4. a target error no greater than 100%; shared feeds additionally require it to be strictly below 100%
  5. an OpenOracle security multiplier of at least 1x and at most type(uint256).max / (percentagePrecision + targetPriceErrorForDispute)
  6. combined OpenOracle fees below the target error
  7. a nonzero escalationHaltMultiplierBps
  8. a timestamp clock with dispute tracking (timeType and trackDisputes both true)
  9. maxSettlementBaseFeeMultiplierBps of at least 1x

Construction also bounds initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas so that the priority-fee component of the minimum report, reportAttoEth(initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas), does not exceed half of min(type(uint128).max, type(uint128).max × 10000 / escalationHaltMultiplierBps); a larger fee reverts with Initial report priority fee exceeds OpenOracle limits. OpenOraclePriceCoordinatorFactory stores the shared configuration without validating it, so an invalid configuration deploys the factory successfully and makes every later coordinator deployment revert.

At a 30 nanoETH base fee, a configured 10 nanoETH priority fee, and 100 WETH of aggregate exposure, the modeled gas cost of one dispute is 0.012 ETH, the minimum report is 3.230769230769230770 WETH, and the escalation halt is 32.307692307692307700 WETH.

Settlement validation, rejection, and recovery

Low-level callback failure

OpenOracle invokes the price source through a low-level callback whose success is intentionally neither stored nor emitted. A failed callback does not automatically undo an otherwise valid OpenOracle settlement. A shared escrow authenticates OpenOracle and its own report ID; the feed accepts settlement only from that candidate’s registered escrow. Failure can leave an individually settled candidate unprocessed without blocking other candidates.

If the settlement transaction cannot retain enough gas headroom after the callback attempt, OpenOracle reverts settlement with InvalidGasLimit. In that case, the report has not settled and coordinator recovery is not yet available.

Price-source settlement validation

A callback that successfully enters the price source can still produce a rejected price. The price source emits PriceReportRejected and leaves the price cache unchanged when the first applicable condition below holds. A shared rejection records reportOutcome(reportId) = 2 and clears the latest-candidate getters only if they still name that report:

  • for a shared feed, the portfolio reporting window is closed: Portfolio reporting window closed;
  • the settlement block’s base fee exceeds the request-time cap: the feed uses request block.basefee + initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas. This is scaled by maxSettlementBaseFeeMultiplierBps / 10000: Base fee too high;
  • storedGame(reportId).numReports is saturated at the uint24 maximum: Counter saturated;
  • numReports is zero, or the settled WETH amount is below reportAttoEth(initialReportPriorityFeeAttoEthPerGas) + reportAttoEth(recordedBaseFee) from the sizing formula, where recordedBaseFee comes from the final disputeHistory(reportId, numReports - 1). Shared mode also requires the amount above the priority-fee component to cover live aggregate exposure / 100, rounded up: Report uneconomic;
  • for a shared feed, settlement eligibility is earlier than the accepted observation (Report timestamp not newer), or equal without larger funded coverage (Report adds no newer observation or capacity);
  • the freshness window measured from settlement eligibility has already closed: Report stale;
  • either settled token amount is zero: Empty oracle settlement;
  • the integer REP/ETH calculation produces a zero price: Oracle price is zero.

These checks establish only that the final WETH position meets the modeled security floor and that the callback data is structurally acceptable. They do not prove that the accepted price is externally correct, that an independent corrector exists, or that a correcting transaction will obtain inclusion.

Rejected-price cleanup

A rejected candidate leaves the accepted price unchanged. It cannot cancel other reports or reserve execution rights. Capital transactions remain subject to their own fresh state checks.

Recovering a settled report after callback failure

Anyone can recover a settled candidate through its report escrow. Shared feeds expose recoverReport(reportId) for any unprocessed settled candidate; recoverSettledPendingReport() is only a convenience for the latest ID. Shared recovery marks that candidate rejected and releases its escrow credits without accepting a price. Independently, anyone may call an escrow’s withdrawFunding() to deliver already-released credits to its original sponsor, including credits released by a dispute before settlement. Each escrow isolates those credits from other reports.

Liveness and exposure limits

Capital growth requires the accepted coverage or an independently funded proof. The feed does not impose an absolute deadline on an individual dispute game; shared candidates can proceed independently when another game remains disputed. See excluded guarantees EG03 and EG04.

Dispute escalation amounts

In this integration, WETH is the escalation variable. Before escalationHalt, OpenOracle requires min(⌊oldAmount1 × multiplier / 100⌋, escalationHalt). With the configured multiplier of 115, integer flooring leaves token1 amounts from one through six attoETH unchanged; seven attoETH is the first amount that grows. At or above the halt, each replacement instead requires oldAmount1 + 1, adding one attoETH of WETH.
