Why Statoblast uses OpenOracle

OpenOracle supplies a fresh REP/ETH price for solvency-sensitive operations. It does not decide market truth; escalation and Zoltar remain responsible for truth and branching.

Economic purpose

A cached price is convenient but expires. A stale price cannot safely authorize a withdrawal, coverage change, or liquidation, so the coordinator stages the operation behind a new report. Report size, duration, liquidity, and correction incentives trade off cost against the ability to keep a price current.

What requires a fresh price

Withdrawals, coverage-commitment changes, and liquidations change how much REP security backs ETH-denominated pool exposure. The coordinator therefore checks the REP/ETH price immediately when the cached value is fresh or stages the operation behind a new report when it is stale. A fresh price does not bypass the pool's downstream vault-health, lifecycle, or snapshot checks.

One report lifecycle

operation requested → cached price checked → immediate execution or report opened → report corrected or left unchanged → report settled → coordinator accepts or rejects → queued operation performs its own safety checks.

For a worked price, a settled report with amount1 = 0.8 WETH and amount2 = 1,600 REP produces lastPrice = 1,600 / 0.8 = 2,000 REP/ETH, represented onchain as 2,000 × 10¹⁸. The coordinator then applies that accepted price to each queued operation's own pool checks.

Security intuition

The triggering sponsor funds the initial report, an independent corrector can replace it before settlement, and a settler closes the report after the dispute window. Settlement makes a report usable; it does not prove that the external price is correct. A callback then lets the coordinator accept or reject the report, while each queued operation still undergoes its own pool-specific checks.

Correction depends on capital and timely inclusion, and disputes can delay settlement indefinitely while valid replacement positions remain profitable. Accepted-price notional is not capped by the coordinator, so these are economic and liveness boundaries rather than absolute guarantees. See A16 timely inclusion, A19 observable correctable price, A20 data availability, and the security appendix.

Boundaries and alternatives

Using a stale cached price would reduce latency but expose pool solvency to old market information. Reading a DEX spot price is simpler but exposes the operation to pool liquidity and manipulation; a TWAP reduces instantaneous manipulation but introduces lag; a conventional external feed may reduce report mechanics while adding an external trust and update dependency. Sharing one global operation queue would simplify deployment but couple unrelated pools. Per-pool coordinators preserve local pending state and freshness decisions.

Guarantees and non-guarantees

  • Settlement proves that the report followed OpenOracle's settlement process; it does not prove that the external-market price is objectively correct.
  • A valid cached price does not bypass downstream vault and pool checks.
  • The coordinator does not cap the total economic value of operations that may use one accepted price.
  • Correction and settlement remain dependent on capital, inclusion, and available chain data.
