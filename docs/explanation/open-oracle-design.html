Why Statoblast uses OpenOracle

OpenOracle supplies a fresh REP/ETH price for solvency-sensitive operations. It does not decide market truth; escalation and Zoltar remain responsible for truth and branching.

Economic purpose

A cached price is convenient but expires. A stale price cannot safely authorize a withdrawal, coverage change, or liquidation, so the coordinator stages the operation behind a new report. Report size, duration, liquidity, and correction incentives trade off cost against the ability to keep a price current.

Security intuition

The triggering sponsor funds the initial report, an independent corrector can replace it before settlement, and a settler closes the report after the dispute window. Settlement makes a report usable; it does not prove that the external price is correct. A callback then lets the coordinator accept or reject the report, while each queued operation still undergoes its own pool-specific checks.

Correction depends on capital and timely inclusion, and disputes can delay settlement indefinitely while valid replacement positions remain profitable. Accepted-price notional is not capped by the coordinator, so these are economic and liveness boundaries rather than absolute guarantees. See A16 timely inclusion, A19 observable correctable price, A20 data availability, and the security appendix.

Boundaries and alternatives

Using a stale cached price would reduce latency but expose pool solvency to old market information. Sharing one global operation queue would simplify deployment but couple unrelated pools. Per-pool coordinators preserve local pending state and freshness decisions.
