Contract architecture

Users call SecurityPools, the Statoblast contracts that hold each market's ETH. Pools rely on Zoltar for universes and REP, on a price coordinator for REP/ETH prices, and on the SecurityPoolForker after a fork.

This map shows contract-to-contract calls and asset movement, not every public user entrypoint. Deployment-helper factories, delegate modules (helper contracts whose code a pool, an Escalation Game, or the SecurityPoolForker runs through delegatecall), the coordinator's VaultOperations executor, event emitters, and external contracts such as WETH are omitted; the contract inventory lists them.

Related reading: the generated transaction reference for exact caller and prerequisite rules, How Statoblast works for lifecycle context, and the invariant catalog for cross-contract safety properties.

Product layers

What each contract owns

Statoblast separates identity, market accounting, price-sensitive execution, dispute escrow, and fork repair:

  • ZoltarQuestionData stores question state and answer encoding; Zoltar manages universes, forks, and each universe's REP token.
  • A SecurityPool tracks market collateral, vault accounting, open interest, and lifecycle state.
  • The ShareToken tracks outcome-share balances for one lineage: an origin pool and every child pool forked from it.
  • The OpenOraclePriceCoordinator queues vault operations that need a fresh REP/ETH price and runs them immediately while its cached price is valid, otherwise after OpenOracle settles a new report. An operation waiting for that report is a staged operation.
  • The EscalationGame holds the REP that reporters stake in the pool's local dispute.
  • The SecurityPoolForker creates child pools after a fork and repairs any ETH shortfall through a Truth Auction.

Each lineage has at most one pool per universe. The table calls that registered pool the canonical pool.

One complete-set path is: a trader calls SecurityPool, the pool accepts ETH collateral, and the pool calls ShareToken to mint one share for every outcome.

Primary interaction flow

Arrows point from the contract initiating an interaction to the contract receiving it. The map has three panels: Deploy & wire, Operate & resolve, and Fork & repair. A contract that takes part in more than one panel is drawn in each. Users and keepers enter through several of these contracts, but are omitted so the plot can focus on contract-to-contract boundaries.

What each arrow means

The Step column names the step within a panel. Deployment and Universe lifecycle belong to Deploy & wire; Share migration, Fork snapshot, Fork migration, and Backing repair belong to Fork & repair; every other step belongs to Operate & resolve.

OpenOraclePriceCoordinator SecurityPool Risk execution Once a fresh price is accepted, calls the executor-only liquidation, REP-withdrawal, or underwriting-commitment entrypoint through its VaultOperations executor, the only caller the pool accepts for them. A liquidation proceeds only if the target vault's REP backing and underwriting commitment are unchanged since it was queued, and moves part or all of that commitment with matching REP backing to the receiver vault, the vault that takes over the commitment. A withdrawal keeps the commitment and must leave it fully backed.
