Explanation

How Trading works

Trading is a separate on-chain exchange for Statoblast outcome shares, one of possibly many that can be built on Statoblast. Statoblast still owns market creation, collateral, migration, resolution, and redemption through each market's question ledger.

From collateral to a directional position

Each collateral deposit first creates a complete set: one Yes, one No, and one Invalid share. A market's trading pool, a Yes/No pair, is an automated market maker (AMM) that holds only Yes and No reserves. The router sends the unwanted directional share into the pair, receives more of the selected directional share, and returns the directional position and the Invalid share to the user. The Invalid share never enters the AMM; it remains a separate claim if the market later resolves Invalid.

What the prices mean

Yes and No prices are conditional on valid resolution. They describe the relative price of the two directional outcomes; they are not estimates of invalidity. Interfaces should therefore label the Yes and No values directly and present Invalid separately.

Spot prices come from current reserves and are not an oracle. Settlement uses the question ledger's resolved outcome, while solvency-sensitive REP/collateral operations use OpenOracle.

One pair per question ledger

Each question ledger maps to its own pair. Parent and child question ledgers therefore have separate pairs, reserves, prices, and liquidity-provider tokens; liquidity, like everything else in a fork, is not copied between them.
