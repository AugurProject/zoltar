Open-interest fees

Each question in a security pool is charged an open-interest fee against its outstanding settlement collateral; the security pool pays it to the vaults that underwrite that collateral. Every security pool uses funded linear fee streams so owners receive fees when they are earned.

Why the fee exists

Vault owners provide REP-backed security, keep their vaults healthy, and may need to take part in reporting and migration. In return, each fee-eligible underwriting commitment earns a share of the fee in proportion to its size. Owners claim aggregate earnings from the security pool without visiting each question.

Fees in a shared security pool

A security pool holds actual question collateral in a shared settlement contract. Each question has a linear fee stream, and an aggregate expiry index advances all streams without visiting them individually. Fees reduce both funded collateral and its internal accounting units. Outcome token supply remains separate, so a fee changes each remaining token's redemption value.

Fractional claim weights let small USDC and USDT markets accrue fees below one raw token unit per second. See the settlement reference for rounding and precision limits.

The creator fixes one annual rate, applied to each question's outstanding principal basis. It does not compound against shrinking collateral. Minting and redemption preserve that basis per remaining share. Fees continue after the question's calendar end until reporting starts or the fee budget above the backing floor runs out. Stale-price intervals and cash-impaired books do not charge ordinary holding fees, and later price recovery never backcharges those intervals.

The creator fixes minimumOutcomeRate, the value reserved per outcome share. Holding fees can spend only the amount above this floor. For example, 1,000 complete sets retain at least 10 collateral tokens in a fully funded book with a 0.01 floor. The settlement reference gives the units and stream-expiry rules.

This caps ordinary fee income; it does not release the remaining underwriting obligation or waive purchase fees and the prepaid resolution premium. Providers must account for a possible period without holding-fee income before redemption or finality. The floor protects against fee depletion, not an underfunded fork: recovery claims may pay less collateral plus REP, and tiny claims still face token-unit rounding.

Each purchase also prepays 52 days of fees on its added principal: the maximum ordinary three-day reporting window plus seven-week extension. An Escalation Game that no fork interrupts always ends within that window, because its only deadline is the curve's crossing of binding capital. This resolution premium stays with the question. Once reporting starts, providers earn only the portion corresponding to time the question remains unresolved, up to the prepaid amount. An expiry index stops those earnings at finality without requiring an owner or keeper to visit each question. Unused premium is burned when the question becomes final or all trader complete sets are returned, because the contract does not record which buyer paid it. The premium is nonrefundable; later providers cannot collect an already resolved question’s remaining budget.

Healthy providers earn a fraction based on their share of total commitment, including unhealthy commitment in the denominator. An eligibility change first credits income under the previous weights. Outstanding retirement commitments earn while healthy, and separately claimable earnings do not count as backing.

Each purchase pays the greater of the flat minimum fee and the backing-based percentage. That fee and recognized REP income vest over at least 24 hours, rewarding commitments that are healthy when each portion unlocks. This minimum prevents providers from capturing a whole stream by supplying capacity for seconds. Providers who retire retain earned fees and give up future releases.

Unassigned collateral and REP are burned. The protocol does not pay keepers or price sponsors from underwriting fees. A failed collateral transfer remains a funded claim that can be retried when transfers resume.

The resolution premium goes to underwriting providers. Wallet reporters instead fund their own gas and rely on the Escalation Game’s REP incentives.

A fork preserves existing purchase-fee and REP stream dates. Remaining resolution premiums follow branch allocations and can fund child reporting once the branch activates. See the underwriting reference for custody and burn rules.
