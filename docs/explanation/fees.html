Open-interest fees

Each SecurityPool charges an open-interest fee against its outstanding settlement collateral and pays it to the vaults that underwrite that collateral. Standalone pools express the fee as retention, the per-second fraction of collateral that stays with complete-set holders. Portfolio-bound pools use funded linear fee streams so shared owners receive fees when they are earned.

Why the fee exists

Vault owners provide REP-backed security, keep their vaults healthy, and may need to take part in reporting and migration. In return, each fee-eligible underwriting commitment earns a share of the fee in proportion to its size. In standalone pools, collateral gradually decays into an unallocated fee reserve, and vault owners claim their shares from that reserve.

How the standalone rate moves

The retention rate depends on utilization: settlement collateral divided by minting capacity, which equals the pool's total underwriting commitments, independent of price and backing. The per-second retention rate declines linearly as utilization rises and stops declining at the dip point, 80% utilization. Compounding the two per-second constants over a 365-day year, the annualized fee rises from about 9.5% at zero utilization to about 50.2% at the dip point and then holds. Security therefore becomes more expensive as open interest consumes underwriting slack.

Minting capacity is zero when the pool has no commitments or once an Escalation Game has opened. The retention rate then returns to its maximum, MAX_RETENTION_RATE, which is the lowest fee, instead of treating the pool as fully utilized. The SecurityPool reference lists the constants, the dip point, and the formula clients use to annualize the rate.

Standalone accrual and cutoff

A pool with no fee-eligible commitments accrues no fee. Fees charge only the fraction of settlement collateral assigned to fee-eligible commitments. Retained underbacked commitments keep earning fees, and previously earned fees remain claimable. Ordinary redemption reduces settlement collateral without retiring underwriting commitments.

When a child pool's Truth Auction finalizes with no purchases, its unassigned commitment stays visible but has no fee recipient until a vault takes over the entire residual commitment and backing and fully backs the result. Fee eligibility starts at takeover, without retroactive rewards. Migrated commitments and sold auction commitments enter the fee denominator at finalization, even when a winning bidder claims later.

Accrual stops at a cutoff. An origin pool uses question end. If its question remains unresolved when the universe forks, fork time replaces that cutoff in either direction: a fork before question end stops accrual early, and a fork during an unresolved Escalation Game moves the cutoff later, so the next accrual also charges the interval from question end to the fork. A later unrelated fork does not reopen fees for an already finalized question. A child starts a new epoch when migration and any Truth Auction finalize, then accrues until local resolution or another fork; a child with a fixed outcome and no continuation game accrues no further fees.

Accrual is lazy: state-changing pool operations checkpoint a global fee index, and vault operations checkpoint each vault against it, so no call iterates over every vault. Fractional amounts wait in explicit remainders until they reach whole attoETH. See the subsystem guardrails for checkpoint and fork-reconciliation sequencing and the SecurityPool contract reference for exact state transitions.

Fees in a shared portfolio

A portfolio holds actual question collateral in a shared settlement contract. Each question has a linear fee stream, and an aggregate expiry index advances all streams without visiting them individually. Fees reduce both funded collateral and its internal accounting units. Outcome token supply remains separate, so a fee changes each remaining token's redemption value.

The creator fixes one annual rate, applied to each question's outstanding principal basis. It does not compound against shrinking collateral. Minting and redemption preserve that basis per remaining share. Fees continue after the question's calendar end until reporting starts or the funded stream runs out. Stale-price intervals and recovery books do not charge ordinary holding fees, and later price recovery never backcharges those intervals.

Each purchase also prepays 52 days of fees on its added principal: the maximum ordinary three-day reporting window plus seven-week extension. This resolution premium stays with the question. Once reporting starts, providers earn only the portion corresponding to time the question remains unresolved, up to the prepaid amount. An expiry index stops those earnings at finality without requiring an owner or keeper to visit each question. Unused premium funds maintenance when the question becomes final or all trader complete sets are returned. The premium is nonrefundable; later providers cannot collect an already resolved question’s remaining budget.

Before collateral, price or commitment changes, the capital ledger credits earlier income under the old eligibility weights. An owner's entire deposited principal determines health; separately claimable earnings do not count as backing. Each healthy commitment earns its fraction of total outstanding commitment, including unhealthy commitment in the denominator. A tiny healthy position therefore cannot collect every unhealthy owner's income. Deposits without commitment earn no underwriting income, and retirement positions keep earning while their healthy commitment remains outstanding.

Every purchase pays the same configurable backing-based fee, including when capacity is reused, plus a fixed reporting charge. The creator assigns a fraction of the backing fee to oracle maintenance; the rest vests over the configured reward duration, as does recognized REP income. These vesting rewards compensate the healthy commitments serving when each portion unlocks, even if the original question has already resolved. A provider who retires gives up future unearned portions.

Income released without eligible backing is not saved for a future provider. Unassigned ETH funds the universe's restricted oracle-maintenance reserve, and unassigned REP is burned. A useful accepted oracle report can earn a capped gas reimbursement, at most once per service interval, credited for separate withdrawal. The reserve is finite: users may still need to sponsor reports when it cannot cover their costs. Owners collect their own aggregate ETH and REP earnings without visiting questions.

The reporting charge funds a question-specific budget. The reporter whose deposit establishes the final unique leader can receive an ETH bonus; adding to an unchanged leader does not take it. A fresh shared price is required when the leader changes. The bonus is capped below the REP cost of manufacturing and reversing a dispute, and later leader changes can only reduce that cap. A stale-price report can still resolve the question but earns no ETH bonus. Unused reward ETH funds maintenance, and recovered reward REP is burned. Fork children require new local reporting work for a bonus. The budget and economic cap may be too small to cover a reporter’s gas costs; they do not promise profitable reporting in every market. See the service reference for the payout rule.

A fork preserves the dates of existing purchase-fee and REP-income streams. If the parent price was fresh at the fork, eligible frozen commitments continue earning them. Parent income that cannot be assigned is burned; principal, earned claims and execution reserves are excluded. Question premiums and maintenance funds follow the chosen commitment’s branch allocation. A question’s remaining funded premium can support its child reporting period; repeated forks do not create additional funding. The portfolio reference specifies these custody boundaries.
