Open-interest fees

Each SecurityPool charges an open-interest fee on its outstanding settlement collateral and pays it to the vaults that underwrite that collateral. The contracts express the fee as retention: the per-second fraction of collateral that stays with complete-set holders.

Why the fee exists

Vault owners provide REP-backed security, keep their vaults healthy, and may need to take part in reporting and migration. In return, each fee-eligible underwriting commitment earns a share of the fee in proportion to its size. Collateral gradually decays into an unallocated fee reserve, and vault owners claim their shares from that reserve.

How the rate moves

The retention rate depends on utilization: settlement collateral divided by minting capacity, which equals the pool's total underwriting commitments, independent of price and backing. The per-second retention rate declines linearly as utilization rises and stops declining at the dip point, 80% utilization. Compounding the two per-second constants over a 365-day year, the annualized fee rises from about 9.5% at zero utilization to about 50.2% at the dip point and then holds. Security therefore becomes more expensive as open interest consumes underwriting slack.

Minting capacity is zero when the pool has no commitments or once an Escalation Game has opened. The retention rate then returns to its maximum, MAX_RETENTION_RATE, which is the lowest fee, instead of treating the pool as fully utilized. The SecurityPool reference lists the constants, the dip point, and the formula clients use to annualize the rate.

Who earns and when accrual stops

A pool with no fee-eligible commitments accrues no fee. Fees charge only the fraction of settlement collateral assigned to fee-eligible commitments. Retained underbacked commitments keep earning fees, and previously earned fees remain claimable. Ordinary redemption reduces settlement collateral without retiring underwriting commitments.

When a child pool's Truth Auction finalizes with no purchases, its unassigned commitment stays visible but has no fee recipient until a vault takes over the entire residual commitment and backing and fully backs the result. Fee eligibility starts at takeover, without retroactive rewards. Migrated commitments and sold auction commitments enter the fee denominator at finalization, even when a winning bidder claims later.

Accrual stops at a cutoff. An origin pool uses question end. If its question remains unresolved when the universe forks, fork time replaces that cutoff in either direction: a fork before question end stops accrual early, and a fork during an unresolved Escalation Game moves the cutoff later, so the next accrual also charges the interval from question end to the fork. A later unrelated fork does not reopen fees for an already finalized question. A child starts a new epoch when migration and any Truth Auction finalize, then accrues until local resolution or another fork; a child with a fixed outcome and no continuation game accrues no further fees.

Accrual is lazy: state-changing pool operations checkpoint a global fee index, and vault operations checkpoint each vault against it, so no call iterates over every vault. Fractional amounts wait in explicit remainders until they reach whole attoETH. See the subsystem guardrails for checkpoint and fork-reconciliation sequencing and the SecurityPool contract reference for exact state transitions.
