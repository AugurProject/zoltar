Collateral Repair Auctions

When a universe forks, each Statoblast portfolio prepares branches, one per child universe (forks and migration). Each owner chooses one branch for its REP and its commitment-weighted share of the portfolio’s settlement collateral, so a child receives only the collateral of the vaults that chose it. For a portfolio using USDC, suppose the parent held 100 USDC of settlement collateral when it forked and a child receives only 70 USDC through migration. The child inherits the parent's full share supply, so its Collateral Repair Auction tries to raise the missing 30 USDC by selling child REP.

  • Full repair: bidders contribute 30 USDC, so the child reaches its 100 USDC target.
  • Partial repair: bidders contribute 12 USDC, so the child operates with 82 USDC and remains impaired.
  • No demand: no qualifying bid contributes USDC, so the child remains at 70 USDC.

The auction repairs collateral and never selects truth. It sells REP belonging to an already-defined child universe. The child resumes with only the collateral that migration and accepted bids actually provide.

Shared portfolio auctions

A portfolio shares backing across several questions. It holds one auction for the branch’s combined collateral shortfall. Only freshly unchosen provider REP can be sold; chosen providers’ backing and REP already owed to older outcome claims are excluded. A fixed one-question portfolio uses this same auction. Reporter escrow is never auction inventory.

Lots cover the entire collateral deficit: a full fill must not leave a remainder merely because the deficit is not divisible by the minimum commitment. The auction starts with one lot per whole minimum commitment in the deficit and rounds each equal lot up to the next raw collateral unit, so the lot count depends on the deficit's size, not on its divisibility. For example, a 35 USDC deficit with a 10 USDC minimum becomes three 11.666667 USDC lots. Rounding up means a full fill can raise up to one raw unit per lot more than the deficit, 0.000001 USDC in this example. Only the deficit reaches the branch, and that surplus has no owner, so it is burned. At opening it chooses fewer, larger equal lots until rounding the required REP up to its grid adds less than 1%. If indivisible REP prevents that, one lot covering the entire deficit remains allowed even above 1%; this exception keeps repair possible. The target is evaluated at the opening quote, and a changed closing price can change the relative rounding. Lot sizing preserves the collateral target throughout, but weak demand or insufficient REP can still cause a partial fill.

A bidder supplies the configured collateral for whole auction lots. No REP deposit is required. Before submitting a bid, the bidder approves the auction to transfer the collateral token. Each bid specifies the least REP acceptable per lot (the bid’s limit) and a personal health target. That minimum REP must itself fully back the lot’s collateral commitment at the opening conservative price and chosen target. Winning bidders automatically become vault owners, with commitment equal to their accepted bid collateral. Each bid has a separate vault account controlled by its bidder, a small clone of one shared implementation whose owner, ledger, auction and bid are fixed at creation.

Lower REP-per-lot bids offer more collateral for the same REP, so they rank first. At closing, every winning lot must also receive at least the REP that backs it at the closing conservative price, rounded up to the auction’s REP price grid, the step between valid REP-per-lot prices. The auction accepts the largest number of lots that fit both its collateral target and the available REP when each lot receives the larger of the marginal winner’s minimum and that closing requirement.

Any unsold REP remains with inherited claims. If no feasible lot exists, no REP is sold.

Opening must leave at least one day before the fixed closing deadline. Closing needs a funded observation from the final day; an older opening quote cannot set the winners’ backing.

Equal bids fill in submission order: an earlier bid at the same limit fills completely before a later one receives any lot, so only the earliest unfilled bid at the marginal limit can fill partially. Competitive bids gather at the lowest limit that still backs a lot at the bidder’s target, and no bid can go below it, so this ordering stops a large bid placed just before closing from crowding out bids already waiting at that limit. The marginal bid limit still determines which bids win when the final REP per lot is higher. The contract reference defines the exact grid and rounding rules.

A new bid is rejected if funded bids that rank before it, at a lower limit or at the same limit submitted earlier, already make winning impossible. A bid that could win when submitted can recover its full collateral before closing once later bids permanently rule it out. Anyone can credit that refund; the bidder then withdraws it and decides how to use the collateral, or credits and withdraws in one transaction. That combined withdrawal pays the bidder’s whole refund credit, including refunds already credited for its other bids, and skips a credit someone else already made. A provisional zero fill is not enough: clearing at the closing price can still reach it. Bids that could still win remain binding until closing, and bids cannot be repriced or cancelled.

At closing, winning backing and commitments enter capital accounting immediately. A shared position preserves each bid’s funded obligations and fee rights until an owner or keeper splits it out. Delaying that transaction cannot omit a winner from aggregate backing or a later fork snapshot. Refunds are independent of that step. Keepers discover unmaterialized winners directly through the auction tree, skipping losing bids and positions already split out. The controlling wallet must still choose a branch before the next fork-choice deadline.

Choices close at week eight and bidding at week nine, even if preparation starts late. Funded prices must cover the maximum auction exposure and fresh inventory, and opening requires such a price to be fresh and at least one full day of bidding to remain. Closing uses the last accepted price only if its settlement-eligibility timestamp lies within the final day before the fixed deadline. That price need not still be fresh at the exact closing second, allowing time for short settlement delays. A week-old opening observation cannot settle the sale, and a late closer cannot select a later price. A higher closing price does not cancel the sale either, since one marginally higher report would void every funded bid. It raises the REP each winning lot must receive, so fewer lots fill when the inventory cannot provide it, and a bidder’s chosen health target is guaranteed only at the opening price. The sale is cancelled if the universe forked at or before the deadline, the accepted quote lacks a final-day settlement-eligibility timestamp, or the accepted price no longer covers those exposure budgets. Accepted collateral otherwise repairs inherited claims. A remaining cash deficit keeps the branch in runoff: existing claims can settle, but new purchases and ordinary holding fees stay closed. Anyone can restore a cash-impaired branch whose providers have enough total underwriting commitment by donating its entire remaining active cash deficit; the donor sets a maximum and pays the deficit current when the transaction executes. A donation cannot impose demand above the providers’ total commitment or enlarge the branch’s oracle exposure. The donation preserves all inherited recovery REP, including already frozen claims, and gives the sponsor no shares, commitment or REP. Holding fees can resume once cash backing is restored, under the ordinary price requirements. A later buyer into an inherited question must also supply that question’s proportional recovery REP, within an explicit budget, so the purchase does not dilute existing holders. A fresh question needs no recovery REP from its buyer.

With weak demand, the auction sells only the REP priced by its funded lots and retains the rest for claim recovery. Revenue still depends on competitive entry; a partial sale does not guarantee full repair. Winners take ordinary vault obligations and liquidation risk, without a minimum holding period. Surplus withdrawals and retirement against spare healthy capacity can release REP, so a sale does not guarantee permanent security. Materializing a winning bid pays no protocol keeper reward. Later liquidation takeovers can transfer a time-increasing REP bonus into a receiver’s vault under the separate liquidation auction.
