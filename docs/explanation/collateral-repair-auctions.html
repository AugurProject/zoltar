Collateral Repair Auctions

When a universe forks, each Statoblast security pool prepares branches, one per child universe (forks and migration). Each owner chooses one branch for its REP and its commitment-weighted share of the security pool’s settlement collateral, so a child receives only the collateral of the vaults that chose it. For a security pool using USDC, suppose the parent held 100 USDC of settlement collateral when it forked and a child receives only 70 USDC through migration. The child inherits the parent's full share supply, so its Collateral Repair Auction tries to raise the missing 30 USDC by selling child REP.

  • Full repair: bidders contribute 30 USDC, so the child reaches its 100 USDC target.
  • Partial repair: bidders contribute 12 USDC, so the child operates with 82 USDC and remains impaired.
  • No demand: no qualifying bid contributes USDC, so the child remains at 70 USDC.

The auction repairs collateral and never selects truth. It sells REP belonging to an already-defined child universe. The child resumes with only the collateral that migration and accepted bids actually provide.

Shared security pool auctions

A security pool shares backing across several questions. It holds one auction for the branch’s combined collateral shortfall. Only freshly unchosen provider REP can be sold; chosen providers’ backing and REP already owed to older outcome claims are excluded. A fixed one-question security pool uses this same auction. Reporter escrow is never auction inventory.

The auction rounds equal lots up so a full fill can cover the entire deficit. It prefers larger lots when smaller ones would add too much REP through rounding. The contract reference gives the lot-size and rounding limits.

A bidder commits collateral for whole lots and sets a minimum REP per lot. The minimum must back the lot at the opening conservative price and the protocol multiplier. Winning lots join the bidder’s own vault, with commitment equal to the accepted collateral. A bid chooses no health target; the winner may raise its vault’s health target afterwards, and until then the vault’s existing target, or 100%, applies.

Lower REP-per-lot bids offer more collateral for the same REP, so they rank first. At closing, every winning lot must also receive at least the REP that backs it at the closing conservative price, rounded up to the auction’s REP price grid, the step between valid REP-per-lot prices. The auction accepts the largest number of lots that fit both its collateral target and the available REP when each lot receives the larger of the marginal winner’s minimum and that closing requirement.

Any unsold REP remains with inherited claims. If no feasible lot exists, no REP is sold.

At the same limit, earlier bids fill first. This prevents a late bid at the minimum viable price from crowding out earlier bids.

A bid must be competitive when submitted. If later bids permanently rule it out, the bidder can recover its entire collateral before closing. Bids that could still win remain binding. See the auction reference for refunds.

Choices close at week eight and bidding at week nine, even if preparation starts late. Opening requires a fresh funded price and at least one full day to bid. Closing uses the accepted price only if its settlement-eligibility timestamp is on the final day and it still covers the auction’s exposure. A higher closing price raises the required REP per lot, which can reduce the number of fills. The auction reference lists cancellation conditions.

Winning backing and commitments count immediately at closing, while the lots still sit in one shared auction position. Each bid then has to be claimed. Claiming moves exactly that bid’s lots into the bidder’s vault; a bid that won nothing is only marked claimed. Anyone may claim any bid, and one call can claim many, bounded by transaction gas: the app claims up to 16 bids per transaction and asks for further claims for the rest, and a keeper can take over a distressed winning bid without waiting for the bidder. A bidder that already has a live position receives the lots in that same position. To keep auction lots apart from existing capital, bid from a separate address.

Until it claims, the bidder’s vault shows only its own position; the unclaimed lots and their earnings are reported per bid. The protocol counts each bidder’s unclaimed bids, including losing ones, and the bidder cannot choose a branch in a later fork of this universe while that count is above zero. Requiring the claim, rather than moving lots during every vault operation, keeps the cost of each operation independent of how many bids the bidder placed, so there is no limit on bids per address.

A cash deficit puts the branch into runoff. Existing claims can settle while new purchases and ordinary holding fees remain closed. A sponsor can fund the remaining cash deficit if there is enough underwriting commitment. New buyers in an inherited question with recovery REP contribute their proportional REP so existing holders keep their entitlement per share.

Winners take on ordinary vault obligations and liquidation risk. Lots that join an existing position share its health, and the combined position keeps the older liquidation clock of the two parts, so already-distressed lots can bring an existing position closer to liquidation. Later withdrawals and retirement can release backing, so a sale does not guarantee permanent security.
