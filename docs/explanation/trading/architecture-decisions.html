Explanation

Trading architecture decisions and limitations

Trading uses a small, immutable pair. Each design choice keeps market identity, collateral accounting, or migration explicit.

Decisions

  1. Two reserves only. A pair holding Yes × No shares avoids the three-way quadratic and does not price Invalid.
  2. Exact question ledger identity. A pair is keyed by the question ledger address, so parent and child branch question ledgers always map to different pairs even when they share one ShareToken. The factory's canonical validation stops a question ledger from borrowing another market's identity.
  3. One immutable fee per factory. There is no owner, protocol fee switch, or mutable parameter.
  4. Balance-delta integration. The router measures complete-set creation and redemption by observed share and ETH balance deltas because holding fees change the ETH-to-share rate; it never assumes a fixed ratio. Its custody checks are listed in the router reference.
  5. Minimum-reserve LP scale. Initial LP supply follows the smaller reserve rather than a geometric mean, which would overflow at large share scales; only proportional ownership matters. The liquidity-provider position gives the rule.
  6. Donations accrue to LPs. Reserve-dependent operations read actual ShareToken balances, so unsolicited Yes or No shares benefit existing LPs instead of the next trader. The security model covers the related custody defenses.
  7. No automatic fork behavior. Parent liquidity is removed as parent shares. The user explicitly chooses migration targets and initializes distinct child pairs.

Limitations and non-goals

Trading is unaudited. The Trading security model lists its defenses and assumptions.
