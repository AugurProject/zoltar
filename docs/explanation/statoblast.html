Prediction markets

How Statoblast works

Follow one question: “Will the fictional Aurora spacecraft launch before 31 December 2030?” Statoblast creates a collateralized market with Yes, No, and Invalid shares, resolves ordinary disagreement locally, and uses Zoltar branches only when local resolution cannot produce a decision or another fork interrupts the pool.

1. Mental model

#

Statoblast is the market layer. Each SecurityPool ledger serves one question in one universe. Separate portfolios keep separate ledgers even when their question and configuration match. A standalone ledger holds ETH settlement collateral and tracks vault owners' underwriting commitments. Portfolio-bound ledgers use shared capital and settlement accounting. Both mint, migrate and settle question shares. Zoltar owns the questions, universes, REP, and forks underneath it; Trading and OpenOracle sit beside it. The system overview introduces all four.

2. Normal market lifecycle

#
  1. Register the example question in Zoltar and deploy its SecurityPool in one universe.
  2. Vault owners deposit REP; traders deposit ETH to mint complete sets.
  3. Traders may exchange shares through an exchange such as Trading.
  4. After the question ends, the first REP deposit on an outcome opens the Escalation Game.
  5. An unchallenged report wins three days later; a contested game runs until one outcome is the strict leader at its deadline. Holders then redeem winning shares from the pool's settlement collateral.

4. Local resolution

#

The game opens with the first REP deposit after the question ends. REP-backed balances then accumulate behind Invalid, Yes, and No. Their median is binding capital and determines the moving deadline. After that deadline, exactly one strict balance leader produces the local winner. Winning shares redeem from that pool.

If two outcomes reach the non-decision threshold, the game does not choose between them. Anyone can then fork the universe on the market question through Statoblast, which pays the fork threshold from the REP held by the pool and the game. See the Escalation Game explanation for the required-support curve, deadlines, payouts, and how games continue across forks.

5. Standalone pool forks and migration

#

An unresolved operational standalone pool can enter the fork path in two ways. Its own Escalation Game can reach non-decision and fork Zoltar on the market question; in this example, that creates Invalid, Yes, and No children. A fork of the pool's universe on another question can also interrupt it, in which case the children represent that categorical or scalar question's valid answers. A finalized pool remains settled and rejects later fork migration. Multi-question portfolios use the shared fork process and fixed deadlines described below.

Migration is open for a migration window of eight weeks, counted from the call that puts the parent pool into fork mode. A vault's parent backing claim, underwriting commitment, and proportional pool collateral are routed into one selected child pool. The same parent vault migration cannot be reused across siblings. Eligible outcome-share balances work differently: users can materialize branch-specific ledger claims in multiple selected child pools. That reproduces claims, not ETH; see what Zoltar does not guarantee.

If a standalone child receives too little ETH, it can sell child REP through a one-week Truth Auction, which anyone can start once the migration window has closed. Full demand can repair the shortfall; weak demand can leave redemption below face value.

6. Fees, underwriting, and liquidations

#

REP deposits do not create underwriting commitments; each vault owner sets one separately. A vault's share of total commitments sets its share of the pool's settlement collateral obligations and of the open-interest fees that outstanding collateral pays.

New complete sets can be minted only before the question ends, while total commitments cover the resulting collateral and no Escalation Game has opened. Standalone minting needs no oracle price or backing check, because vault owners already answer for their full commitments (invariant BAL-03).

A vault whose REP no longer backs its commitment can be liquidated: some or all of its commitment and backing move to an authorized receiver vault. See vault liquidation.

7. Managing several questions

#

A SecurityPoolPortfolio lets traders buy outcomes for many questions against one aggregate backing pool. REP stays in a central capital ledger. Each owner retains an exact claim to their own principal and chooses how much standing commitment to authorize. There is no common REP share price and no purchase from individual vaults. Questions retain separate outcome tokens and reporting games.

All REP deposited for an owner in a portfolio universe backs that owner's single commitment, including any surplus above its required backing. Depositing more REP immediately strengthens that backing; there is no separate pledge or top-up step. Each owner chooses a health target for authorizing commitment. A larger buffer supports less commitment with the same REP but gives more protection against price changes. Admission and surplus withdrawals use the conservative edge of the oracle’s configured error band. Paid liquidation uses the opposite edge and compares the entire deposit with required backing, so a small price error alone cannot create an execution reward. Falling below the owner's chosen buffer alone does not trigger it. New purchases require enough healthy commitment at a fresh shared REP/ETH price. New commitments must fit a finalized report’s funded exposure. Independent report candidates prevent one pending report from blocking another sponsor. An owner can authorize growth using an independently finalized, sufficiently funded report; its conservative collateral requirement stays attached to that owner until a new shared report covers the enlarged pool.

The creator fixes an admission-policy contract, the annual time-fee rate, the question horizon and economic minimums. The interface offers either open admission or a permanently fixed question list. Other policies work onchain. Registration moves no REP and creates no claim obligation for owners. Every purchase checks admission, pays the configurable backing-based fee, question reporting charge and prepaid resolution premium, and delivers its three outcomes atomically. Unused payment budget returns to the buyer.

Income is separate from principal. Continuously earned question fees belong to the healthy commitments that served during that interval. Purchase fees and REP income vest over a configured duration. A new deposit cannot collect an earlier owner's earned fees, and borrowing REP for one transaction cannot capture a whole prepaid reward. Owners collect aggregate earnings without visiting questions. See portfolio fees for the earning rules.

Reporter REP has its own custody and cannot be taken from a vault's backing. Irrevocably released dispute surplus enters the separate REP income stream. It does not raise an entry or exit exchange rate. Final reporter claims and their required burns remain separately funded.

The chosen health target protects voluntary changes: authorizing more commitment, receiving a takeover, withdrawing backing or cancelling retirement requires enough REP at that target and a fresh conservative price. Raising the target on a live position also requires sufficient backing; an owner can deposit REP and raise it in one transaction. Lowering the target never increases commitment automatically. A price move that leaves the position below its health target does not itself cause liquidation, and deposits and earned-fee claims remain available.

An owner can withdraw surplus REP while keeping the remaining backing sufficient to meet the chosen target. This does not require other owners to be healthy. Principal with no remaining commitment is freely withdrawable. To reduce commitment, the owner requests retirement to an explicit remaining amount, including zero for a full exit. Returning complete sets reduces aggregate demand, letting queued commitments retire before new purchases consume the spare capacity.

Each owner has at most one live position, either active or in a retirement cohort. Partial retirement keeps the whole deposit behind the smaller commitment and stops at the requested amount, then returns that position to active status. The request fixes a commitment amount; it does not automatically adjust to later prices. Full retirement releases the entire deposit. The pool serves a closed current cohort before the next cohort. An owner must cancel retirement before authorizing more commitment; a later retirement request joins the next cohort.

Full exits settle in groups, while each service call updates at most one partial-retirement boundary. While a partial request waits, its intended remaining commitment stays available to purchases if the whole position is healthy; only the queued reduction is reserved for exit. An owner can complete their own request without processing earlier owners when spare capacity also covers those earlier requests. Otherwise retirement waits for more capacity or progresses through the queue. Purchases do not have to clear the whole queue, and withdrawal does not add a question-by-question recovery task.

Liquidation affects the undercollateralized vault. A cure removes available unused commitment toward restoring health, subject to the minimum useful action size, while retaining its REP. If spare capacity is insufficient, the position remains eligible for takeover by a funded buyer. The buyer's REP reward comes only from the target position. The takeover clock begins automatically at the first accepted adverse price after the position's latest recovery, so no keeper must pay to start it. The target funds its own execution reserve and can add more ETH directly to that live budget. Actual progress earns the larger of the configured payment and a bounded gas-cost payment, subject to available funding and the remaining commitment's reserve. Other owners' principal is unchanged. Finite rewards cannot guarantee execution during every gas-price or REP-price shock.

Health and fee accounting use indexed aggregates. Work follows tree paths and the owner's single position, rather than all questions, owners or expired streams. The healthy-commitment index is balanced. Purchase-fee and REP-income rewards use a fixed vesting duration, so searches exclude already released history and are bounded by that duration. Question resolution premiums use an expiry index that releases only earned fees and routes unused terminal funding to maintenance, without scanning questions. Finality checkpoints read the newly matured prefix and append one immutable record; they do not copy historical tree paths. Storage still grows with activity. Creating a question, using it for the first time in a branch and processing one's tokens still require transactions, but they do not impose a migration batch on every backing owner.

A native universe fork fixes an eight-week choice window. Each owner may make one irreversible branch choice for their commitment and its entire deposit in that universe, including the surplus buffer. Principal with no remaining commitment and old earnings stay separately claimable in the parent. After choices close, one shared migration prepares each requested branch. Each branch inherits the outstanding outcome claims, including tokens not yet materialized there. Parent ETH follows the fraction of total ETH commitment that chose the branch, so choosing excess REP cannot capture extra cash.

Chosen providers retain their own REP, commitment, health target and execution reserve. REP in branches they did not choose backs those branches’ inherited outcome claims. A shared truth auction can sell freshly unchosen REP to repair the branch’s ETH shortfall. Bidders, including former owners, supply ETH and a separate ETH keeper reserve; the purchased REP backs their accepted ETH commitment. A positive fill below the auction’s ETH target sells all fresh inventory. With no feasible lot, none is sold; a sale reaching the target can leave unsold REP with the claims. Old recovery REP is excluded from the sale. At week nine, a prepared branch can activate even if no sale succeeds. Returning complete sets or redeeming winning shares then pays the claim’s proportional ETH and remaining recovery REP together. Full repayment in ETH is not guaranteed. A branch with recovery REP or a cash shortfall cannot mint new complete sets or charge ordinary holding fees; later buyers cannot capture its inherited recovery assets.

Missing the choice cutoff does not select a default branch: the owner’s committed REP backs inherited claims in every child, while unused execution reserve becomes withdrawable in the parent. For questions still live at the fork, holders recover unassigned parent ETH by combining separate Invalid, Yes and No recovery rights. These rights transfer independently of child-token migration rights. Questions already final retain their ETH and REP claims in the source pool. A later fork migrates only the remaining live recovery REP, once per branch. Child forks cannot begin before week ten; late preparation does not restart these deadlines. Questions and reporter claims are prepared lazily when used, and traders still migrate shares through each intervening universe. The portfolio fork reference specifies the interfaces.

8. Security assumptions and non-guarantees

#
  • REP-backed incentives do not prove objective truth; users and applications decide which branches retain activity.
  • OpenOracle depends on timely, profitable correction of inaccurate REP/ETH reports.
  • A fork gives each child only the ETH that migrates to it (Zoltar's non-guarantees).
  • ETH pushed into a pool outside its accounting is not counted as collateral. Steps that anyone may trigger, such as forking or starting an auction, still need someone to send the transaction.
  • Spot prices from trading pools are manipulable and are never protocol oracles.

Normative assumptions and guarantees are listed in the security model and invariant catalog.

Design rationale

Statoblast's ancestry includes the Arctic Tern Oracle, Sisyphean Exchange, Partial Colored Coins, and Isonzo. Those research ideas influenced the design; the deployed contracts govern current behavior.
