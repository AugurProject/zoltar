Prediction markets

How Statoblast works

Follow one question: “Will the fictional Aurora spacecraft launch before 31 December 2030?” Statoblast creates a collateralized market with Yes, No, and Invalid shares, resolves ordinary disagreement locally, and uses Zoltar branches only when local resolution cannot produce a decision or another fork interrupts the pool.

1. Mental model

#

Statoblast is the market layer. Each SecurityPool ledger serves one question in one universe. Separate portfolios keep separate ledgers even when their question and configuration match. Every ledger belongs to a portfolio and uses its shared capital and settlement accounting. A fixed portfolio containing one question uses the same contracts and rules as a portfolio containing many questions. Zoltar owns the questions, universes, REP, and forks underneath it; Trading and OpenOracle sit beside it. The system overview introduces all four.

2. Normal market lifecycle

#
  1. Register the example question in Zoltar and add it to a portfolio whose admission policy permits it.
  2. Vault owners deposit REP; traders deposit the portfolio’s immutable ERC-20 collateral token to mint complete sets. Collateral follows the asset requirements; the creation UI offers WETH and USDC.
  3. Traders may exchange shares through an exchange such as Trading.
  4. After the question ends, the first REP deposit on an outcome opens the Escalation Game.
  5. An unchallenged report wins three days later; a contested game runs until one outcome is the strict leader at its deadline. Holders then redeem winning shares from the pool's settlement collateral.

4. Local resolution

#

The game opens with the first REP deposit after the question ends. REP-backed balances then accumulate behind Invalid, Yes, and No. Their median is binding capital. Once the scheduled deadline has passed, exactly one strict balance leader produces the local winner. Winning shares redeem from that pool.

If two outcomes reach the non-decision threshold, the game does not choose between them. Anyone can then fork the universe on the market question through Statoblast, which pays the fork threshold from the triggering question’s REP escrow in the portfolio reporter ledger. The game fixes its non-decision threshold when it opens, while the genesis fork threshold follows the live external REP supply until the first fork. If that supply grew in between, the escrow falls short, and the caller pays the difference from REP approved to the reporter ledger. That REP joins the question’s escrow without a separate claim. See the Escalation Game explanation for the required-support curve, deadlines, payouts, and how games continue across forks.

5. Forks and migration

A question's non-decision or a fork on another question enters the same shared branch process. Owners choose once per portfolio and universe. Questions, outcome tokens and reporter continuations are prepared lazily when used. See the choice windows and recovery rules below.

6. Fees, underwriting, and liquidations

Each owner's REP backs their authorized collateral commitment. Purchases require sufficient healthy aggregate commitment and a fresh funded oracle price. Returning complete sets releases demand; it does not require owners to recover REP question by question. Fees accrue separately from principal. Liquidation transfers unsupported commitment and a growing REP bonus into the funded receiver’s vault.

7. Managing several questions

#

A SecurityPoolPortfolio lets traders buy outcomes for many questions against one aggregate backing pool. REP stays in a central capital ledger. Each owner has individually accounted principal and chooses how much standing commitment to authorize; liquidation can transfer pledged REP to a receiver. There is no common REP share price and no purchase from individual vaults. Questions retain separate outcome tokens and reporting games.

All REP deposited for an owner in a portfolio universe backs that owner's single commitment, including any surplus above its required backing. While the position is healthy at the current liquidation price, depositing more REP immediately strengthens that backing; there is no separate pledge or top-up step. Each owner chooses a health target for authorizing commitment. A larger buffer supports less commitment with the same REP but gives more protection against price changes. Admission and surplus withdrawals use the conservative edge of the oracle’s configured error band. Repair eligibility uses the opposite edge and compares the entire deposit with required backing. Falling below the owner's chosen buffer alone does not trigger it. See liquidation repairs for the price limits. New purchases require enough healthy commitment at a fresh shared REP/collateral price. New commitments must fit a finalized report’s funded exposure. Independent report candidates prevent one pending report from blocking another sponsor. An owner can authorize growth using an independently finalized, sufficiently funded report; its conservative collateral requirement stays attached to that owner until a new shared report covers the enlarged pool.

The creator fixes an admission-policy contract, the annual time-fee rate, the question horizon and economic minimums. The interface offers either open admission or a permanently fixed question list. Other policies work onchain. Registration moves no REP and creates no claim obligation for owners. Every purchase checks admission, pays the configurable backing-based fee and prepaid resolution premium, and delivers its three outcomes atomically. Unused payment budget remains in the buyer’s wallet.

A portfolio admits new questions only while its origin universe remains unforked. Once that universe forks, admission closes permanently for that portfolio, including its continuations in child universes. Children inherit the questions registered before the fork; continuing an existing question does not admit a new one. To introduce a new question in an unforked child universe, create a new portfolio with that child as its origin universe. An open admission policy does not override this restriction.

Income is separate from principal. Continuously earned question fees belong to the healthy commitments that served during that interval. Purchase fees and REP income vest over a configured duration. A new deposit cannot collect an earlier owner's earned fees, and borrowing REP for one transaction cannot capture a whole prepaid reward. Fee share follows commitment, not use; see purchase fee vesting. Owners collect aggregate earnings without visiting questions. See portfolio fees for the earning rules.

Reporter REP has its own custody and cannot be taken from a vault's backing. Irrevocably released dispute surplus enters the separate REP income stream. It does not raise an entry or exit exchange rate. Final reporter claims and their required burns remain separately funded.

The chosen health target protects voluntary changes: authorizing more commitment or withdrawing backing requires enough REP at that target and a fresh conservative price. Receiving a takeover checks the same health target using the last accepted conservative valuation; an auction already started can execute after that report expires. Raising the target on a live position also requires sufficient backing; an owner can deposit REP and raise it in one transaction. Lowering the target never increases commitment automatically. A price move that leaves the position below its health target does not itself cause liquidation, and deposits and earned-fee claims remain available.

An owner can withdraw pledged surplus REP when healthy aggregate commitment covers demand and the remaining backing meets the owner's chosen target. Previously free principal and principal released by full retirement remain withdrawable. To reduce commitment, the owner sets a lower amount, including zero for a full exit, and the reduction is queued for retirement. Returning complete sets reduces aggregate demand, letting queued commitments retire before new purchases consume the spare capacity.

Each owner has at most one live position, either active or in a retirement cohort. Partial retirement keeps the deposit behind the requested remaining commitment; full retirement requests zero. Requests have immutable FIFO priority, and later REP deposits cannot improve it. Each service call processes its specified position, reserving capacity for earlier reductions. Anyone may serve a ready request; serving only carries out the owner's own request. Pending requests cannot be changed or cancelled, but the owner may resubmit their requested remaining commitment to attempt an immediate cure at a fresh price. If an owner reduction has an uncovered deficit below the economic minimum, it queues without an immediate cure, whether the requested exit is partial or full.

Retirement waits for spare capacity after earlier requested reductions. Purchases reserve queued reductions through the available-capacity check; they do not process the retirement queue. Principal becomes withdrawable as the owner’s request is served.

An owner can add REP before liquidation starts, or reduce unused commitment under the cure rules. REP top-ups are rejected while the position remains underbacked at the current liquidation price; a later oracle recovery can make deposits available again. After an adverse observation, a one-hour auction offers a growing REP bonus, up to all pledged principal, directly to the receiver’s vault. Repairs first apply an unpaid cure at the conservative price, then calculate the transferred commitment from the source’s backing after the bonus. A smaller transfer must either restore conservative backing completely or leave the source eligible for further liquidation. The receiver supplies any remaining backing and earns future fees; old earnings remain with their owners. Pledged withdrawals wait while healthy commitment is below demand; ordinary retirement preserves exit priority. See liquidation repairs for the mechanism and its limits.

Health and fee accounting use indexed aggregates. Work follows tree paths and the owner's single position, rather than all questions, owners or expired streams. The healthy-commitment index is balanced. Purchase-fee and REP-income rewards use a fixed vesting duration, so searches exclude already released history and are bounded by that duration. Question resolution premiums use an expiry index that releases only earned fees and burns unused terminal funding, without scanning questions. Finality checkpoints read the newly matured prefix and append one immutable record; they do not copy historical tree paths. Storage still grows with activity. Creating a question, using it for the first time in a branch and processing one's tokens still require transactions, but they do not impose a migration batch on every backing owner.

A native universe fork fixes an eight-week choice window. Each owner may make one irreversible branch choice for their commitment and its entire deposit in that universe, including the surplus buffer. Principal with no remaining commitment and old earnings stay separately claimable in the parent. After choices close, one shared migration prepares each requested branch. Each branch inherits the outstanding outcome claims, including tokens not yet materialized there. Source settlement collateral follows the fraction of total collateral commitment that chose the branch, so choosing excess REP cannot capture extra cash.

Chosen providers retain their own REP, commitment and health target. REP in branches they did not choose backs those branches’ inherited outcome claims. A shared collateral repair auction can sell freshly unchosen REP to repair the branch’s collateral shortfall. Bidders, including former owners, supply only their bid collateral; the purchased REP backs their accepted collateral commitment. Each filled auction lot receives the REP priced by the marginal bid or the closing backing floor, whichever is higher. Unsold REP remains with the claims, including when demand is weak. Old recovery REP is excluded from the sale. At week nine, a prepared branch can activate even if no sale succeeds. Returning complete sets or redeeming winning shares then pays the claim’s proportional collateral and remaining recovery REP together. Full repayment in collateral is not guaranteed. A branch with a cash shortfall cannot mint new complete sets or charge ordinary holding fees. After cash backing is fully repaired, purchases can resume. If recovery REP remains, new buyers must contribute proportional recovery REP alongside cash so existing holders retain their entitlement per share.

Missing the choice cutoff does not select a default branch: the owner’s committed REP backs inherited claims in every child. For questions still live at the fork, holders recover unassigned source settlement collateral by combining separate Invalid, Yes and No recovery rights. These rights transfer independently of child-token migration rights. Questions already final retain their collateral and REP claims in the source pool; their shares redeem there and cannot migrate. A later fork migrates only the remaining live recovery REP, once per branch. Child forks cannot begin before week ten; late preparation does not restart these deadlines. Questions and reporter continuations are prepared lazily when used, and traders still migrate shares through each intervening universe. An intervening universe that forked before the question was prepared there needs no separate transaction: once its branch custody exists, the migration itself prepares the question’s ledger there. The portfolio fork reference specifies the interfaces.

8. Security assumptions and non-guarantees

#
  • REP-backed incentives do not prove objective truth; users and applications decide which branches retain activity.
  • OpenOracle depends on timely, profitable correction of inaccurate REP/collateral reports.
  • A fork gives each child only the collateral that migrates to it (Zoltar's non-guarantees).
  • Tokens or native ETH pushed into a pool outside its accounting create no collateral claim. Steps that anyone may trigger, such as forking or starting an auction, still need someone to send the transaction.
  • Spot prices from trading pools are manipulable and are never protocol oracles.

Normative assumptions and guarantees are listed in the security model and invariant catalog.

Design rationale

Statoblast's ancestry includes the Arctic Tern Oracle, Sisyphean Exchange, Partial Colored Coins, and Isonzo. Those research ideas influenced the design; the deployed contracts govern current behavior.
