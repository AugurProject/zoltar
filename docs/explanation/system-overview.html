System overview

Consider one recurring example: “Will the fictional Aurora spacecraft launch before 31 December 2030?” Its possible outcomes are Yes, No, and Invalid. The system registers that question, creates a collateralized market, lets people trade its shares, and then resolves or branches the market.

Four separate components

Zoltar
Registers global questions, defines branching universes, manages universe-specific REP, and coordinates forks. It neither resolves market truth nor manages Statoblast settlement collateral.
Statoblast core
Deploys a SecurityPool for a question in a universe, holds ETH settlement collateral, mints and burns Yes, No, and Invalid outcome shares, tracks vault REP and capacity ownership, resolves ordinary disputes locally, and handles migration and collateral repair after a fork.
Statoblast Trading
One separate on-chain trading implementation built around Statoblast shares. Other trading implementations can be built on top of Statoblast. It does not create or resolve markets.
OpenOracle
Supplies fresh, economically contestable REP/ETH prices for solvency-sensitive operations. It does not decide prediction-market outcomes.

One market lifecycle

Alice registers the example question. A creator deploys its SecurityPool in a selected universe. Vault owners deposit REP to provide security capacity, and traders deposit ETH to mint complete sets. After the question ends, reporting begins.

In the ordinary path, one outcome has a strict local reporting lead and its shares redeem from the original pool. If two outcomes reach the non-decision threshold, Statoblast forks on the market question and can migrate into its Invalid, Yes, and No children. While the pool is still unresolved and operational, a fork of its universe on another question can also interrupt it; those children follow that other categorical or scalar question's valid answers. A finalized pool remains settled and rejects later fork migration. Users choose child universes; ETH follows specific migration claims and is never copied. A child with insufficient collateral may try to raise the difference by selling child REP in a Truth Auction.
