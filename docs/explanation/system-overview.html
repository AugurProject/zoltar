System overview

Zoltar and Statoblast are Ethereum protocols for prediction markets, designed to make outcome manipulation costly.

The problem

A prediction market lets people trade on a question such as “Will the fictional Aurora spacecraft launch before 31 December 2030?” Its shares are worth something only after someone decides what actually happened. That decision is the weak point: whoever controls it controls the money.

Zoltar and Statoblast make the decision expensive to corrupt in two ways. First, anyone who reports an outcome must stake REP, the protocol’s reputation token, behind it. A report can win when it has the strongest backing at settlement. Reporters whose outcome loses forfeit their stake. Whether that pressure keeps reports honest depends on the participation assumptions.

Second, when reporters cannot agree, the universe, the ledger that holds REP balances and the markets that use them, can fork into one child per possible answer. Even a dishonest REP majority cannot stop others from moving REP to a child that records the honest answer. See the limits of a fork.

How markets are backed

Security pool
A shared capital and settlement system that backs admitted questions. Each owner keeps an individual REP account and commitment; each question keeps separate outcome shares and accounting. A security pool can hold one fixed question or many.
Question ledger
The question contract inside a security pool. It exposes outcome shares, reporting and redemption; security pool modules hold REP and collateral.

Four components

Zoltar
The question and REP registry. It records questions, universes, REP, and forks. It never judges which answer is true.
Statoblast core
The market. It deploys a question ledger for one question and universe in one security pool, mints and redeems shares, settles ordinary disputes locally, and moves markets into child universes after a fork.
Trading
An exchange for Statoblast shares. It is one of possibly many; it neither creates nor resolves markets.
OpenOracle
A price feed. It supplies a contestable REP price in the security pool’s collateral token so the protocol can check whether REP still covers its obligations. It has no say in market outcomes.

One market, start to finish

Someone registers the Aurora question in Zoltar and admits it to a security pool under that security pool’s admission policy. Owners deposit REP and authorize shared commitments. Traders purchase complete sets through the security pool: one Yes, one No and one Invalid share at the question’s current backing rate. The collateral token goes to central settlement custody, with a separate balance for the question. Traders can exchange those shares; reporting begins after the question ends.

Reporters stake REP behind an answer in the question ledger's Escalation Game, a dispute round whose deadline moves later as the contested amount grows. When the deadline finally passes, the market resolves if exactly one answer holds more REP than every other, and winning shares redeem the question’s backing from central settlement custody. Usually that is the end of the story.

If two Aurora answers reach the non-decision threshold, the universe can split into Invalid, Yes, and No children. Each child receives only the collateral allocated to it. Forks and migration explains branch choices, claim preparation, and repair.
