EscalationGame

The Escalation Game's deposit, timing, outcome-selection, and continuation rules fit into the wider prediction-market lifecycle described in the Statoblast white paper.

When a Statoblast market ends, the security pool routes a valid deposit through its escalation-deposit delegate. The deposit must pass the pool's vault-backing and lifecycle checks and normally meet the configured starting bond; only a deposit that fills an outcome to the non-decision threshold may use a smaller amount. The initial deposit does not automatically resolve the market.

An accepted deposit extends the local deadline only when it increases the median of the three outcome balances; a deposit that leaves the median unchanged does not move the deadline. A strict leader identifies the local result, but the pool can finalize it only after the deadline has passed strictly, while competing outcomes that reach the non-decision threshold follow the fork path.

Lifecycle: tentative claim → conflicting deposit → accepted amount and new deadline → local settlement or non-decision → child continuation after a fork → proof-backed claims.

Escalation

The escalation game's running time can be extended by any participant whose valid, vault-backed deposit meets the configured start bond. The deposit adds to the selected outcome's cumulative balance; it does not have to exceed the leading outcome. The contract waits three days after start() before the escalation clock becomes active. The chart then shows the seven-week escalation interval from activation; its illustrative curve uses a 10% start-bond-to-threshold ratio, while deployed games use their configured start bond and non-decision threshold. The deadline advances only when a deposit increases the median balance.

The game end

The game can end two ways

  1. The required escalation game increased high enough that only one side was able to keep up with it: That side wins and Statoblast finalizes on that outcome
  2. Two sides have reached the maximal deposit amount: Statoblast triggers a fork

After the game ends, the payout separates three amounts. Binding capital is the amount needed to support the final result. The safety boundary is the interval above that capital in which deposits remain eligible for rewards. Deposits beyond the reward-eligible cap return principal but do not share the reward pool. On a local win, eligible deposits on the winning outcome share the reward pool; on a fork, eligible deposits on each forked outcome are handled by the continuation rules. The winning-deposit haircut is burned as specified by the game.

Example: if final binding capital is 10 REP, the reward-eligible cap is 15 REP, the safety boundary is (10 REP, 15 REP], and the reward pool is 6 REP. If the winning side contributes 15 REP inside that reward-eligible window, each 5 REP deposit receives 7 REP. Winning depth above 15 REP receives principal back with no share of the reward pool.

Escalation game continuation

In AugurV2, when a fork happens, it cancels all currently running escalation games, and the players are refunded. This opens an attack vector for the oracle, discussed more in Zoltar Forks Should Not Cancel Escalation Games. The fix to the issue is to fork escalation games. This is called continuation.

Escalation game continuation uses Merkle Mountain Range carry proofs and nullifier roots to migrate user game deposits, without requiring expensive data copying efforts onchain.
