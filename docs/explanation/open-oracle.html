Explanation

Why Statoblast uses OpenOracle

Statoblast needs a fresh REP/collateral exchange rate (quoted as REP per collateral token) when it measures whether REP adequately secures collateral obligations. OpenOracle supplies an economically contestable price. A reporter posts a collateral/REP position at the claimed price, and anyone who believes the price is wrong can replace the report by trading against that position, so an inaccurate report is a profit opportunity. OpenOracle never decides whether a prediction-market answer is Yes, No, or Invalid.

Protected operations

A shared security pool feed protects commitment growth, surplus REP withdrawals, purchases and takeovers. The question coordinator is an adapter to that feed. Each capital or settlement operation still checks its own live safety conditions. Reporter deposits use separate REP custody.

Cached and fresh prices

An accepted report supplies a shared price for a limited freshness window. A sponsor can fund a new independent candidate when it expires. Child universes need their own accepted reports; a copied numeric price is not fresh evidence.

One request, dispute, and settlement

Alice wants to increase her commitment while the shared REP/collateral price is stale. She funds an independent report. Bob can trade against an inaccurate report before it settles, restarting its settlement clock. Once a valid report is accepted, Alice can submit her capital change with explicit bounds.

The security pool shares one accepted price across its questions, but each candidate has its own escrow. A pending dispute cannot lock another sponsor’s funds. A sponsor can attach capital operations to a report, though another candidate may settle first. See shared report coverage for funding and callback rules.

Security limitations

OpenOracle relies on participants noticing and profitably correcting inaccurate reports. Congestion, censorship, incorrect gas assumptions, insufficient economic incentives, or repeated disputes can delay or undermine that process. A fresh price is not a guarantee that an operation will succeed, and a settled price says nothing about the market question's outcome.

The security pool creator must choose the minimum report stake in the selected collateral when creating the security pool. The floor is immutable and inherited by children; reports also need funding proportional to exposure. The system assumes that the chosen floor keeps correcting harmful prices profitable after gas and other costs, even when exposure is low. The contracts do not establish this economic condition or query a collateral/ETH price feed. Assumption A18 states the requirement and what fails if it no longer holds.

See the integration reference for funding, callbacks, and recovery, and the security model for participation assumptions.
