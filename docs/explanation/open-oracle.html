Explanation

Why Statoblast uses OpenOracle

Statoblast needs a fresh REP/ETH exchange rate (quoted as REP per ETH) when it measures whether REP adequately secures ETH obligations. OpenOracle supplies an economically contestable price. A reporter posts a WETH/REP position at the claimed price, and anyone who believes the price is wrong can replace the report by trading against that position, so an inaccurate report is a profit opportunity. OpenOracle never decides whether a prediction-market answer is Yes, No, or Invalid.

Protected operations

A per-pool price coordinator protects REP withdrawals, liquidations, and staged changes to a vault's underwriting commitment, because each depends on REP value. Vault-backed Escalation Game deposits also require a fresh accepted price when the pool has any underwriting commitment; wallet deposits do not. Each SecurityPool still applies its own current health and authorization checks after a price is available.

Cached and fresh prices

An accepted report becomes the coordinator's cached price for a limited freshness window. An operation can use that cache while it remains fresh. When it is stale, a sponsor funds a new report and the coordinator stages eligible work until settlement. A child pool may inherit a numeric starting value, but it cannot use that value until the child accepts its own report.

One request, dispute, and settlement

Alice requests a REP withdrawal from her own vault while the pool's cached price is stale. She funds an initial REP/ETH report plus a settlement bounty, and the coordinator records her pending operation. Bob believes the report is inaccurate, so he replaces it with the required opposing position before settlement. The dispute restarts the settlement window. If nobody replaces Bob's report before its new deadline, any account can settle it; that settler collects Alice's bounty, and OpenOracle calls the coordinator. The coordinator validates the report, refreshes its cache, and attempts Alice's withdrawal against the pool's live safety checks.

Bob's replacement traded against Alice's position at her reported price, so she ends with a different mix of WETH and REP rather than her original deposit; the coordinator forwards whatever OpenOracle credits it for her report back to her. Her bounty is spent on settlement whether or not her withdrawal later succeeds.

Alice's staged withdrawal expires once the settlement period plus her chosen validity window (at most five minutes) has passed since she queued it. Because Bob's dispute restarted the settlement window, the price may arrive after that point; her withdrawal then fails as expired and she must stage it again.

Security limitations

OpenOracle relies on participants noticing and profitably correcting inaccurate reports. Congestion, censorship, incorrect gas assumptions, insufficient economic incentives, or repeated disputes can delay or undermine that process. A fresh price is not a guarantee that an operation will succeed, and a settled price says nothing about the market question's outcome.

Two limits are deliberate. First, the coordinator keeps no notional budget: one accepted price may authorize several operations during its freshness window, so the report position is a correction incentive rather than a bond sized to the value those operations move. Second, there is no absolute deadline: every valid dispute restarts the settlement clock, so a participant willing to keep funding replacements, each paying fees and posting the required position, can delay settlement indefinitely. The protocol does not claim liveness against such a participant.

Each report is sized from a gas-cost assumption. The priority fee in that assumption is fixed when the origin pool is created and inherited by its children; it is not observed from the request block. If prevailing priority fees are higher, the modeled incentive for a disputer is weaker; a value set too high raises the sponsor's initial WETH and REP requirement and can make requests impractical.

The reference's stress model estimates what it costs an attacker to hold a manipulated report against correction while collecting an outside payoff. Its results depend on a dispute barrier that the model does not derive: the default is only OpenOracle's fixed fees, and gas, priority fees, price impact, capital lockup, and price volatility over the settlement horizon all raise the real barrier (see OpenOracle's report-cost derivation). A deployment analysis must calibrate that barrier and decide what interval one model step represents; the model's output is an estimate, not an exposure cap the contracts enforce.

The OpenOracle integration reference specifies funding formulas, report sizing, sponsor rules, queues, callbacks, recovery paths, configuration, and interactive estimators. The security model states the participation assumptions.
