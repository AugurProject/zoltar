Vault liquidation

Liquidation moves part or all of an unhealthy vault's underwriting commitment, with a capped REP award, to an authorized receiver vault. The receiver must back its entire resulting commitment. Total commitments remain constant, so unrelated vaults retain their proportional obligations. The account that submits the liquidation receives no automatic protocol payment.

Target, receiver, and operator

  • The target vault is the unhealthy vault whose commitment is reduced.
  • The receiver vault accepts the transferred commitment and receives the REP award.
  • The operator requests and stages the liquidation, paying that transaction's gas and any oracle cost.

If the liquidation remains staged, any account may execute it later and pay the execution transaction's gas. That permissionless executor does not replace the staged operator named by a delegated approval.

The receiver and target must differ. The operator may be either one, but identity alone is not a health or Sybil-resistance boundary. Staging through the generic requestPriceIfNeededAndStageOperation call makes the operator the receiver and requires no signed approval; requestPriceIfNeededAndStageLiquidation names the receiver explicitly; a receiver other than the operator also needs a delegated approval.

The first release assumes the operator and receiver coordinate compensation externally; the protocol neither creates a keeper marketplace nor takes a fee from the award.

When a vault is liquidatable

Each owner sets an underwriting commitment separately from REP deposits. A price change affects health, not commitments. Each vault covers settlement collateral in proportion to its share of total commitments, but health is measured against its entire commitment, so a vault may be liquidatable with zero settlement collateral. Minting within total commitments needs no price or backing check; see the collateral capacity invariant.

Health combines two backing constraints, each evaluated at the live REP/ETH price with upward rounding. The associated-REP constraint requires pool-held backing plus eligible dispute stake to cover the commitment's REP value times the pool's security multiplier. The pool-held constraint requires pool-held backing alone to cover that REP value times a smaller multiplier: halfway between one and the security multiplier, but never below the liquidation reserve of 10,500 BPS (one plus the 5% award bonus). The reserve means a vault that meets the pool-held constraint holds enough REP to pay a full liquidation award.

A liquidation executes only when the target fails at least one constraint, the live price lies at least minLiquidationPriceDistanceBps (10% in the deployment configuration) beyond the target's failure threshold, and neither the target nor the receiver carries bad debt. The distance guard keeps a slightly wrong accepted price from liquidating a vault that is only marginally unhealthy. The SecurityPool reference specifies the exact checks.

A delegated approval may demand that the receiver be healthier than the protocol minimum after the transfer. That requirement is checked against live post-liquidation state at execution; a queue-time preview is never a guarantee. The LiquidationApprovalRegistry reference defines the field.

Commitment transfer and residual exposure

The requested amount bounds the commitment transferred. The REP award is the moved commitment's REP value at the live price plus a 5% bonus, capped by the target's transferable backing; a partial liquidation also leaves the target its minimum REP deposit. A sufficiently funded receiver can take the entire commitment even when the award is underfunded. Partial liquidation leaves the residual commitment with the target; it writes off nothing and deletes no settlement liabilities.

The receiver must authorize the transferred commitment and satisfy both backing constraints for its entire resulting commitment. Remaining target commitments and receiver positions must meet the minimum sizes. Earned fees stay with their owners and are checkpointed before weights move. Retained underbacked commitments continue earning fees; poor health does not suspend fee rights. The contract reference specifies award rounding.

Bounded receiver approval

A receiver can approve an operator directly or by a bounded signature. The approval is specific to a pool, limits how much commitment the receiver will accept, sets a validity window and minimum resulting health, and can be revoked for future liquidations. Existing staged work retains its reservation so cleanup remains deterministic. See the LiquidationApprovalRegistry reference for fields, nonce rules, and wallet-signature handling.

Reservation and queued execution

When the receiver is not the operator, the price coordinator reserves the receiver's approval quota when the liquidation is staged, whether it runs immediately on a fresh REP/ETH price or waits for one. Execution then rechecks the live target and receiver rather than trusting the earlier preview. Success consumes only the commitment actually moved and releases unused quota; expiration or terminal failure releases the reservation so the receiver is not blocked permanently. The REP/ETH oracle operations reference covers staging, recovery, and failure handling.

Fork isolation

Approvals and reservations belong to one pool and its coordinator and are not copied into child pools or universes; see what Zoltar does not guarantee. A receiver approving liabilities in one universe has not approved them in a child. Pending parent operations remain permissionlessly cleanable after the parent becomes unable to execute, releasing their reservations.
