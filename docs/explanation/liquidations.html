Vault Liquidation

The protocol relies on security pools maintaining enough REP value to secure their open interest. If a vault no longer provides sufficient REP backing for its coverage commitment, it can be liquidated. This ensures that coverage is transferred to vaults that are able to maintain the required level of security.

A vault becomes liquidatable when, at the current REP/ETH price, its REP backing falls below the minimum required for the amount of open interest it secures. Liquidation transfers a bounded portion of that coverage commitment from the unhealthy vault to a healthy liquidator vault.

For every unit of coverage transferred, the liquidator receives the fixed REP reward defined by the protocol. As a result, only the portion of the unhealthy vault's coverage that can be fully funded by its pool-held REP is transferred. Non-transferable claims, accrued fees, and any surplus REP remain with the original vault. If a maximum liquidation still cannot transfer all requested coverage, the remaining uncovered amount is recorded as bad debt.

Position Model and Actors

The target vault is the unsafe position being reduced, and the liquidator vault is the healthy position accepting its liabilities. Each vault can hold an ETH-denominated coverage commitment, pool-held vault REP backing represented by REP backing units, accrued fees, and non-transferable escalation claims. Locally attributed escrow records REP deposited by that vault in the current game and counts toward current-game health. Inherited continuation backing remains aggregate until a committed deposit proof is settled and does not receive per-vault child health credit.

Execution first caps the coverage commitment slice to the amount whose fixed 5%-bonus REP award is fully funded. Only that pool-held vault REP backing and funded coverage commitment move. Accrued fees and escalation claims stay with the target vault, and the liquidator must remain healthy after assuming the funded coverage commitment and receiving its complete award.

Two Health Conditions

Ordinary underwriting counts pool-held vault REP backing and locally attributed escrow in the current game because both remain pool-associated security capital. Inherited continuation backing stays aggregate and is settled from committed deposit proofs. Liquidation never moves a claim. Migration safety counts only pool-held vault REP backing because every locked escalation claim can lose and therefore cannot credibly choose a child universe for parent open interest.

These tests apply to each affected vault. Aggregate pool backing is checked separately, so a well-backed pool cannot conceal an individually unsafe vault and one weak vault does not misstate the pool-wide reserve. Separate partial and maximum examples

Use 2 REP / ETH and the fixed 5% award for these accounting examples. The unrounded award for 7 ETH is 7 × 2 × 1.05 = 14.7 REP. The contract ceilings at integer attoREP precision, so 14.7 REP remains 14.7 REP; an unrounded 14.7000000000000000001 REP becomes 14.700000000000000001 REP. Backing-unit conversion can change the reported transfer, so VaultLiquidated is authoritative for the receipt.

Integer execution rounds the award up, converts it to REP backing units with another ceiling, and converts those backing units back to the reported REP amount with a floor. The ceilings ensure the liquidator receives at least the complete attoREP award. The vaultRepBackingTransferredAttoRep field in VaultLiquidated reports this final post-conversion amount; target surplus never substitutes for an underfunded award.

  • Coverage commitment is capped by the request, target coverage commitment, and the complete award fundable from available pool-held vault REP backing. A partial request also preserves the minimum target REP and coverage commitment positions.
  • The award is quoted with an attoREP ceiling, converted to REP backing units with a ceiling, and converted back to the reported pool-held vault REP backing amount.
  • The target retains all accrued unpaid fees, including fees checkpointed immediately before liquidation.
  • Every escalation claim remains owned by its committed depositor and pays only that depositor if it wins.
  • A maximum request normally moves the funded coverage commitment and its complete award. Any untransferred residual coverage commitment is recorded in vaultBadDebtAttoEth and cumulative totalBadDebtAttoEth, then removed from total and fee-eligible active coverage commitment.
  • If a positive funded slice would leave the caller's resulting coverage commitment below MIN_COVERAGE_COMMITMENT_ATTO_ETH, the maximum-request path transfers no coverage commitment, REP, or REP backing units and records the target's complete coverage commitment as bad debt. It emits no VaultLiquidated or post-transfer caller checkpoint; fee updates before execution may still emit caller or target checkpoints.

There is no transfer or approval function for escalation claims. The claim remains bound to the depositor committed in the carry leaf, and settlement is its only payout path.

Because claim ownership cannot change, a vault owner can continue reporting through the same security vault without owner-slot or per-vault bundle limits. Each carry leaf independently commits its depositor, outcome, amount, stable index, cumulative amount, and source node.

Liquidation does not inspect or value this claim commitment. Its gas cost is independent of the target's reporter count, portfolio size, and fork depth.

Bounded Execution

Liquidation reads only the target and liquidator vault accounting. It performs no claim scan, proof verification, owner rewrite, or descendant import, so its work is constant regardless of reporter count and fork depth. The carry commitment is used only when a relayer later settles an inherited claim.

Settlement relay calls accept caller-sized deposit or proof arrays and should be chunked to fit available gas. They consume committed leaves with replay protection and do not create owner registries.

Queued Execution

The oracle coordinator snapshots the target's coverage commitment, pool-held vault REP backing, pool-held REP-per-backing-unit rate, and numerically attributed current-game dispute-staked REP when liquidation is queued, then validates the settled price at execution. Its minimum-distance guard uses those queue-time values, but execution invalidates only changed target backing units or coverage commitment and re-evaluates health from the live pool rate, live balances, and live escalation escrow. A target deposit, withdrawal, earlier liquidation, or coverage commitment change therefore cancels the old quote, so REP backing deliberately assigned to the target cannot become a queued keeper's windfall. A raw REP transfer to the pool changes no vault REP backing units and does not cancel execution. That distinction is game-theoretically necessary: otherwise an unsafe target could spend one attoREP after every queue to buy a deterministic liquidation veto. The live pool rate converts the bonus-priced REP award into backing units. A donation that does not cross either live health boundary cannot stop execution; even a small donation can stop it when the target is sufficiently close to a boundary. A partial target with positive remaining coverage commitment preserves both its REP and coverage commitment floors. Whenever funded coverage commitment transfers, the caller must satisfy both floors and remain healthy. A bad-debt-only maximum execution transfers nothing to the caller and therefore performs no caller position-floor check.
