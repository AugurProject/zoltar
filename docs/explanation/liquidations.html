Vault liquidation

A funded repair transfers unsupported underwriting commitment to a consenting receiver. The original provider keeps all principal REP; the receiver pledges their own REP and earns future fees on the transferred commitment.

Target, receiver, and keeper

The target owns an underbacked position. The receiver funds the transferred commitment at its chosen health target. Neither principal nor past earnings change owner. There is no immediate liquidation award or protocol-funded gas reimbursement.

When a position is liquidatable

All free and committed REP deposited for the owner in this security pool universe counts toward its backing. Earned REP income is separate. The owner's voluntary health target controls new exposure and surplus withdrawals; merely falling below that buffer does not trigger liquidation.

The protocol detects distress at the shared oracle’s protective liquidation price. Admission and receiver backing use the conservative price. An adverse observation establishes eligibility, but elapsed time never increases compensation or transfers REP ownership.

Cure before takeover

The owner can add REP while the position is healthy at the current liquidation price. While it remains underbacked at that price, REP top-ups are rejected and cannot cancel liquidation. A later oracle observation that restores health makes deposits available again. The owner can still reduce unused commitment under the ordinary cure rules. A takeover first removes available unused commitment without releasing principal. If that cure restores health, the call ends without a transfer. Otherwise the requested transfer is capped at the commitment needed to restore the source at the conservative price, while retaining its economic minimum. The receiver supplies all backing for the transferred obligation. While a minimum-size source remains underbacked, it needs additional healthy capacity followed by ordinary retirement; oracle price recovery can also restore its health. Repeated repairs cannot take principal or keep reducing an already healthy position.

Existing earned fees remain with their owner. Underbacked commitment stops earning new fees. Principal, fees, and reporter claims use separate accounting. See security pool capital and retirement for exits and the liquidation reference for exact contract bounds.

Receiver consent

The receiver calls the capital ledger directly. Bounded repairs pin the accepted price epoch, receiver health target and source cohort, and cap resulting principal, commitment and own REP contribution. The minimum-award argument must be zero; positive principal-award requests revert.

Execution and incentives

Quotes are checked against live state. Insufficient backing or changed consent bounds revert atomically. Receivers invest new capital for future fee income, which depends on demand, fees and continued health; participation is not guaranteed. Pledged principal cannot be withdrawn while healthy commitment is below demand. Previously free principal and separately earned income remain withdrawable. A wrong accepted oracle price can still misclassify health; these rules do not establish the fair REP price.

Fork isolation

Positions belong to one security pool and universe. A fork stops ordinary parent operations. Each owner chooses a branch during the shared choice window; the receiver in a child must authorize its own child-universe exposure. See shared fork choices.
