Vault liquidation

A funded repair transfers unsupported underwriting commitment to a consenting receiver. A one-hour auction transfers a growing REP bonus from the target’s backing directly into the receiver’s vault. The receiver supplies any remaining backing and earns future fees on the transferred commitment.

Target, receiver, and keeper

The target owns an underbacked position. The receiver funds the transferred commitment at its chosen health target. The bonus changes principal ownership inside the capital ledger; no bonus is paid to the receiver’s wallet. Past earnings remain with their original owners. There is no protocol-funded gas reimbursement.

When a position is liquidatable

All free and committed REP deposited for the owner in this portfolio universe counts toward its backing. Earned REP income is separate. The owner's voluntary health target controls new exposure and surplus withdrawals; merely falling below that buffer does not trigger liquidation.

The protocol detects distress at the shared oracle’s protective liquidation price. Admission and receiver backing use the conservative price. An adverse observation starts the auction. Its available bonus grows linearly from zero to all pledged REP over one hour, then stays at maximum terms until repair or recovery. There is no separate penalty cap or fixed expiry. Partial fills consume the vested bonus rather than restarting its growth. The auction clock keeps running while the price is stale, but liquidation cannot execute until a fresh report arrives. If the fresh report confirms continuing distress, the bonus reflects all elapsed time, including the stale interval; renewal does not reset the clock or earlier partial awards.

Cure before takeover

The owner can add REP while the position is healthy at the current liquidation price. While it remains underbacked at that price, REP top-ups are rejected and cannot cancel liquidation. A later oracle observation that restores health makes deposits available again. The owner can still reduce unused commitment under the ordinary cure rules. A takeover first removes available unused commitment without releasing principal, measuring health at the conservative repair price. If that cure restores health at that price, the call ends without a transfer. Otherwise the repair amount is calculated using the target’s backing after subtracting the bonus. The receiver’s bonus and own REP must back its entire resulting commitment at its chosen health target. A smaller fill receives only its proportional bonus and must either restore conservative backing completely or leave the source below the liquidation price so another receiver can continue the repair; a transfer that would strand it between the liquidation and conservative prices reverts. Inherited retirement targets below the economic minimum become full exits for a new receiver. If a minimum-size remainder cannot be retained, the entire commitment can move; unawarded REP remains credited to the original owner. Repeated fills cannot collect the same vested REP twice, and a healthy position is no longer eligible.

Existing earned fees remain with their owner. Underbacked commitment stops earning new fees. Principal, fees, and reporter claims use separate accounting. See portfolio capital and retirement for exits and the liquidation reference for exact contract bounds.

Receiver consent

The receiver calls the capital ledger directly. Bounded repairs pin the accepted price epoch, receiver health target and source cohort, and cap resulting principal, commitment and own REP contribution. The minimum-award argument protects the receiver’s expected REP bonus; insufficient awards revert atomically.

Execution and incentives

Quotes are checked against live state. Insufficient backing or changed consent bounds revert atomically. Receivers acquire pledged REP as well as future fee rights. The award reduces the new REP they must supply, but is not spendable gas funding. Fee income depends on demand, fees and continued health; participation is not guaranteed. Pledged principal cannot be withdrawn while healthy commitment is below demand. Previously free principal and separately earned income remain withdrawable. A wrong accepted oracle price can misclassify health and transfer the target’s pledged REP to a receiver; these rules do not establish the fair REP price.

Fork isolation

Positions belong to one portfolio and universe. A fork stops ordinary parent operations. A child starts a new liquidation history after activation; parent auction age is not inherited. Each owner chooses a branch during the shared choice window; the receiver in a child must authorize its own child-universe exposure. See shared fork choices.
