Vault liquidation

Liquidation transfers an underbacked vault's standing ETH commitment and a capped REP award to an authorized receiver. The receiver must back its entire resulting limit. Total commitments remain constant, so unrelated vaults retain their proportional obligations. The operator receives no automatic protocol payment.

Target, receiver, and operator

  • The target vault is the unhealthy vault whose position is reduced.
  • The receiver vault accepts the transferred ETH commitment and receives the REP award.
  • The operator requests and stages the liquidation, paying that transaction's gas and any oracle cost.

If the liquidation remains staged, any account may execute it later and pay the execution transaction's gas. That permissionless executor does not replace the staged operator named by a delegated approval.

The receiver and target must differ. The operator may be either one, but identity alone is not a health or Sybil-resistance boundary. For the backward-compatible path, omitting a delegated receiver makes the operator the receiver and requires no signed approval.

The operator receives no liquidation ownership or REP merely for submitting the transaction. The first release assumes the operator and receiver coordinate compensation externally; the protocol neither creates a keeper marketplace nor takes a fee from the award.

Capacity and live health

Each owner sets a standing ETH underwriting limit, separately from REP deposits. The pool maintains the sum of limits incrementally. A price change affects backing health, not the authorized limits. Each vault covers settlement collateral in proportion to its share of total commitments, while backing and liquidation use its full limit. See the coverage certificate invariants for mint safety without an on-chain vault scan.

Full-limit backing uses the live REP/ETH price and upward rounding. Associated REP includes pool-held backing and eligible dispute stake. The separate migration-safety requirement counts only pool-held REP and includes the liquidation reserve. A vault may be liquidatable with zero settlement collateral.

A delegated approval may demand that the receiver be healthier than the protocol minimum after the transfer. That requirement is checked against live post-liquidation state at execution; a queue-time preview is never a guarantee. The LiquidationApprovalRegistry reference defines the field.

Commitment transfer and residual exposure

The requested amount bounds the ETH commitment transferred. The REP award is capped by the target's available backing; a sufficiently funded receiver can take the entire commitment even when the nominal award is underfunded. Partial liquidation retains the residual limit. It does not create a write-off or delete settlement liabilities.

The receiver must authorize the transferred commitment and satisfy both backing constraints for its entire resulting limit. Remaining target commitments and receiver positions must meet the minimum sizes. Earned fees stay with their owners and are checkpointed before weights move. Retained underbacked commitments continue earning fees; mint health does not suspend fee rights. The contract reference specifies award rounding.

Bounded receiver approval

A receiver can approve an operator directly or by a bounded signature. The approval is specific to a pool, limits how much ETH commitment the receiver will accept, sets a validity window and minimum resulting health, and can be revoked for future liquidations. Existing staged work retains its reservation so cleanup remains deterministic. See the LiquidationApprovalRegistry reference for fields, nonce rules, and wallet-signature handling.

Reservation and queued execution

The price coordinator reserves the receiver's approval quota while it obtains a fresh REP/ETH price. Execution then rechecks the live target and receiver rather than trusting the earlier preview. Success consumes only the commitment actually moved and releases unused quota; expiration or terminal failure releases the reservation so the receiver is not blocked permanently. The REP/ETH oracle operations reference covers staging, recovery, and failure handling.

Fork isolation

Approvals and reservations belong to one pool and its coordinator and are not copied into child pools or universes; see what Zoltar does not guarantee. A receiver approving liabilities in one universe has not approved them in a child. Pending parent operations remain permissionlessly cleanable after the parent becomes unable to execute, releasing their reservations.
