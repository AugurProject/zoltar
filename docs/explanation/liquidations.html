Vault liquidation

A funded repair transfers unsupported underwriting commitment to a consenting receiver. A one-hour auction transfers a growing takeover reward (REP taken from the target’s pledged REP) directly into the receiver’s vault. For the target this is a liquidation penalty. The receiver supplies any remaining backing and earns future fees on the transferred commitment.

Target, receiver, and keeper

The target owns an underbacked position. The receiver funds the transferred commitment at its chosen health target. The takeover reward changes principal ownership inside the capital ledger; nothing is paid to the receiver’s wallet, and the protocol adds no REP of its own. Past earnings remain with their original owners. There is no protocol-funded gas reimbursement.

When a position is liquidatable

All free and committed REP deposited for the owner in this security pool and universe counts toward its backing. Earned REP income is separate. The owner's voluntary health target controls new exposure and surplus withdrawals; merely falling below that buffer does not trigger liquidation.

The protocol detects distress at the protective liquidation price. Admission and receiver backing use the conservative price. A fresh adverse observation starts an auction whose takeover reward grows from zero to all pledged REP over one hour. Partial fills consume vested rewards without restarting the clock. The auction remains fillable after the observation expires, and a later accepted price updates the valuation or ends the auction if health recovers.

Cure before takeover

The owner can add REP while healthy at the liquidation price. While underbacked, the owner can reduce unused commitment under the cure rules. A takeover first releases unused commitment without moving principal. If that restores backing at the conservative repair price, no transfer is needed. Otherwise the takeover reward reduces the backing left behind, and the receiver must back its resulting commitment at its own health target. A partial fill must either restore conservative backing or leave the source eligible for another takeover.

Existing earned fees remain with their owner. Underbacked commitment stops earning new fees. Principal, fees, and reporter claims use separate accounting. See security pool capital and retirement for exits and the liquidation reference for exact contract bounds.

Receiver consent

The receiver calls the capital ledger directly and chooses bounds on its own contribution and the resulting position. The liquidation reference gives the exact limits and retirement rules.

Execution and incentives

Quotes are checked against live state. Insufficient backing or changed consent bounds revert atomically. Receivers acquire pledged REP as well as future fee rights. The takeover reward reduces the new REP they must supply, but is not spendable gas funding. Fee income depends on demand, fees and continued health; participation is not guaranteed. Pledged principal cannot be withdrawn while healthy commitment is below demand. Previously free principal and separately earned income remain withdrawable. A wrong accepted oracle price can misclassify health and transfer the target’s pledged REP to a receiver; these rules do not establish the fair REP price.

Fork isolation

Positions belong to one security pool and universe. A fork stops ordinary parent operations. A child starts a new liquidation history after activation; parent auction age is not inherited. Each owner chooses a branch during the shared choice window; the receiver in a child must authorize its own child-universe exposure. See shared fork choices.
