Liquidation Design

Liquidation transfers one proportional slice of an unsafe vault to a healthy vault. The slice contains the same fraction of security-bond allowance, free REP, unpaid fees, every non-tradeable escalation claim, and the claim's future payout and continuation or child-REP entitlements.

Two Health Conditions

Ordinary underwriting counts free REP and locally attributed escrow in the current game because both remain pool-associated security capital. Inherited continuation backing stays aggregate, while each continuation copies current payout ownership into one consolidated checkpoint. Liquidation therefore moves claims from the current game only, and those claims control eventual payout without receiving per-vault child health credit. Migration safety counts only free REP because every locked escalation claim can lose and therefore cannot credibly choose a child universe for parent open interest.

These tests apply to each affected vault. Aggregate pool backing is checked separately, so a well-backed pool cannot conceal an individually unsafe vault and one weak vault does not misstate the pool-wide reserve.

The migration multiplier is derived halfway between one and the configured pool multiplier, so 1 < migrationSecurityMultiplier < poolSecurityMultiplier. Multipliers use basis points: an origin pool therefore requires at least 10,002 BPS (1.0002×), which derives a migration multiplier of 10,001 BPS (1.0001×). The exact minimum preserves both strict inequalities despite integer division. The lower bound makes OI routing costly to corrupt; the upper bound leaves some REP available for escalation without weakening the ordinary underwriting target.

Proportional Bundle

For a target allowance A and moved allowance D, the common economic fraction is f = D / A. It is a ratio, not one universal Solidity expression: each stored category applies that ratio in its own units.

f=debtMovedtargetAllowance

Bundle fractionEvery position category uses the same debt fraction.

  • Debt is capped by the requested and available allowance; debt-dust or REP-dust rules can promote the slice to a full close.
  • Free REP first floors the moved pool-ownership units and then converts those units to REP, so a partial slice can contain a second floor.
  • Accrued fees floor directly by f; each escalation bundle independently floors the owner shares moved by f.
  • Residual integer dust normally stays with the target. If a nonzero liquidation fraction is too small to move even one share of any live escalation bundle, the whole transaction reverts; a full close transfers each remaining stored balance exactly. Exhausted bounded claim-owner capacity also reverts instead of silently omitting a claim.
  • The liquidator receives fraction f of free REP and accrued unpaid fees.
  • The same fraction of every escalation claim changes vault ownership, while the underlying REP stays locked in the game.
  • If a transferred claim wins, the game divides payout among its bounded current vault owners in proportion to their claim shares; non-final owners receive floored integer amounts and the final share-exhausting owner receives the atomic-unit remainder. If it loses, that part of the bundle pays nothing.
  • A full liquidation uses f = 1 and transfers the complete bundle.

There is no user transfer or approval function for escalation claims. Liquidation is the only ownership-change path, and settlement is the only payout path. This prevents a secondary market from separating likely winners from liabilities while still allowing an unsafe vault to be resolved.

A vault also cannot add another deposit to the same game after liquidation has split or fully moved its claim bundle. Deposits from one vault share one fixed bundle identity, so aggregating fresh principal after a split would give the old liquidator shares in capital it never acquired. Rejecting the deposit preserves the bargain made at liquidation: transferred shares cover only claims that already existed. The vault may participate again in a later game with a fresh bundle, and every existing owner can still claim this game's final payout.

The sub-share revert is atomic for the same reason. Moving allowance and aggregate principal while every claim-share calculation floors to zero would make accounting say the liquidator owns risk that its payout registry does not own. Keepers must choose a larger debt slice (or a full close), ensuring liabilities, contingent upside, and loss exposure always move together.

Why Cherry-picking Is Forbidden

If liquidators could take only free REP, rational actors would leave likely losing claims and debt behind. The original vault would become a toxic remainder and liquidation would amplify adverse selection instead of transferring risk. A fixed proportional bundle makes the liquidator price the whole position: liquid claims, contingent claims, fees, and obligations move together.

The transfer is path-independent apart from integer floors: two slices totaling the same fraction move the same economic categories as one combined slice. A liquidator must remain healthy after accepting the bundle, so liquidation cannot merely relocate insolvency.

A proportional partial transfer does not promise to make the target healthy: moving the same fraction of backing and allowance usually preserves its coverage ratio. It reduces the target's absolute exposure and leaves a later liquidator free to take another bounded slice or the full remainder. Requiring instant target health would make most proportional partial liquidations impossible and would force every keeper to finance a full close.

The protocol also does not manufacture a separate REP bonus. A rational liquidator compares the acquired free REP, accrued fees, and risk-adjusted value of contingent claims with the allowance and capital it assumes, plus gas and delay. If that complete bundle has negative expected value, the transaction is optional and may not occur. This is an explicit liveness boundary under A01 and A02, not a hidden guarantee of profitable liquidation.

Bounded Execution

Liquidation reads one consolidated current-game checkpoint and fixed-size bundle and owner registries. The canonical registry, bundle, owner, and internal scan limits avoid unbounded Solidity loops and make worst-case gas knowable. Reaching a cap rejects another split rather than creating a position that later cannot be settled.

Those limits apply to ownership changes and liquidation. Separate settlement relay calls accept caller-sized deposit or proof arrays and should be chunked to fit available gas; they cannot create more bundles or owners.

Queued Execution

The oracle coordinator snapshots the target's allowance, free-REP ownership, pool REP-per-ownership rate, and numerically attributed current-game escalation REP when liquidation is queued, then validates the settled price at execution. Its minimum-distance guard uses those queue-time values, but execution invalidates only a changed target ownership or allowance and re-evaluates health from the live pool rate, live balances, and live escalation escrow. A target deposit, withdrawal, earlier liquidation, or allowance change therefore cancels the old quote, so collateral deliberately assigned to the target cannot become a queued keeper's windfall. A raw REP transfer to the pool changes no vault ownership and does not cancel execution. That distinction is game-theoretically necessary: otherwise an unsafe target could spend one atomic REP after every queue to buy a deterministic liquidation veto. Pool-wide surplus belongs to all ownership units proportionally, so moving the quoted liability fraction together with the same ownership fraction remains the coherent bundle at the live rate. A sufficiently large donation can still make the target healthy and stop liquidation through the live safety check; a dust donation cannot. The liquidator must remain healthy; both vaults must obey minimum REP and allowance floors, while the target may remain unsafe after a partial slice.
