Liquidation Design

Liquidation transfers only the security-bond allowance whose complete fixed 5% award can be paid from an unsafe vault's free REP. Non-tradeable escalation claims, accrued vault fees, and free REP above the award never transfer. A maximum request records any untransferred residual as bad debt and removes it from active pool allowance instead of underpaying the liquidator or diluting other holders.

Position Model and Actors

The target vault is the unsafe position being reduced, and the liquidator vault is the healthy position accepting its liabilities. Each vault can hold an ETH-denominated security-bond allowance, free REP represented by pool ownership, accrued fees, and non-tradeable escalation claims. Locally attributed escrow records REP deposited by that vault in the current game and counts toward current-game health. Inherited continuation backing remains aggregate until a committed deposit proof is settled and does not receive per-vault child health credit.

Execution first caps the debt slice to the amount whose fixed 5%-bonus REP award is fully funded. Only that free REP and funded debt move. Accrued fees and escalation claims stay with the target vault, and the liquidator must remain healthy after assuming the funded debt and receiving its complete award.

Two Health Conditions

Ordinary underwriting counts free REP and locally attributed escrow in the current game because both remain pool-associated security capital. Inherited continuation backing stays aggregate and is settled from committed deposit proofs. Liquidation never moves a claim. Migration safety counts only free REP because every locked escalation claim can lose and therefore cannot credibly choose a child universe for parent open interest.

These tests apply to each affected vault. Aggregate pool backing is checked separately, so a well-backed pool cannot conceal an individually unsafe vault and one weak vault does not misstate the pool-wide reserve.

The configured migration multiplier is derived halfway between one and the pool multiplier. The effective free-REP multiplier is the greater of that value and 10,500 BPS, matching the complete liquidation award. At the minimum permitted pool multiplier of 10,002 BPS, the halfway value is only 10,001 BPS, so the 10,500-BPS liquidation reserve applies. This prevents a healthy vault from deliberately locking the free REP needed to fund its ordinary 105% liquidation award.

Bonus-Priced Liquidation

For moved allowance D, the liquidator receives the fixed 5%-bonus award entirely as free REP. Escalation claims and earned ETH fees are outside the liquidated position.

Funded debt capIn an initialized pool, a partial request first computes reserveOwnership = ⌈MIN_REP_DEPOSIT × poolOwnershipDenominator / totalRep⌉. If that reserve is at least the target ownership, no partial debt or REP transfer is fundable. Otherwise, availableFreeRep = ⌊(targetOwnership - reserveOwnership) × totalRep / poolOwnershipDenominator⌋. A maximum request asks for at least the target's full current allowance (requestedDebt >= targetAllowance); it uses availableFreeRep = ⌊targetOwnership × totalRep / poolOwnershipDenominator⌋. The user queues that amount through the coordinator, which calls the pool; the pool's maximum-request path records remaining allowance through the bad-debt backstop. With a zero denominator, ownership converts at PRICE_PRECISION; with nonzero ownership denominator and zero pool REP, the available amount is zero.

Integer execution rounds the award up, converts it to pool ownership with another ceiling, and converts that ownership back to the reported REP amount with a floor. The ceilings ensure the liquidator receives at least the complete atomic-unit award. The repAmountMoved field in VaultLiquidated reports this final post-conversion amount; target surplus never substitutes for an underfunded award.

  • Debt is capped by the request, target allowance, and the complete award fundable from available free REP. A partial request also preserves the minimum target REP and debt positions.
  • The award is quoted with an atomic-unit ceiling, converted to pool ownership with a ceiling, and converted back to the reported free-REP amount.
  • The target retains all accrued unpaid fees, including fees checkpointed immediately before liquidation.
  • Every escalation claim remains owned by its committed depositor and pays only that vault if it wins.
  • A maximum request normally moves the funded debt and its complete award. Any untransferred residual allowance is recorded in vaultBadDebt and cumulative totalBadDebt, then removed from total and fee-eligible active allowance.
  • If a positive funded slice would leave the caller's resulting allowance below MIN_SECURITY_BOND_DEBT, the maximum-request path transfers no debt, REP, or ownership and records the target's complete allowance as bad debt. It emits no VaultLiquidated or post-transfer caller checkpoint; fee updates before execution may still emit caller or target checkpoints.

There is no transfer or approval function for escalation claims. The claim remains bound to the depositor committed in the carry leaf, and settlement is its only payout path.

Because claim ownership cannot change, a vault can continue reporting in the same game without owner-slot or per-vault bundle limits. Each carry leaf independently commits its depositor, outcome, amount, stable index, cumulative amount, and source node.

Liquidation does not inspect or value this claim commitment. Its gas cost is independent of the target's reporter count, portfolio size, and fork depth.

Why Claims Stay Put

A liquidator cannot cherry-pick escalation claims because it cannot acquire any of them. Its compensation is the deterministic 105% free-REP award for assuming debt; the target retains both the upside and downside of every report it originated.

This separation also keeps settlement identity simple: the committed depositor remains the payout address at every fork depth. A liquidator must remain healthy after accepting the debt and free REP.

A partial transfer does not promise to make the target healthy. It reduces the target's absolute exposure and leaves a later liquidator free to take another bounded slice or submit a maximum request that moves only funded debt and records the untransferred remainder as bad debt. Requiring instant target health would make many partial liquidations impossible and would force keepers to finance a larger slice than requested, potentially the target's complete allowance.

The 105% quote is paid from free REP and compensates execution risk, gas, and capital. Accrued ETH fees, escalation claims, and free-REP surplus are always inaccessible. The backstop never mints ownership or socializes the missing bonus: it writes the residual allowance out of active capacity, emits VaultBadDebtRecorded, and can leave existing collateral above active allowance so no additional complete sets can be created until capacity is restored.

Bounded Execution

Liquidation reads only the target and liquidator vault accounting. It performs no claim scan, proof verification, owner rewrite, or descendant import, so its work is constant regardless of reporter count and fork depth. The carry commitment is used only when a relayer later settles an inherited claim.

Settlement relay calls accept caller-sized deposit or proof arrays and should be chunked to fit available gas. They consume committed leaves with replay protection and do not create owner registries.

Queued Execution

The oracle coordinator snapshots the target's allowance, free-REP ownership, pool REP-per-ownership rate, and numerically attributed current-game escalation REP when liquidation is queued, then validates the settled price at execution. Its minimum-distance guard uses those queue-time values, but execution invalidates only a changed target ownership or allowance and re-evaluates health from the live pool rate, live balances, and live escalation escrow. A target deposit, withdrawal, earlier liquidation, or allowance change therefore cancels the old quote, so collateral deliberately assigned to the target cannot become a queued keeper's windfall. A raw REP transfer to the pool changes no vault ownership and does not cancel execution. That distinction is game-theoretically necessary: otherwise an unsafe target could spend one atomic REP after every queue to buy a deterministic liquidation veto. The live pool rate converts the bonus-priced REP award into ownership units. A donation that does not cross either live health boundary cannot stop execution; even a small donation can stop it when the target is sufficiently close to a boundary. A partial target with positive remaining allowance preserves both its REP and allowance floors. Whenever funded debt moves, the caller must satisfy both floors and remain healthy. A bad-debt-only maximum execution transfers nothing to the caller and therefore performs no caller position-floor check.
