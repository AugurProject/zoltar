Liquidation Design

Liquidation transfers security-bond allowance from an unsafe vault to a healthy vault. The protocol computes a gross REP award with a fixed 5% bonus, credits the matching fraction of every non-tradeable escalation claim against it, and transfers only the uncovered award as free REP. Accrued vault fees never transfer. Free-REP surplus remains with the target unless a nonzero remainder would fall below the minimum deposit, in which case that dust remainder is swept.

Position Model and Actors

The target vault is the unsafe position being reduced, and the liquidator vault is the healthy position accepting its liabilities. Each vault can hold an ETH-denominated security-bond allowance, free REP represented by pool ownership, and non-tradeable escalation claims. Locally attributed escrow records REP deposited by that vault in the current game and counts toward current-game health. The consolidated payout checkpoint is broader: it records every local or inherited claim bundle that can eventually pay the vault, but inherited continuation backing does not receive per-vault health credit.

Execution first quotes the debt slice, floors the shares moved from each payout bundle, and values exactly those moved shares in current REP units. That claim value is credited against the bonus-priced gross award; free REP supplies only the uncovered remainder. The resulting target and liquidator positions must then satisfy their applicable minimum-balance rules, and the liquidator must remain healthy.

Two Health Conditions

Ordinary underwriting counts free REP and locally attributed escrow in the current game because both remain pool-associated security capital. Inherited continuation backing stays aggregate, while each continuation copies current payout ownership into one consolidated checkpoint. Liquidation therefore moves claims from the current game only, and those claims control eventual payout without receiving per-vault child health credit. Migration safety counts only free REP because every locked escalation claim can lose and therefore cannot credibly choose a child universe for parent open interest.

These tests apply to each affected vault. Aggregate pool backing is checked separately, so a well-backed pool cannot conceal an individually unsafe vault and one weak vault does not misstate the pool-wide reserve.

Multipliers use basis points: an origin pool therefore requires at least 10,002 BPS (1.0002×), which derives a migration multiplier of 10,001 BPS (1.0001×). The exact minimum preserves both strict inequalities despite integer division. The lower bound makes OI routing costly to corrupt; the upper bound leaves some REP available for escalation without weakening the ordinary underwriting target.

Bonus-Priced Liquidation

For a target allowance A and moved allowance D, escalation claim ownership moves by f = D / A. The claim's current effective REP amount is credited at face value against the fixed 5%-bonus gross award. Only a positive remainder is paid as free REP; earned ETH fees are outside the liquidated position.

After claim credit, integer execution converts the remaining free-REP quote to ownership with a floor and converts it back to the reported REP amount with another floor. The repAmountMoved field in VaultLiquidated reports this final post-conversion amount. Full and dust-promoted closes use the same capped award, transferring all free REP only when the target remainder would otherwise violate the minimum-deposit floor.

  • Debt is capped by the requested and available allowance; debt-dust or REP-dust rules can promote the slice to a full close.
  • The gross award is quoted with an atomic-unit ceiling; the exact current REP represented by the shares actually moved from each bundle is summed and deducted before free REP is converted to pool ownership with a floor and then back to the reported amount.
  • Each escalation bundle independently floors the owner shares moved by f.
  • Residual integer dust normally stays with the target. A bundle whose share calculation floors to zero is left unchanged while other bundles and the debt slice move; it contributes zero claim credit. A full close transfers each remaining stored balance exactly. Exhausted bounded claim-owner capacity still reverts instead of silently omitting a claim.
  • The target retains all accrued unpaid fees, including fees checkpointed immediately before liquidation.
  • The same fraction of every escalation claim changes vault ownership, while the underlying REP stays locked in the game.
  • If a transferred claim wins, the game divides payout among its bounded current vault owners in proportion to their claim shares; non-final owners receive floored integer amounts and the final share-exhausting owner receives the atomic-unit remainder. If it loses, that part of the bundle pays nothing.
  • A full liquidation uses f = 1 and transfers every escalation claim, but leaves free REP above the uncovered gross award and all accrued fees with the target unless the free-REP remainder would be nonzero below MIN_REP_DEPOSIT; only that dust case sweeps the remainder.

There is no user transfer or approval function for escalation claims. Liquidation is the only ownership-change path, and settlement is the only payout path. This prevents a secondary market from separating likely winners from liabilities while still allowing an unsafe vault to be resolved.

A vault also cannot add another deposit to the same game after liquidation has split or fully moved its claim bundle. Deposits from one vault share one fixed bundle identity, so aggregating fresh principal after a split would give the old liquidator shares in capital it never acquired. Rejecting the deposit preserves the bargain made at liquidation: transferred shares cover only claims that already existed. The vault may participate again in a later game with a fresh bundle, and every existing owner can still claim this game's final payout.

Claim credit follows the registry's exact integer result, bundle by bundle. A sub-share remainder stays with the target and is not credited to the liquidator; this lets a tiny fragmented claim coexist with liquidation of the target's economically meaningful claims without overstating the transferred compensation.

Why Cherry-picking Is Forbidden

If liquidators could take only free REP, rational actors would leave likely losing claims and debt behind. The original vault would become a toxic remainder and liquidation would amplify adverse selection instead of transferring risk. Moving the debt fraction of every escalation claim prevents that cherry-picking, while fee isolation preserves compensation already earned by the target.

Escalation claim movement is path-independent apart from integer floors. On every execution, the moved claim's current REP amount reduces the price-based gross award before free-REP ownership conversion. A liquidator must remain healthy after accepting the debt and collateral.

A partial transfer does not promise to make the target healthy. It reduces the target's absolute exposure and leaves a later liquidator free to take another bounded slice or the full remainder. Requiring instant target health would make many partial liquidations impossible and would force every keeper to finance a full close.

The 105% quote caps the free-REP supplementation target, not the mandatory claim transfer. The complete proportional claim portfolio still moves and can exceed that quote; when it does, the liquidator receives no additional free REP. When claims do not cover the quote, the uncovered 5% margin compensates execution risk, gas, and capital. Accrued ETH fees are always inaccessible; free-REP surplus is inaccessible except when its nonzero remainder would fall below the minimum-deposit floor and must be swept.

Bounded Execution

Liquidation reads one consolidated current-game checkpoint and fixed-size bundle and owner registries. Claim movement rejects while a continuation checkpoint is incomplete, so debt cannot move against only an imported prefix. The canonical 64-bundle global and per-vault checkpoint cap, eight-owner cap, eight-bundle import batch, and internal scan limits avoid unbounded Solidity loops and make worst-case gas knowable. A maximum checkpoint therefore needs exactly eight continuation calls. Reaching a cap rejects another bundle rather than creating unbounded descendant work.

Those limits apply to ownership changes and liquidation. Separate settlement relay calls accept caller-sized deposit or proof arrays and should be chunked to fit available gas; they cannot create more bundles or owners.

Queued Execution

The oracle coordinator snapshots the target's allowance, free-REP ownership, pool REP-per-ownership rate, and numerically attributed current-game escalation REP when liquidation is queued, then validates the settled price at execution. Its minimum-distance guard uses those queue-time values, but execution invalidates only a changed target ownership or allowance and re-evaluates health from the live pool rate, live balances, and live escalation escrow. A target deposit, withdrawal, earlier liquidation, or allowance change therefore cancels the old quote, so collateral deliberately assigned to the target cannot become a queued keeper's windfall. A raw REP transfer to the pool changes no vault ownership and does not cancel execution. That distinction is game-theoretically necessary: otherwise an unsafe target could spend one atomic REP after every queue to buy a deterministic liquidation veto. The live pool rate converts the bonus-priced REP award into ownership units. A donation that does not cross either live health boundary cannot stop execution; even a small donation can stop it when the target is sufficiently close to a boundary. The liquidator must remain healthy; both vaults must obey minimum REP and allowance floors, while the target may remain unsafe after a partial slice.
