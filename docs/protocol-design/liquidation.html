Liquidation Design

Liquidation transfers security-bond allowance from an unsafe vault to a healthy vault. The protocol computes a REP award with a fixed 5% bonus and transfers that award entirely as free REP. Non-tradeable escalation claims and accrued vault fees never transfer. Free-REP surplus remains with the target unless a nonzero remainder would fall below the minimum deposit, in which case that dust remainder is swept.

Position Model and Actors

The target vault is the unsafe position being reduced, and the liquidator vault is the healthy position accepting its liabilities. Each vault can hold an ETH-denominated security-bond allowance, free REP represented by pool ownership, accrued fees, and non-tradeable escalation claims. Locally attributed escrow records REP deposited by that vault in the current game and counts toward current-game health. Inherited continuation backing remains aggregate until a committed deposit proof is settled and does not receive per-vault child health credit.

Execution quotes the debt slice and its fixed 5%-bonus REP award. Only free REP moves with the debt. Accrued fees and escalation claims stay with the target vault, and the liquidator must remain healthy after assuming the debt and receiving the free-REP award.

Two Health Conditions

Ordinary underwriting counts free REP and locally attributed escrow in the current game because both remain pool-associated security capital. Inherited continuation backing stays aggregate and is settled from committed deposit proofs. Liquidation never moves a claim. Migration safety counts only free REP because every locked escalation claim can lose and therefore cannot credibly choose a child universe for parent open interest.

These tests apply to each affected vault. Aggregate pool backing is checked separately, so a well-backed pool cannot conceal an individually unsafe vault and one weak vault does not misstate the pool-wide reserve.

The migration multiplier is derived halfway between one and the configured pool multiplier, so 1 < migrationSecurityMultiplier < poolSecurityMultiplier. Multipliers use basis points: an origin pool therefore requires at least 10,002 BPS (1.0002×), which derives a migration multiplier of 10,001 BPS (1.0001×). The exact minimum preserves both strict inequalities despite integer division. The lower bound makes OI routing costly to corrupt; the upper bound leaves some REP available for escalation without weakening the ordinary underwriting target.

Bonus-Priced Liquidation

For moved allowance D, the liquidator receives the fixed 5%-bonus award entirely as free REP. Escalation claims and earned ETH fees are outside the liquidated position.

bonusRepQuote=⌈debtMoved⋅repEthPrice⋅(BPS_DENOMINATOR+LIQUIDATION_REP_BONUS_BPS)PRICE_PRECISION⋅BPS_DENOMINATOR⌉

Liquidation awardDebt and output use atomic token units; repEthPrice is scaled by PRICE_PRECISION = 10¹⁸, and the 500 BPS bonus is applied over BPS_DENOMINATOR = 10,000.

Integer execution converts the free-REP quote to ownership with a floor and converts it back to the reported REP amount with another floor. The repAmountMoved field in VaultLiquidated reports this final post-conversion amount. Full and dust-promoted closes use the same award, transferring all free REP only when the target remainder would otherwise violate the minimum-deposit floor.

  • Debt is capped by the requested and available allowance; debt-dust or REP-dust rules can promote the slice to a full close.
  • The award is quoted with an atomic-unit ceiling, converted to pool ownership with a floor, and converted back to the reported free-REP amount.
  • The target retains all accrued unpaid fees, including fees checkpointed immediately before liquidation.
  • Every escalation claim remains owned by its committed depositor and pays only that vault if it wins.
  • A full liquidation moves all allowance and the free-REP award, but leaves escalation claims, accrued fees, and free REP above the award with the target unless the free-REP remainder would be nonzero below MIN_REP_DEPOSIT; only that dust case sweeps the remainder.

There is no transfer or approval function for escalation claims. The claim remains bound to the depositor committed in the carry leaf, and settlement is its only payout path.

Because claim ownership cannot change, a vault can continue reporting in the same game without owner-slot or per-vault bundle limits. Each carry leaf independently commits its depositor, outcome, amount, stable index, cumulative amount, and source node.

Liquidation does not inspect or value this claim commitment. Its gas cost is independent of the target's reporter count, portfolio size, and fork depth.

Why Claims Stay Put

A liquidator cannot cherry-pick escalation claims because it cannot acquire any of them. Its compensation is the deterministic 105% free-REP award for assuming debt; the target retains both the upside and downside of every report it originated.

This separation also keeps settlement identity simple: the committed depositor remains the payout address at every fork depth. A liquidator must remain healthy after accepting the debt and free REP.

A partial transfer does not promise to make the target healthy. It reduces the target's absolute exposure and leaves a later liquidator free to take another bounded slice or the full remainder. Requiring instant target health would make many partial liquidations impossible and would force every keeper to finance a full close.

The 105% quote is paid from free REP and compensates execution risk, gas, and capital. Accrued ETH fees and escalation claims are always inaccessible; free-REP surplus is inaccessible except when its nonzero remainder would fall below the minimum-deposit floor and must be swept.

Bounded Execution

Liquidation reads only the target and liquidator vault accounting. It performs no claim scan, proof verification, owner rewrite, or descendant import, so its work is constant regardless of reporter count and fork depth. The carry commitment is used only when a relayer later settles an inherited claim.

Settlement relay calls accept caller-sized deposit or proof arrays and should be chunked to fit available gas. They consume committed leaves with replay protection and do not create owner registries.

Queued Execution

The oracle coordinator snapshots the target's allowance, free-REP ownership, pool REP-per-ownership rate, and numerically attributed current-game escalation REP when liquidation is queued, then validates the settled price at execution. Its minimum-distance guard uses those queue-time values, but execution invalidates only a changed target ownership or allowance and re-evaluates health from the live pool rate, live balances, and live escalation escrow. A target deposit, withdrawal, earlier liquidation, or allowance change therefore cancels the old quote, so collateral deliberately assigned to the target cannot become a queued keeper's windfall. A raw REP transfer to the pool changes no vault ownership and does not cancel execution. That distinction is game-theoretically necessary: otherwise an unsafe target could spend one atomic REP after every queue to buy a deterministic liquidation veto. The live pool rate converts the bonus-priced REP award into ownership units. A donation that does not cross either live health boundary cannot stop execution; even a small donation can stop it when the target is sufficiently close to a boundary. The liquidator must remain healthy; both vaults must obey minimum REP and allowance floors, while the target may remain unsafe after a partial slice.
